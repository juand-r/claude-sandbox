## Chapter plans

### Chapter 1 (plan, revised 2026-10-01)

Time of writing, inside the fiction: January 2020. Movement I (Order), and
only its beginning. Working title: "Concord".

The author's notes on the first draft of this plan (2026-10-01): it moved
the plot too fast, and its tone was McPhee's only. The first plan put four
tells in one chapter (the missing serpette, the pond, a sentence breaking
off at the creek, giving up wine). That is most of movement I spent in its
first pages. Now: almost no plot. The reader should finish chapter 1 having
enjoyed a strange, charming, over-precise book about grapes, with at most a
faint unease they could not justify.

Tone: *The Fall* as much as *Oranges* (`STYLE.md`, 2.6). From the first
page he talks to "you", as Clamence talks to his stranger: easy, courteous,
amused at himself, aware of being good company. Aphorisms that sound like
judgments of people in general ("We are all loyal to the first version of a
thing ..."). Irony about his own precision, which does not stop him being
precise.

Movements of the chapter:

1. Opening address. He introduces the subject, and himself just enough: an
   old man in Napa who has spent his life around wine and has decided to
   write about the grape instead. Why the grape and not the wine: a light,
   witty answer, not the real one. (The real one, that he no longer drinks,
   is held back for later chapters.)
2. The grape, encyclopedically: what it is botanically; the genus; how many
   kinds; how many tonnes. He buys a bunch of grapes in January (imported,
   out of season) and counts its berries. Counting twice gives two numbers;
   he finds this funny, and counts a third time. (Character, not plot.)
3. The first grape he knew was not a grape: Welch's grape jelly, in a small
   Iowa town he does not bother to name ("it does not matter which; they
   were all the same town"). The jar, the glass it became, his father's
   store, the heat. Warm, funny, specific. The taste Americans call "grape"
   is the taste of one variety, the Concord.
4. The Concord and its maker: Ephraim Bull, the seedling, the epitaph.
   Welch, the dentist, the communion juice. (Notes and subnotes; citations.)
   The Clamence turn: a remark about how we honour the one who profits and
   forget the one who planted, said lightly, a judgment he does not apply to
   himself.
5. Close: back to the bunch on his desk in January, the count, and the book
   ahead. No pond, no serpette, no Odile, no Army, no names.

Held back for later chapters (not in chapter 1): Iowa's lost vineyards and
the riverbank grape; the pond; the serpette; that he no longer drinks wine;
Odile in any form, even "O.".

Asides (margin) in this chapter: the method only. Card 1, the date and the
minute; the shoeboxes; the typewriter. Wit, precision, a little vanity. At
most one that is slightly odd (the hour he is writing), with an innocent
reading.

Tells (`STYLE.md`, 4.2): at most one or two, each fully deniable: the count
that changes, and the town he does not name.

Facts: only from `book/research/FACTS.md`, each cited in the source with
`% Fnnn`.
