# The narrator: Hollis Vane

A private file for the authors. The reader never sees it. Everything here is
invented unless it is marked REAL; real-world anchors are listed at the end
and must be verified, or attributed to a source, before they appear in the
book.

Fixed by the author: elderly, male, the only narrator of the book; a wine
connoisseur in Napa who grew up in a small Iowa town like the Endora of
*What's Eating Gilbert Grape* (Endora is also in Iowa); moved to France young
to learn the trade and work at a vineyard; moved to Napa later for a business
opportunity; has lost the love of his life; full of regrets; retreats into
writing; killed her, not on purpose (provisional, see below). The rest I
filled in, and can change.

### Ardath, Iowa

Invented. A town like Endora, in the same flat state: a few hundred people, one main street, a grain
elevator taller than anything else for forty miles, a café, a hardware store
(his father's), a Lutheran church, a water tower with the town's name on it.
Nothing happens there, and everybody knows when it does. Corn, not vines: the
nearest grape is in a jar of jelly.

He left at 17 and went back only for funerals. He speaks of it rarely and
with a mixture of contempt and longing.

He can refer to *What's Eating Gilbert Grape* (Hedges's novel, 1991; the
film, 1993), because of the pun, and because Endora is his town under
another name: the people who stayed, the store, the boredom with weight to
it. He saw the film in 1994, in St. Helena, with Peg, and left before the
end.

### Timeline

- 1943: born in Ardath. (Birth year to be confirmed together with the year
  the book is written; see PLAN.md.)
- 1960 (17): leaves Ardath for France. HOW: TO DECIDE. The author rejected
  the earlier backstory (his father billeted at the Domaine Ferrand in 1944,
  the boy sent to the Ferrands in 1960) on 2026-10-01. The new one: a
  teenager who wants to escape rural America. Options under discussion: runs
  away and works his passage; enlists and is posted to France; an exchange
  year from which he does not come back. Whatever the route, he ends up at
  the Domaine Ferrand in Meursault and never explains why he left.
- 1960–1971: in Burgundy. Works harvests, then the cellar. Gaston, a silent
  man, teaches him to taste and to estimate a harvest by counting clusters,
  vine by vine. Hollis has counted things ever since.
- 1961: the harvest. He meets Gaston's daughter, Odile, then 17. Gaston gives
  him a serpette, a small curved pruning knife.
- 1971: the opportunity. A Napa winery, growing fast, wants a man who knows
  Burgundy and speaks French, to buy and to advise. He takes it and leaves
  within a month. He tells Odile he will send for her. He does not. He and
  Gaston quarrel before he leaves; about what, he never says. (Regret one.)
- 1973: marries Peg.
- 1970s–2000s: a respected palate in Napa. Buyer, consultant, judge at
  competitions, a column in a trade newsletter. Known for exact, joyless
  tasting notes.
- 1979: buys the house and its small vineyard, an old prune orchard
  replanted to vines.
- 1979: a minor scandal, settled privately: a wine sold under a better label
  than it deserved. He says it was the bottler's error. (Shows he bends the
  truth when the truth would cost him.)
- Late 1980s–1990s: his vineyard must be replanted after phylloxera returns
  to Napa. He keeps a ledger of every vine pulled.
- 2006: Peg dies. June and he quarrel at the funeral, in Ardath.
- 2007: Odile, long married to a wine broker in Beaune, is widowed.
- 2008: she writes to him. The first letter in 37 years.
- 2009, at harvest: she comes to Napa "for a month" and stays.
- 2009–2024: fifteen years together. Not married. She keeps a strawberry bed
  by the pond and refuses to learn the names of the clones in his vineyard.
- Night of 18–19 October 2024, the last night of harvest: Odile dies.
- 2025: he stops drinking wine. He cannot say why. He begins the book on
  grapes: the fruit before it becomes wine.

## The death of Odile

What the public record says:

- She was found at about 7 a.m. on 19 October 2024 in the frost-protection
  pond below the house, by a picker arriving for work.
- She had been drinking. There was a bruise at her left temple.
- The coroner recorded drowning; manner of death "undetermined". The sheriff
  closed the case.
- Two glasses and an empty bottle were on the table on the terrace: a 1961
  Meursault from the Domaine Ferrand, the harvest at which they met.

What only Hollis knows, and does not tell:

- They argued that evening.
- He says he went down to the cellar and worked until about 2 a.m., then went
  to bed without looking for her.
- He records everything. His ledgers, cards and diaries account for every
  day of the last sixty years, to the minute. The night of 18 October 2024 is
  the only gap: the page for that date is cut out of the diary, cleanly, at
  the gutter. The book never says who cut it.
- His serpette has been missing since that night.

Other people:

- Thibault Ferrand, Odile's nephew, heir to her share of the Domaine.
  Believes Hollis killed her. Writes him letters, which Hollis files unread
  and numbers.

### What actually happened (decided 2026-10-01, PROVISIONAL)

He killed her, and he did not mean to. During the quarrel on the terrace, or
on the path down to the pond, he struck or pushed her; she fell, hit her
head, and went into the water. Not premeditated: an accident of anger.

The author marked this "I think; revisit later". Open details, to be settled
when we return to it: whether he struck or pushed; whether he saw her go into
the water; whether he left her, or tried and failed to reach her; what
happened to the serpette; who cut the page.

Earlier candidates, set aside for now: a plain accident with no one else
there; someone else there; that she chose it.

However it is settled, the book never confirms it (`STYLE.md`, 2.5). Every
clue must keep an innocent reading.

## Habits, tics, and how he writes

- Counts: berries, steps from the desk to the door (14), cards written per
  night. Rounds nothing.
- Records times to the minute, and corrects them ("at 11:42, or rather
  11:43").
- Checks the cellar lock three times. Says it is only twice.
- Works at night, in the old tank room, at a steel table.
- Notes go on index cards, numbered, in shoeboxes labelled by year. He cites
  them as sources ("Card 1142"). The cards are this book's notes before they
  were notes.
- Types fair copies on a Hermes 3000 typewriter that Odile gave him in 2010.
  He crosses out, never erases.
- Does not drink wine anymore. Tastes grapes instead, and describes them as
  if they were wines.
- Calls her "O." at first. Her full name appears only late in the book.
- Addresses "you" in the asides. It is never clear whether "you" is the
  reader, Odile, or Thibault.

## Motifs (plant early, return often)

- Strawberries: her bed by the pond; grey mould on forgotten fruit. The
  strawberry already "returns throughout this book" in chapter 1.
- Water: the pond, still surfaces, the word "drowned", which he avoids until
  late.
- The 1961 Meursault: the year they met, the bottle on the terrace.
- The serpette.
- The missing page, and gaps in general: missing cards, notes that are
  referred to but not there.
- Counting that does not come out the same twice.
- Gilbert Grape: the pun he cannot resist and then regrets; Endora as Ardath;
  "what's eating" him.
- Doubles: Odile and Peg; Odile and Odette (in *Swan Lake* the two are danced
  by one ballerina); Iowa and Burgundy; France and Napa; the grape and
  the wine.

## Real-world anchors (REAL; verify or attribute before use)

These are real things the biography leans on. None is to be stated in the
book until checked against a source, or attributed to one.

- *What's Eating Gilbert Grape*: novel by Peter Hedges (1991), film (1993),
  set in Endora, a fictional small town in Iowa. Checked 2026-10-01 against
  secondary sources (IMDb FAQ, SuperSummary); check a primary source before
  quoting.
- Napa Valley grew prunes and walnuts widely before vineyards dominated it.
- Napa's wine business grew fast in the late 1960s and 1970s.
- Phylloxera returned to Napa in the late 1980s and 1990s, when the AxR1
  rootstock failed, and many vineyards were replanted.
- Frost-protection ponds are common in Napa vineyards.
- Meursault is a wine village in the Côte de Beaune, Burgundy.
- Vignerons estimate yield by counting clusters on sample vines.
- The Hermes 3000 is a Swiss portable typewriter made from the late 1950s.
- *Swan Lake*: Odette and Odile are traditionally danced by one ballerina.
- In California, a coroner can record the manner of death as "undetermined".

Invented: the town of Ardath, Iowa (no such town found in web searches,
2026-10-01, in Iowa or Nebraska; there are Ardaths in Missouri and
Saskatchewan, and a former townsite called Ardon in Muscatine County, Iowa); the Domaine
Ferrand; the Ferrand and Vane families; Peg, June, Thibault; every event of
the plot.
