# The narrator: Hollis Vane

A private file for the authors. The reader never sees it. Everything here is
invented unless it is marked REAL; real-world anchors are listed at the end
and must be verified before they appear in the book.

The author fixed: elderly, male, a wine connoisseur from Napa, moved to France
young to learn the trade and work at a vineyard, has lost the love of his life,
it is unclear whether there was foul play, full of regrets, retreats into
writing. The rest I filled in. Everything below can be changed.

## Facts of his life

| | |
|---|---|
| Name | Hollis Ambrose Vane |
| Born | 3 March 1943, Napa County, California |
| Age in the book | 83 (the book is written 2025–2026) |
| Lives | The house his father built, on a slope east of the Silverado Trail, above a small vineyard and its frost-protection pond |
| Family | Father Ambrose, a prune and walnut grower. Mother Ruth (née Lindqvist), who kept bees and kept ledgers of everything. One sister, June (b. 1947), in Sacramento; they have not spoken since 2006 |
| Wife | Margaret "Peg" Ahlquist, married 1973, died 2006 of cancer. A dutiful, cold marriage. No children |
| The love of his life | Odile Ferrand (b. 1944), of Meursault |

### Timeline

- 1943: born. Grows up among prune trees, before Napa was wine country in the
  way it later became.
- 1960 (17): sent to France in the autumn, "to learn wine". The family never
  said why he was sent so suddenly. He never says either. (First of the
  book's unexplained departures.)
- 1960–1971: in Burgundy, at the Domaine Ferrand, a small estate in
  Meursault. Works harvests, then the cellar. His teacher is Gaston Ferrand,
  a silent vigneron who taught him to taste and to estimate a harvest by
  counting clusters, vine by vine. Hollis has counted things ever since.
- 1961: the harvest. He meets Gaston's daughter, Odile, then 17.
- 1971: leaves France abruptly, without her. Official reason: his father's
  illness. Real reason: never stated. Gaston and he quarrelled; Odile was
  told he would come back. He did not. (Regret one.)
- 1973: marries Peg.
- 1970s–2000s: a respected palate in Napa. Consultant, buyer, judge at
  competitions, a column in a trade newsletter. Known for exact, joyless
  tasting notes.
- 1979: a minor scandal, settled privately: a wine sold under a better label
  than it deserved. He says it was the bottler's error. (Shows he can bend the
  truth when it costs him something not to.)
- Late 1980s–1990s: his vineyard must be replanted after phylloxera returns
  to Napa. He keeps a ledger of every vine pulled.
- 2006: Peg dies. June and he quarrel at the funeral.
- 2007: Odile, long married to a Beaune wine broker, is widowed.
- 2008: she writes to him. The first letter in 37 years.
- 2009, at harvest: she comes to Napa "for a month" and stays.
- 2009–2024: fifteen years together. Not married. She tends a strawberry bed
  by the pond and refuses to learn the names of the clones in his vineyard.
- Night of 18–19 October 2024, the last night of harvest: Odile dies.
- 2025: he stops drinking wine. He cannot say why. He begins the book on
  grapes: the fruit before it becomes wine.

## The death of Odile

What the public record says:

- She was found at about 7 a.m. on 19 October 2024 in the frost-protection
  pond below the house, by a picker arriving for work.
- She had been drinking. There was a bruise at her left temple.
- The coroner recorded drowning; manner of death "undetermined". The sheriff
  closed the case.
- Two glasses and an empty bottle were on the table on the terrace: a 1961
  Meursault from the Domaine Ferrand, the harvest at which they met.

What only Hollis knows, and does not tell:

- They argued that evening. About what, he does not say.
- He says he went down to the cellar and worked until about 2 a.m. and then
  went to bed without looking for her.
- He records everything. His ledgers, cards and diaries account for every
  day of the last sixty years, to the minute. The night of 18 October 2024 is
  the only gap: the page for that date is missing from the diary, cut out
  cleanly at the gutter. The book never says who cut it.
- His serpette, the small curved pruning knife Gaston gave him in 1961, has
  been missing since that night.

Other people:

- Thibault Ferrand, Odile's nephew, the heir to her share of the Domaine.
  Believes Hollis killed her. Writes him letters, which Hollis files unread
  and numbers.

### What actually happened: UNDECIDED

Decide before writing movement II, so the clues are consistent. Candidates:

- A. Accident. She walked down to the pond drunk, after the quarrel, and fell.
  His guilt is for the quarrel and for not looking for her.
- B. He struck her during the quarrel, and she fell. Whether he then left her
  is the thing he cannot write.
- C. Someone else was there.
- D. She chose it. She was ill and had not told him; the quarrel was about
  that.

Whatever is chosen, the book never confirms it (`STYLE.md`, 2.5).

## Habits, tics, and how he writes

- Counts: berries, steps from the desk to the door (14), cards written per
  night. Rounds nothing.
- Records times to the minute, and corrects them ("at 11:42, or rather
  11:43").
- Checks the cellar lock three times. Says it is only twice.
- Works at night, in the old tank room, at a steel table.
- Notes go on index cards, numbered, in shoeboxes labelled by year. He cites
  them as sources ("Card 1142"). The cards are this book's notes before they
  were notes.
- Types fair copies on a Hermes 3000 typewriter that Odile gave him in 2010.
  He crosses out, never erases.
- Does not drink wine anymore. Tastes grapes instead, and describes them as
  if they were wines.
- Calls her "O." at first. Her full name appears only late in the book.
- Addresses "you" in the asides. It is never clear whether "you" is the
  reader, Odile, or Thibault.

## Motifs (plant early, return often)

- Strawberries: her bed by the pond; grey mould on forgotten fruit. The
  strawberry already "returns throughout this book" in chapter 1.
- Water: the pond, still surfaces, the word "drowned", which he avoids until
  late.
- The 1961 Meursault: the year they met, the bottle on the terrace.
- The serpette.
- The missing page, and gaps in general: missing cards, notes that are
  referred to but not there.
- Counting that does not come out the same twice.
- Doubles: Odile and Peg; Odile and Odette (in *Swan Lake* the two are danced
  by one ballerina); France and Napa; the grape and the wine.

## Real-world anchors (REAL; verify before use)

These are real things the biography leans on. None is to be stated in the
book until checked against a source.

- Napa Valley grew prunes and walnuts widely before vineyards dominated it.
- Phylloxera returned to Napa in the late 1980s and 1990s, when the AxR1
  rootstock failed, and many vineyards were replanted.
- Frost-protection ponds are common in Napa vineyards.
- Meursault is a wine village in the Côte de Beaune, Burgundy.
- Vignerons estimate yield by counting clusters on sample vines.
- The Hermes 3000 is a Swiss portable typewriter made from the late 1950s.
- *Swan Lake*: Odette and Odile are traditionally danced by one ballerina.
- In California, a coroner can record the manner of death as "undetermined".

The Domaine Ferrand, the Ferrand family, the Vane family, Peg, June, Thibault
and every event of the plot are invented.
