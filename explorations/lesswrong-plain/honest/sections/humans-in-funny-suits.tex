\honest{Humans in Funny Suits}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2008}

On screen, the human species travels to the stars and finds aliens who look ``remarkably like humans in funny suits.'' I pretend to explain this by convergent evolution: any intelligence, I say with a straight face, needs two legs, two eyes, a nose, attractive females and English. Then the real explanation: costumed humans are ``the easy way out.''

But the real problem is mind. An alien is a human in a funny suit if it thinks like a human, especially an English speaker of our time. The Americans in \textsc{Psycho} (1960) seemed to me more alien than most screen aliens. Living abroad would help, I suspect; ``this I have never done, being busy.''

In every known culture, I say, humans ``seem to experience'' the same six emotions, and these are shown ``by the same facial expressions.'' \nb{The second claim was disputed well before the post. A 1994 review by James Russell argued that the supporting studies' methods may have shaped their results, and a 2019 review by Barrett and colleagues found that expressions vary substantially across cultures.} Humans are alike because, in a sexual species, complex adaptations must be shared or they would not assemble.

Our ancestors had to model only minds like their own, so ``we evolved to predict Other Minds by putting ourselves in their shoes.'' A brain is too complex to model ``neuron-by-neuron,'' so the only way to grasp another is to run a shadow of its anger and beliefs on your own. I call this ``empathic inference.'' \nb{This is simulation theory, one side of a debate. Its rival holds that people use a tacit theory of mind, an abstract model of goals and beliefs, which the post's argument does not consider. ``Belief in Intelligence,'' the previous post in the book, uses such a model.}

Empathic inference fails for minds unlike ours. An alien with no sense of humor would not see the point of the Marx Brothers; ``you've never antled.'' Readers will say evolution must give every intelligence a sense of humor, or that my jokes are not funny enough; I compare the second to shouting English abroad. \nb{The first objection gets no answer.} Laughter may be valuable even if not all possible minds share it.

The artists who drew aliens carrying off girls in torn dresses ``did not make that error by reasoning''; it ``just seemed to them'' that the girl was sexy. \nb{In ``Mind Projection Fallacy,'' Yudkowsky had offered this account of the artists with ``Probably.''} Do not explain why any intelligent mind must have emotions, I say: natural selection builds complex machines without them. \nb{Yudkowsky calls evolution ``stupid in an absolute sense'' in ``Thou Art Godshatter,'' and does not count it as an intelligence. It shows that making complex machines does not need emotions, not that intelligence does not.}

Good writers of real aliens include Jack Vance. The low point, which I think Orson Scott Card named, was the Star Trek episode whose aliens had rewritten the preamble to the U.S. Constitution. ``The inability to imagine the alien is the inability to see yourself.''
