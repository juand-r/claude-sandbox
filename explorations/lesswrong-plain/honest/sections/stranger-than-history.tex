\honest{Stranger Than History}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

Suppose I told you, as fact, that a certain color of paint will make you fall upward; that the sky will fill with giant black spheres that lower male prostitutes on bungee cords for money; and that your grandchildren will think it evil to jail thieves instead of spanking them. You would think I was crazy.

Now suppose it is 1901, and you must choose between those claims and these: there is an absolute speed limit, at which lengths and time ``change around''; a global network of adding machines will carry moving pictures of lesbian sex ``by pretending they are made out of numbers''; and your grandchildren will think it evil to bar someone from the presidency ``because she is black.'' \nb{In 1901 part of the first claim was already in physics: FitzGerald and Lorentz had proposed that moving bodies contract. And in December 1900 John Elfreth Watkins forecast ``cameras connected electrically with screens at opposite ends of circuits, thousands of miles at a span.''}

The second list is true. I chose its items for their strangeness, and I worded them oddly. I made the first list up to be absurd. So the comparison shows that the truth can sound crazy, which is my point.

The idea is Robin Hanson's, from a comment on my previous post: a fiction our ancestors could not tell from the truth, ``to make it very clear how surprising the truth turned out to be.''
