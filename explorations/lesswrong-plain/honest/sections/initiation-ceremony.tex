\honest{Initiation Ceremony}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2008}

This is a story. Brennan climbs a stair lit by torches of the wrong color, counting the steps: sixteen times sixteen. A robed guide leads him to a gate of warped glass. The guide's gloves hide the color of the skin, and the whisper hides the guide's sex. \nb{This is Brennan's first appearance in the book's order; he returns as a student of Jeffreyssai in ``The Failures of Eld Science.''}

``Do you want to know?'' Brennan suspects a trap in so obvious a question, but says yes. ``Know what, exactly?'' He falls back on the truth: ``It doesn't matter,'' he says, ``the answer is still yes.'' The gate opens.

Inside, hooded figures line a room whose mirrored walls repeat them to infinity. The guide names the absent: Jakob Bernoulli, Abraham de Moivre, Pierre-Simon Laplace, Edwin Thompson Jaynes. Each, the room answers, ``Is dead but not forgotten.'' \nb{Four mathematicians of probability, who died between 1705 and 1998. The post Yudkowsky published the same day, ``To Spread Science, Keep It Secret,'' proposed initiation rites of just this kind for science.}

The guide offers a ring, closes the hand, and asks: if three-fourths of the room are women, and three-fourths of the women and half of the men belong to the Heresy of Virtue, and the guide is a member, what is the probability that the guide is a man? ``Two-elevenths,'' says Brennan. ``It's one-sixth, actually,'' says the guide, and the room laughs. \nb{Brennan is right: the odds are 2 to 9.}

Brennan wants to flee the city and change his name. The guide says that we may know an honest mistake ``by its relinquishment,'' and asks again. Brennan starts to say ``One'' and stops. ``Just say `one-sixth' already,'' the guide stage-whispers, to more laughter. \nb{The scene recalls Asch's experiments, in which a unanimous group gave a wrong answer before the subject spoke.}

Brennan works it through aloud and keeps two-elevenths, on the information given. He adds that he does not believe the information: it seems too neat, and the room holds an odd number of people. \nb{He is right again: the fractions require a room of some multiple of sixteen.}

He gets the ring and is told: ``You are now a novice of the Bayesian Conspiracy.'' \nb{Yudkowsky's ``An Intuitive Explanation of Bayes's Theorem,'' placed earlier in the book, ends: ``You are now an initiate of the Bayesian Conspiracy.'' The story tests an answer that can be checked in one's head. It does not show the harder case, when to defer to someone who may know more.}
