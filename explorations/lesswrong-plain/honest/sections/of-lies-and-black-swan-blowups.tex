\honest{Of Lies and Black Swan Blowups}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2009}

Judge Marcus Einfeld went to jail for two years. He was 70 years old, a Queen's Counsel since 1977, an Australian Living Treasure, winner of a United Nations Peace Award and founding president of Australia's human rights commission. Though retired, he was ``routinely brought back to judge important cases.'' His perjuries and lies started with a \$77 speeding ticket for 6 mph over the limit. \nb{The honours and the ticket match the Wikipedia article the post links. To contest the ticket he named as the driver a woman who had died years before, then invented a second woman of the same name. The claim that he was routinely brought back to judge could not be confirmed.}

So the theory that honest people are bad at lying, that lies leave traces, and that the whole thing can blow up in a rare catastrophe, ``actually does have a certain number of exemplars in real life.'' The theory does sound ``suspiciously virtuous,'' and ``obvious selective reporting is at work in our hearing about this one.'' \nb{Those caveats mean the case can show only that lies sometimes blow up, which ``Entangled Truths, Contagious Lies'' had already granted. Evidence put to a court in July 2009, after the post, was that Einfeld had named absent people as the drivers of his car on earlier occasions going back to 1999. Those lies seem to have come to light only after the 2006 case, so he was not a newcomer to this kind of lie.}
