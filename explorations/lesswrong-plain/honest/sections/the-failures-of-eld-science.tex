\honest{The Failures of Eld Science}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2008}

This is a story. No robes this time: Jeffreyssai teaches a class of five in the Conspiracy, Brennan among them. \nb{The book has already shown Brennan's initiation, in ``Initiation Ceremony,'' and Jeffreyssai, in ``The Ritual.''}

Jeffreyssai asks how the scientists of the Eld era, ours, failed ``for over thirty years'' to see something about quantum physics: ``Not one of them saw it; not Einstein, not Schrödinger, not even von Neumann.'' I do not name what they missed. \nb{The students' talk of a ``universal law'' points to many-worlds, which Yudkowsky had defended the day before in ``Many Worlds, One Best Guess''; the notes on that post assess the case. Schrödinger came close: in a Dublin lecture in 1952 he said that the alternatives his equation describes are ``not alternatives but all really happen simultaneously.''}

The students try answers. Hiriwa suspects religion. Styrlyn says too few people could work on the problem, and is told off for propaganda. Taji lists grant proposals and tenure. Yin replies that Schrödinger ``retired to a villa for a month, with his mistress to provide inspiration, and emerged with his eponymous equation.'' \nb{The usual account agrees: a mountain cabin at Arosa, December 1925.} Brennan says the Manhattan Project had a weapon to build and quantum theory had no such use.

The teacher's answer is mine: Eld science thought it ``acceptable'' to take thirty years. Then a drill. How long to dodge a knife? ``Less than a second.'' How long to change your mind? ``Less than a second.'' ``WRONG!'' Brennan must stop answering by pattern and think. In the Eld days, the teacher adds, a grant agency might spend six months on a proposal. \nb{True: that was the National Science Foundation's target at the time.} A month holds 28,800 waking minutes, so at five minutes an insight a major problem could be solved in a month. The teacher admits 5,760 insights is not realistic, but says a problem needs fewer, and ``yes, it has been done.'' I give no example.

Given five minutes before the class, Brennan says Eld scientists were trained to use known science, not to resolve a major confusion, which was ``something you did once per lifetime if you were lucky.'' So the founders were ``amateurs.'' Hiriwa adds: ``And no probability theory.'' \nb{Einstein had resolved major confusions twice, with special and general relativity, and quantum mechanics grew out of twenty-five years of the confused ``old quantum theory.'' Jeffreys's Bayesian \textsc{Theory of Probability} appeared in 1939.}

The teacher accepts this and leaves the class 480 minutes to find one more flaw. \nb{The numbers fit: five students, five minutes an insight, 480 insights in all, 96 in series.} I do not say what the flaw is.
