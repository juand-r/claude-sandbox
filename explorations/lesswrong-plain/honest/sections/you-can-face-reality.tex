\honest{You Can Face Reality}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

This post is a paragraph by Eugene Gendlin, set out in short lines like a poem. I add the title and his name, and nothing else.

The paragraph says that what is true is already so; owning up to it does not make it worse, and not being open about it does not make it go away; because it is true, it is what there is to deal with, and anything untrue is not there to be lived; and ``People can stand what is true, for they are already enduring it.''

\nb{That last line holds for facts whose effects you already live with. It does not hold for news you have not yet heard. A parent whose child has died is not enduring the grief until someone tells them. In the hard cases, learning the truth is itself the new cost.}

I name the author but not the book. \nb{It is from Gendlin's book \textsc{Focusing}, a self-help book about attending to your own feelings.}

\nb{In another post, ``Avoiding Your Belief's Real Weak Points,'' Yudkowsky says what to do with it. When you doubt a belief you cherish: ``Punch yourself in the solar plexus. Stick a knife in your heart, and wiggle to widen the hole.'' Then recite Gendlin's paragraph.}
