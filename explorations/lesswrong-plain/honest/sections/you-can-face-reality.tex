\honest{You Can Face Reality}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

This post is a paragraph by Eugene Gendlin, set out in short lines like a poem. I add the title. I add nothing else.

The paragraph says that what is true is already so, that owning up to it does not make it worse, and that ``People can stand what is true, for they are already enduring it.''

That last line holds for facts whose effects you already live with. It does not hold for news you have not yet heard. A parent whose child has died is not enduring the grief until someone tells them. In the hard cases, learning the truth is itself the new cost.

I do not say where the paragraph comes from. It is from Gendlin's book \textsc{Focusing}, a self-help book about attending to your own feelings. There it is printed as ordinary prose, in a part of the book about people helping each other, and it seems to be advice about showing a listener your own feelings. The line breaks are mine. So is the step from your own feelings, shown to a friend, to any truth whatever.

In another post I tell you what to do with it. When you doubt a belief you cherish, I say, ``Punch yourself in the solar plexus. Stick a knife in your heart, and wiggle to widen the hole.'' Then recite Gendlin's paragraph.
