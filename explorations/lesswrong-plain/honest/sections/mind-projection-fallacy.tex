\honest{Mind Projection Fallacy}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2008}

On old science fiction magazine covers, alien monsters carry off women in torn dresses. ``Oddly enough, the aliens never go after men in torn shirts.'' Would an alien with a different evolutionary history desire a human woman? ``It seems rather unlikely. To put it mildly.''

The artists, I say, did not reason that all minds are alike. ``Probably the artist did not even think to ask.'' They saw sexiness as a property of the woman herself, like her height. I do not consider that the woman was on the cover to sell the magazine to human buyers, in which case the artist believed nothing about aliens at all.

``Apparently we instinctively represent Sexiness as a direct attribute of the Woman object.'' If your brain uses ``that data structure, or something metaphorically similar to it,'' then sexiness feels like part of the woman.

E. T. Jaynes named this error the Mind Projection Fallacy: taking a property of your own mind for a property of the world. Jaynes applied it mainly to probability, which he saw as a state of knowledge and not a feature of objects. ``More about this shortly,'' I say; the argument is in another post.

The error, I say, is everywhere: in the dispute over the falling tree, in the magazine cover, in ``Kant's declaration that space by its very nature is flat,'' and in Hume's definition of a priori ideas. Kant held that space is a form of our intuition, not a property of things in themselves. He put space in the mind, and I list him for projecting the mind onto the world. The Hume passage I quote is not a definition of a priori ideas. And Hume wrote that ``the mind has a great propensity to spread itself on external objects'': he described this very error. I credit the name to a twentieth-century physicist, and list the eighteenth-century philosopher who described the error as a case of it.

I end with two stories about romance between humans and aliens, one involving a plant and one an insect in a chiffon dress. ``Just thought I'd mention that.''
