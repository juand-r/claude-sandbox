\honest{The Design Space of Minds-In-General}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2008}

People ask me what AIs will be like. I tell them they have asked a trick question.

Animals, plants and bacteria, you and a petunia included, have essentially the same molecular machine, ATP synthase, because genes that many others depend on are hard to change. ``Any two AI designs might be less similar to each other than you are to a petunia.''

Humans form a natural class because we share one brain architecture. But ``AI'' names a far larger space of possibilities, so when people talk about AIs, I say, ``we are really talking about minds-in-general.'' \nb{The question was what AIs will be like, and so about the ones that will be built. That the possible designs are many does not show that the built ones will differ much. The post's own reason why humans form a class is a shared architecture, and AIs made by shared methods might share one too. The post does not consider this. Quintin Pope made this objection in 2023.}

Then I draw my standard diagram. Humans are a tiny dot, since a sexually reproducing species cannot vary much. The dot sits in a transhuman region, inside a posthuman region, inside a sphere of minds-in-general, and the sphere floats in a larger space of optimization processes, with natural selection at the bottom. Hutter's AIXI may be a powerful optimizer that we would not call a mind, but that is for another day.

My moral is to resist generalizing over all of mind design space. \nb{Aaron Sloman proposed mapping the space of possible minds in 1984, in ``The structure of the space of possible minds''.} There are about two to the trillionth power minds that can be specified in a trillion bits, so a claim that all minds do something has that many chances to be false. \nb{The count is right, and it counts against claims about behavior that varies with design. It does not count against claims that follow from what all such minds share.} People make such claims by imagining themselves in the mind's place.

``Somewhere in mind design space is at least one mind with almost any kind of logically consistent property you care to imagine.'' So explain what a mind does from its particular makeup. And do not say that a mind which does things differently is ``by definition'' not a mind.
