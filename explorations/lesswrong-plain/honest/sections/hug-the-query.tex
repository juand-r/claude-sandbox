\honest{Hug the Query}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

Evidence close to a question, I say, beats evidence further away. The Wright brothers say their plane will fly. Compare their credentials with Lord Kelvin's, and Kelvin wins. Check both sides' calculations, and credentials matter less; Kelvin, I guess, ``probably doesn't have any apart from his own incredulity.'' Watch the plane fly, and the calculations become moot ``for many purposes.''

\nb{It was the Wrights' calculations that failed. Their 1901 glider gave about a third of the lift they had calculated, because they used a coefficient that had been accepted for more than a century and was wrong. They found the error by building a wind tunnel, not by checking their arithmetic.}

Then I invoke a theorem: ``It's a theorem of these causal graphs that you can never get more information from distant nodes, than from strictly closer nodes that screen off the distant ones.'' \nb{That is true. But a reader who checked the Wrights' calculations in 1901 would have found nothing wrong: the error was in an accepted constant, and only experiment showed it.}

I quote a writing teacher, Jerry Cleaver: what does you in is ``overlooking the basics.''

It is better to argue physics than credentials, I say, and better to argue physics than rationality. Accusing someone of ``Bias \#182'' cannot settle a question of fact. If you forget this, I say, ``learning about more biases will hurt you, because it will distract you from more direct arguments.''

Then the concession. You cannot always check the calculations: you may lack the background, the information may be private, or there may be no time. I admit: ``There's a sadly large number of times when it's worthwhile to judge the speaker's rationality.'' I say to do it ``with a hollow feeling in your heart,'' and I give no method for doing it. \nb{That is the ordinary situation of anyone judging a question in medicine or climate science.}

In a footnote to that concession, I point to my own post defending molecular nanotechnology. \nb{Molecular nanotechnology was a position Yudkowsky held, and no experiment had yet tested it.}

I end: ``Whenever you can, dance as near to the original question as possible---press yourself up against it---get close enough to hug the query!''
