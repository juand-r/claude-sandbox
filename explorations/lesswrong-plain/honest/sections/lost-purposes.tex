\honest{Lost Purposes}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

In kindergarten or first grade I was told that a Hebrew prayer works even if you do not know what the words mean. That was the beginning of my break with Judaism.

Students study ``12th-century knitting patterns'' because the degree requires them. In the Bay Area, 80\% of elementary teachers spend less than an hour a week on science, and 16\% none; I understand the cause to be the No Child Left Behind Act. ``Virtually all classroom time is now spent on preparing for tests.'' \nb{The survey's figures are as the post reports them. The ``virtually all'' has no source. A national survey the same year found elementary schools still spending about three hours a week on science, and 44\% of districts cutting other subjects to add reading and mathematics.} Soviet factories met their quotas with tiny shoes. It is ``now being suggested'' that most significant findings in medicine are untrue, and physics journals, I say, ``require a threshold of p<0.0001.'' \nb{The medical claim follows its source, a 2005 paper by Ioannidis. No such rule for physics journals was found. Particle physics requires a far smaller p-value, but only to claim a discovery.}

Nobody keeps opening the car door after learning the supermarket has no chocolate. Organizations, though, reward only what they can measure today. I trace No Child Left Behind from politicians to bureaucrats, textbook committees and teachers; teachers ``won't get through a fourth of the textbook.'' I give no sources for any link. Schools ``seem'' to be ``collapsing into a state not much better than nothing.'' \nb{The US national assessment reported in 2007 that fourth-graders scored higher in reading and mathematics than in any earlier year.} Bureaucrats, I conclude, are untrustworthy genies. \nb{The point that measured targets corrupt the process they measure, above all test scores in schools, is Donald Campbell's, from 1976. The post does not mention him.}

Musashi says every movement of the sword must cut the enemy. Every rule must cut through to the truth, and every expected utility to utility. People who draft wishes for an imagined AI do not ask, as I ``reflexively'' ask, why they think a wish is good. This bothers most people less than it bothers me, ``or why were all my classmates willing to say prayers without knowing what they meant?'' \nb{The classmates were small children.}

The worst threat to a complex civilization is its own complexity. My life, I close, ``has been driven by an exceptionally strong abhorrence to lost purposes,'' and I hope that can be taught.
