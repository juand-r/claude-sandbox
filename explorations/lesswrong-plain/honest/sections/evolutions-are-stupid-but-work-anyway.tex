\honest{Evolutions Are Stupid (But Work Anyway)}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

Yesterday I wrote that ``Science has a very exact idea of the capabilities of evolution.'' Today I show what I meant. I say ``evolutions,'' plural, because each population evolves on its own. None of this, I add, is creationism; it is ``standard Evolutionary Biology 201.''

Evolutions are simple enough, I say, that ``we can calculate exactly how stupid they are.'' A gene with a 3 per cent advantage takes about 768 generations to spread through a population of 100,000; the formula is $2\ln(N)/s$. The chance that it spreads at all is about twice its advantage, 6 per cent. \nb{The arithmetic is right, and the figures agree to within about 5 per cent with the standard results of Kimura and Ohta and of Haldane. Both are approximations for an idealized population of constant size, good for orders of magnitude rather than exact.} Genes have no investors or imitators; they spread only by their bearers having more children.

Complex adaptations take far longer. A gene B that helps only in the presence of gene A gains little while A is rare, so ``Only then,'' once A has spread, can B begin to rise. \nb{This assumes B appears as a new mutation after A has spread. A gene already present at low frequency starts rising as soon as A is common, so the steps can overlap and adaptation is faster.} Then ``other evolutions don't imitate it'': a snake's new venom does not help the fox. \nb{True of snakes and foxes. Bacteria pass genes between species, and antibiotic resistance spreads this way.}

A human programmer can design a hundred interdependent parts in an afternoon. Humans plan ahead, change several parts together, learn from single cases and choose which changes to try. I quote the biologist Cynthia Kenyon, at a dinner, saying that a graduate student can do in an hour what evolution could not do in a billion years. Evolutions, I add, have invented the rotating wheel three times. I give no source for the count. Then the programmer posts the code to the Internet.

Technology already beats biology in many ways. So, once again, for a human to learn design from natural selection would be like a modern bacterium imitating the first replicator. Don't praise evolutions ``one millimeter more than they deserve.''

I promise ``More exciting mathematical bounds on evolution'' for tomorrow. \nb{That post, ``Natural Selection's Speed Limit and Complexity Bound,'' is not in the book. It now carries Yudkowsky's warning that a simulation written to check it ``did not return the expected results.''}
