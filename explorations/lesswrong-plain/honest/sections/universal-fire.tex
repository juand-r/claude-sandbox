\honest{Universal Fire}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

In a fantasy story by L. Sprague de Camp, the hero is carried into the world of Norse myth, and a match he brought from Earth will not strike. I realize it was only a story, but: ``No.'' \nb{The story, ``The Roaring Trumpet'' (1940), was written with Fletcher Pratt.}

In the late eighteenth century Lavoisier discovered fire. People had used fire for ages, but nobody knew how it worked; Greek and medieval thinkers called it one of the four elements. In Lavoisier's time it was explained by ``phlogiston,'' which I describe as a substance ``said to explain fire, and also every other phenomenon in alchemy.'' \nb{By then phlogiston was a chemists' theory, held by Stahl and later Priestley, not alchemy.}

Lavoisier weighed everything, air included, and ``discovered that matter was neither created nor destroyed.'' Before him, people thought some reactions changed the total weight: antimony burned under a burning glass for an hour became ashes a tenth heavier, even as it lost a thick white smoke. Lavoisier found that the air lost what the ashes gained. \nb{In 1674 John Mayow had already attributed the gain in antimony's weight to particles from the air. And conservation of mass was widely assumed in eighteenth-century chemistry; Lomonosov stated it in 1756. Lavoisier proved it by weighing gases too.} He found that a candle uses up ``vital air'' and makes ``fixed air,'' oxygen and carbon dioxide, and goes out when the vital air is gone: burning turns vital air into fixed air and fuel into ash.

Forget what you know of biology, including ``the discovery of DNA, which occurred only in 1953.'' \nb{DNA was isolated in 1869; 1953 is the year of its structure.} Forget the cell theory of 1839 too; imagine looking at your hand with no idea why muscle moves while clay in the same shape sits still. Then imagine discovering that people breathe in vital air and breathe out fixed air: people run on combustion. Lavoisier measured the heat that animals, and his assistant Seguin, produced when exercising: more heat, more vital air consumed, more fixed air breathed out. Deprive people of oxygen or fuel, and the light goes out. \nb{The link between burning and breathing was old. Mayow had concluded that a candle and a small animal use up the same part of the air, and Joseph Black that animals breathe out fixed air. Lavoisier supplied the chemistry and the measurements.}

Matches light because of phosphorus, on the strip of safety matches or in the heads of strike-anywhere ones; pure phosphorus glows in the dark and may catch fire by itself, and Henning Brand, who purified it in 1669, announced that he had found Elemental Fire. Phosphorus is highly reactive, and ``thus also well-suited to its role'' in ATP, the body's store of chemical energy, which powers muscles and neurons and on which almost every metabolic reaction depends. \nb{ATP is built from phosphate, the fully oxidized form of phosphorus, and chemists explain life's use of phosphate by its stability in water. The link runs through the element, not through its reactivity.} ``If a match stops working, so do you. You can't change just one thing.''

We store ``Matches catch fire when struck'' and ``Humans need air to breathe'' as separate rules, and can imagine one without the other; even once connected, the link seems a distant school fact. But if your map is folded into four pieces for storage, the territory is not. But the territory is not divided the way our map is. Phosphorus is only our word for electrons and quarks arranged a certain way, and its chemistry cannot change unless their laws do. In a world where matches failed to strike, ``you would cease to exist as organized matter.'' Reality is laced together far more tightly than we like to believe.
