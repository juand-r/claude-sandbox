\honest{Universal Fire}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

In a fantasy story by L. Sprague de Camp, the hero is carried into the world of Norse myth, and a match he brought from Earth will not strike. I realize it was only a story, but: ``No.'' \nb{The story, ``The Roaring Trumpet'' (1940), was written with Fletcher Pratt.}

In the late eighteenth century Lavoisier discovered fire. People had used fire for ages, but nobody knew how it worked. In Lavoisier's time it was explained by ``phlogiston,'' which I describe as a substance ``said to explain fire, and also every other phenomenon in alchemy.'' \nb{By then phlogiston was a chemists' theory, held by Stahl and later Priestley, not alchemy.}

Lavoisier weighed everything, air included, and ``discovered that matter was neither created nor destroyed.'' Before him, people thought some reactions changed the total weight: burnt antimony got heavier even as it lost a white smoke. \nb{In 1674 John Mayow had already attributed the gain in antimony's weight to particles from the air. And conservation of mass was widely assumed in eighteenth-century chemistry; Lomonosov stated it in 1756. Lavoisier proved it by weighing gases too.} He found that a candle uses up ``vital air'' and makes ``fixed air,'' oxygen and carbon dioxide.

Forget what you know of biology, including ``the discovery of DNA, which occurred only in 1953.'' \nb{DNA was isolated in 1869; 1953 is the year of its structure.} Then imagine discovering that people breathe in vital air and breathe out fixed air: people run on combustion. \nb{The link between burning and breathing was old. Mayow had concluded that a candle and a small animal use up the same part of the air, and Joseph Black that animals breathe out fixed air. Lavoisier supplied the chemistry and the measurements.}

Matches light because of phosphorus. Phosphorus is highly reactive, and ``thus also well-suited to its role'' in ATP, the body's store of chemical energy. \nb{ATP is built from phosphate, the fully oxidized form of phosphorus, and chemists explain life's use of phosphate by its stability in water. The link runs through the element, not through its reactivity.} ``If a match stops working, so do you. You can't change just one thing.''

We store ``Matches catch fire when struck'' and ``Humans need air to breathe'' as separate rules, and can imagine one without the other. But the territory is not divided the way our map is. Phosphorus is only our word for electrons and quarks arranged a certain way, and its chemistry cannot change unless their laws do. In a world where matches failed to strike, ``you would cease to exist as organized matter.''
