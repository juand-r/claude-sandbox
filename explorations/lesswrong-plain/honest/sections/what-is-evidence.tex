\honest{What Is Evidence?}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

I open with two quotations about truth, from Tarski and from Aristotle. This post is about evidence.

Your shoelaces come untied. Light bounces off them into your eye, your brain processes it, and you come to believe that they are untied. Your belief now mirrors your shoelaces.

Evidence, I say, is ``an event entangled, by links of cause and effect, with whatever you want to know about.'' A machine that beeps for every lottery number, winning or losing, tells you nothing: evidence must come out differently depending on the facts. Then, in a parenthesis, comes the technical version: mutual information, relative to your current uncertainty. \nb{The first definition is about the world; the second depends on what you already believe.} I treat them as one.

Light from your shoelaces that hits a rock leaves no trace in the rock; a photograph or a working eye keeps one.

Then my real subject. Rationalists, I say, value the ``paradoxical-seeming'' claim that a belief is worth holding only if something could change it. \nb{It is an ordinary idea with a long history.} Some belief systems, ``in a rather obvious trick,'' say that certain beliefs must be held no matter what. I do not name the belief systems or state their position.

I say: ``Rational thought produces beliefs which are themselves evidence.'' \nb{Since the only account the post gives of rational thought is that it produces beliefs tied to the facts, this is true by definition.}

Your beliefs, once spoken, can be evidence for someone else. So rational beliefs are ``contagious, among honest folk who believe each other to be honest.'' Therefore, I say, a belief held for private reasons that cannot be passed on is ``suspicious.'' But I have just said that passing a belief on depends on the listener's trust. \nb{A belief that does not spread may be missing a trusting listener, not truth. A defendant who remembers not committing the crime holds a belief tied to the facts by memory, and the jury need not share it.}

If your beliefs would not persuade others, I conclude, ``stop believing.'' Indeed, if you understand this ``on a gut level,'' you will stop ``automatically,'' because a belief that is not tied to reality is not accurate. \nb{A stopped clock is not tied to the time, and it is right twice a day. Unreliable is not the same as false.}

I end by asking you to explain why your way of thinking produces true beliefs. I do not explain why mine does.
