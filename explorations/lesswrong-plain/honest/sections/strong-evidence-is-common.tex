\honest{Strong Evidence Is Common}{Mark Xu}{2021}

Someone asked my name. I told them, and they believed me. I guess they would have happily bet at 20 to 1 on it. Before I spoke, the odds that a given person is called Mark Xu were about a million to one against. So my saying it multiplied the odds by some twenty million, about 24 bits of evidence.

An ordinary sentence, I say, can carry a huge amount of evidence. \nb{This is true, and worth knowing.} I do not say why. \nb{The reason is that the claim is very specific, and people rarely lie about their names.} Later I apply the idea to claims where that reason does not hold, and I do not notice the difference.

I add three more cases of the same kind: a Wikipedia page, someone who says they can juggle, a result from Mathematica. \nb{All are reliable testimony or reliable tools.} I do not point that out either.

Now the part I actually care about: you. Perhaps you are in the top 1\% of traders. To be 50\% sure of that, I say, you need only 200 to 1 evidence, and that ``might be easy to get.'' I do not say what the evidence would be. \nb{A good year is weak evidence, since returns are noisy and the traders who failed have mostly stopped trading.} On the way I also say that the efficient market hypothesis makes it hard to make money. \nb{It says it is hard to beat the market.}

Perhaps you are also much less overconfident than most people. That takes only 50 to 1 evidence, and an hour of calibration practice might supply it.

So I have moved from my own name, where testimony is strongest, to your own excellence, where evidence is weakest and wishful thinking is strongest. I made the move without comment.

I close by saying that working through Bayes's rule by hand may push people toward timid estimates. I give no argument for this. Then I accept Carl Sagan's saying that extraordinary claims need extraordinary evidence, and add that such evidence ``might be more common than you think.'' \nb{Sagan meant claims that contradict what we know, such as alien visits.} My name is not one of those.
