\honest{Politics Is the Mind-Killer}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

People think badly about politics. The reason, I say, is ``so obvious as to be worth belaboring'': in the ancestral environment, being on the wrong side of an argument could get you killed. Having said it is worth belaboring, I do not belabor it. No evidence follows, then or later.

My advice is this. When you want to teach a point about rationality, do not take your example from current politics; use Louis XVI. Politics is a terrible place to learn rationality, ``unless all the discussants are already rational.'' Any group that wants to argue about politics can decide it qualifies, so anyone can exempt themselves from the rule.

Next I explain that arguments are soldiers, and that sensible scientists turn into slogan-chanting zombies when a political issue comes up. I describe this as something other people do.

Then comes my example, a standard problem from artificial intelligence: all Quakers are pacifists, all Republicans are not, and Nixon is both. I state it with ``all'', which turns it into a plain contradiction. The problem was invented to show how to reason with rules that have exceptions, and the real version says ``usually''.

I spend two of my eight paragraphs asking why the problem's author, whom I do not name, chose Nixon. Was it to make Republicans feel unwelcome in courses on artificial intelligence? I decide it was to get in a dig at the other side. That is a dig at the author's motives, in an essay whose advice is not to do that. A footnote assures you that I am neither a Republican nor a Democrat.

Finally, the rule. I am not asking you to be apolitical, only to skip gratuitous jabs at a party, because some readers may belong to it. Whether the party really is at fault does not matter. What matters is ``the spiritual growth of the community.'' So the rule exists to keep the peace, and I say so. Keeping the peace and finding out what is true are different aims, and I put keeping the peace first.

My title says that politics kills minds. My essay says that politics is a poor place to practice. Readers will remember the title, and use it to end conversations. Seven years later I will say that I meant ``national politics as seen on TV.''
