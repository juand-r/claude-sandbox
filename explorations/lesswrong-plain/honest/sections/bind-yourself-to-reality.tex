\honest{Bind Yourself to Reality}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2008}

You may ask what my posts about joy in the merely real have to do with reductionism. I give three answers.

First, I plan to take important things apart on this blog, and I want to leave you a line of retreat, so that taking things apart does not feel as if it removes magic from the world and ``unweaves the rainbow.'' Second, I am on a crusade against ``Hollywood Rationality,'' the idea that understanding the rainbow subtracts its beauty. ``The rainbow is still beautiful plus you get the beauty of physics.'' \nb{That is the thesis of Richard Dawkins's \textsc{Unweaving the Rainbow} (1998), named after the same lines of Keats. The post does not mention the book.}

Third, and most deeply, the posts are about ``binding yourself to reality.'' In one of the \textsc{Dune} books, if I remember correctly, a Truthsayer learns to detect lies by never lying. It would not work, but I find it beautiful.

My first example is the lottery. Once you see that tickets are a bad bet, you do not give up hope of being rich; you put the hope into your job, your school, ``your startup, your eBay sideline.'' The lottery is not hard, only ``un-actionable.''

I say all this to show what I mean by taking ``emotional energy that is flowing off to nowhere'' and binding it to reality. I do not mean safe, ``realistic'' goals.

The Hollywood stereotype says that reason destroys emotion. In ``Feeling Rational'' I set against it two lines, ``That which can be destroyed by the truth should be'' and ``That which the truth nourishes should thrive.'' Emotions that follow from your best picture of the truth cannot be destroyed by truth, so they are not irrational. \nb{The first line is P. C. Hodgell's.} So let us redirect those energies, not destroy them.

``Want to fly? Don't give up on flight. Give up on flying potions and build yourself an airplane.'' \nb{The airplane gives what the potion promised, flight. In the rainbow case, the physics of light is a different pleasure from the magic it replaces, and the post does not show that the feeling will follow.}

This is ``Think Like Reality'' applied to feelings: bind them to the lawful universe, with its Moon landings and its evolution of intelligence, ``Just not any magic, anywhere, ever.''

People say that science takes the fun out of life. I say: ``Science puts the fun back into life.''
