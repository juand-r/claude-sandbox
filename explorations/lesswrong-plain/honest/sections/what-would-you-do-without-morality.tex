\honest{What Would You Do Without Morality?}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2008}

To people who say nothing is real, I once asked how the nothing works.

Now suppose you learned that nothing is moral and nothing is right, ``that everything is permissible and nothing is forbidden.'' \nb{Philosophers who hold that nothing is morally right, the moral error theorists, do not add that everything is permissible, since permissibility is itself a moral status.} Suppose I tore apart whatever your moral philosophy rests on, showed that nothing could replace it, and ``proved that all utilities equaled zero.'' I am not telling you this in real life. I know your moral philosophy is as undisprovable as 2 + 2 = 4. \nb{In ``How to Convince Me That 2 + 2 = 3,'' Yudkowsky had written: ``I find it quite easy to imagine a situation which would convince me that 2 + 2 = 3.'' The joke is at the reader's certainty.}

Would you still tip cab drivers, stay faithful, pull a fainted child off the train tracks? Would you eat the cheapest food, since you have no reason to enjoy yourself, or the most expensive, since you have no reason to save? Would you wear black and write gloomy poetry? That would only be a cached thought. Would you get out of bed? Would you keep reading this blog, and keep trying to be rational?

Close your eyes and take your time: what would you do, if nothing were right? I do not answer. \nb{Yudkowsky answered the next day, in ``The Moral Void,'' a post not in this book. The next post in the book, ``Changing Your Metaethics,'' sums it up: ``you can just drag the kid off the train tracks anyway.''}
