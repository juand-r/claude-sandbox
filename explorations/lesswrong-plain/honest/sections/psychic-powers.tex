\honest{Psychic Powers}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2008}

I open by quoting my previous post: ``You can never get Bayesian confirmation for a hypothesis of irreducibility,'' because any prediction you can make could also be made by a reducible thing, your brain. Benja Fallenstein objected. No test could logically prove irreducibility, but an outcome could make it ``subjectively much more probable,'' as a working hypercomputer might.

I promise a crushing reply, and it is: ``You're right.'' After publishing ``yesterday's post'' I found a similar flaw myself. \nb{LessWrong dates both posts 12 September 2008; in United States time the first went up the evening before.}

If beliefs were ontologically basic, a law linking minds directly would be much simpler than it is if beliefs are complex patterns of neurons. So information passing between brains with no material connection is ``probabilistically a privileged prediction of supernatural models.'' A reductionist model can match any concrete prediction a parapsychologist makes, but less simply. A naive Occam rule that counts ``belief'' as one basic entity favours psychic powers; the naturalistic rule does not.

So telepathy, precognition and influencing events by wishing would be ``strong Bayesian evidence'' that beliefs are fundamental: ``Not logical proof.'' Non-reductionists ``own'' this prediction, since they put it forward. \nb{The history supports this. F.~W.~H. Myers, a founder of the Society for Psychical Research, hoped telepathy would show mind communicating with mind apart from recognised channels, and so support survival after death.}

``I am therefore confident in dismissing psychic powers as a priori implausible, despite all the claimed experimental evidence in favor of them.'' I do not describe that evidence. \nb{It existed and was contested. Meta-analyses of ganzfeld experiments reported an effect in 1994, found none in 30 further studies in 1999, and reported one again, pooling 79 studies, in 2001.} The word ``confident'' links to my post ``I Defy the Data!'' \nb{That post's addendum allows defying the data of one experiment, not of many. Beyond that, it says, one must give up the theory or dismiss the data with reasons, such as a design flaw or a larger body of failed replications. The post gives no such reason.}
