\honest{The Gift We Give To Tomorrow}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2008}

This post is a dialogue. ``I'' ask how natural selection, a cruel and mindless process, produced beings capable of love. ``You'' answer. I will call you the answerer. The answerer knows the science and gets the last word, and the reader is addressed as the answerer. So the reader is on the winning side before any argument starts.

My questions get harder: mothers love adopted children, people make friends who are not relatives, and some die for their friends. Then I ask about Gandhi. The answerer says Gandhi ``Believed certain complicated propositions about `What should be done?' and did them.'' I reply: ``That sounds like it could explain any possible human behavior.'' The answerer lists possible causes, hedged with ``something like,'' ``probably'' and ``plus perhaps,'' and then says: ``Unless you think it was magic, it has to fit into the lawful causal development of the universe somehow.'' I had not suggested magic. I promise not to, and the question about Gandhi is dropped.

Then I say it is amazing that evolution produced beings so much nicer than itself. The answerer says I value beauty and altruism because I evolved to, so of course I find it marvelous that evolution produced them, and no coincidence needs explaining. A standard of value outside human minds that happened to agree with ours would be ``far too much coincidence.'' \nb{Moral philosophers call this an evolutionary debunking argument. Sharon Street published a version in 2006, and philosophers have been replying to it since.} In my dialogue it wins by rhetorical question. \nb{It also assumes that there is no standard of value outside human minds, which is what it set out to show.}

Next the answerer explains why flowers are beautiful. They ``evolved to attract bees---by imitating certain mating signs of bees.'' \nb{Flowers that attract insects by imitating their mating signals are known almost only among orchids; the known cases outside them are a South African daisy and an Asian iris. And many flowers are pollinated by other animals, or by wind.} Humans, the answerer goes on, like flowers because healthy flowers were a sign of fertile land. Before this story, the answerer asks whether I think ``science does not know the story.'' \nb{The part about humans is a guess.}

The answerer also states a law, with no argument: ``A complex pattern must be explained by a cause which is not already that complex pattern.'' \nb{Anyone who thinks love comes from a source that already loves is ruled out by the law, not by evidence.}

I end with a fairy tale. Our descendants will ask where love began, and will be told that once ``there were intelligent beings who were not themselves intelligently designed,'' back ``when all of civilization was a single galaxy.'' So the future contains people designed by other people, and a civilization bigger than a galaxy. I do not argue for either. They appear as background in a bedtime story.
