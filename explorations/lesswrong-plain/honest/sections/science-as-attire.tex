\honest{Science as Attire}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

An \textsc{X-Men} trailer tells us that every human carries the genetic code for mutation, and the mutant Storm throws lightning. Consider what that would take: organs to make electricity, protection against it, and fine control of it. If an animal acquired all this in one generation by mutation, it would ``outright falsify'' evolutionary theory, worse than rabbit fossils in the Precambrian. A theory that could cover Storm could explain anything.

The comics use words like ``evolution'' and ``mutation'' ``purely'' to place themselves in the literary genre of science. What scares me is wondering how many people understand science only that way.

Some people who firmly believe in evolution have no idea what it permits and forbids. They talk of ``the next step in the evolution of humanity,'' as if selection had a plan, or call computer chips, corporations splitting and uploading ``evolution.'' \nb{In its ordinary sense, ``evolution'' is any process of change toward a better or more complex state; one of Merriam-Webster's example sentences is ``an important step in the evolution of computers.'' Someone who calls a better chip design ``evolution'' is not claiming that biology covers it.}

``Probably an actual majority'' of believers in evolution use ``because of evolution'' to be part of the scientific in-crowd, and would say ``because of intelligent design'' as cheerfully if the in-crowd did. \nb{The post gives no evidence for this. Its own test, asking what a belief forbids, can show that a belief forbids nothing, but not why someone holds it.}

I turn to the critics of AI. People who accept mildly smarter-than-human AI call strongly superhuman AI ``pseudoscience.'' Their ``devastating critiques consist purely of comparisons to apocalyptic literature,'' not of known laws that forbid the outcome. They have no theory of intelligence that sets an upper bound on it. Superhuman AI simply calls to mind apocalyptic fiction, while an AI running a small company calls to mind \textsc{Wired} magazine. They do not see that science is about models. I quote none of them. \nb{The post asks the critics for a model and gives none for the outcome they doubt. The post it links to, on nanotechnology, grounds that case in Drexler's reasoning ``from known science,'' and still calls the conclusion ``merely a best guess.''}

Last, a test for you. Take a scientific idea you are proud of believing but do not use in your work, and ask which future experiences it forbids. That is all of it you have really made your own. ``Anything else is probably passwords or attire.''
