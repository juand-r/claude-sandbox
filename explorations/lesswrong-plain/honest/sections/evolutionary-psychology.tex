\honest{Evolutionary Psychology}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

``Reproductive organ'' is redundant: all organs are reproductive organs. A bird's wings are there because they helped its ancestors reproduce. The brain is no different. Lust is directly reproductive, anger indirectly. Anger exists ``because angry ancestors had more kids,'' and ``There's no other way it could have gotten there.'' \nb{A trait can also be a by-product of selection for something else, what Gould and Lewontin called a spandrel. In ``An Especially Elegant Evpsych Experiment'' (2009) Yudkowsky allows that the pain of sunk costs may be ``an unfortunate spandrel.'' Later research supports the view that anger is an adaptation; this post gives no evidence for it.}

This historical fact confuses ``all too many people.'' They ask whether I mean that they are trying to have children when someone punches them. \nb{No one who makes this mistake is named.} ``No. No. No. NO!''

As I wrote earlier the same day, the cause, shape and consequence of an adaptation are separate things. A toaster does not know it was meant to make toast. But a toaster is not a mind, so no one is tempted to credit it with goals. A human is a mind, and a human's evolutionary ``purpose'' gets mixed up with its real purposes, the way the meaning of the word ``blue'' gets in the way of naming the color it is printed in.

So: ``Cognitive causes are made of neurons. Evolutionary causes are made of ancestors.'' \nb{Biologists call this the distinction between proximate and ultimate causes.} Emotions and even unconscious motives exist in the brain; something in Stalin's brain, it ``seems likely,'' lit up for power. Evolution, by contrast, is a statistical fact about ancestors. Wings do not understand flight, and until the twentieth century no brain represented inclusive genetic fitness.

Thinking that anger contains a wish to reproduce is like thinking your hand contains a wish to pick things up. Keep apart the episode of anger, the circuitry, the genes that build it, and the ancestral history that explains the genes. Evolutionary psychology demands ``X-Treme Nitpicking.''

Then a story. A man and a woman meet in a bar. He likes her clear skin, she likes his confident smile: cues to fertility and to status. They use a condom. Their detectors do not know what they were for.

You must hold all of this in mind ``without a single mixup or conflation.''
