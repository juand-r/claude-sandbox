\honest{Evolutionary Psychology}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

``Reproductive organ'' is redundant: all organs are reproductive organs. A bird's wings are there because they helped its ancestors reproduce. The brain is no different. Lust is directly reproductive, anger indirectly. Anger exists ``because angry ancestors had more kids,'' and ``There's no other way it could have gotten there.'' \nb{A trait can also be a by-product of selection for something else, what Gould and Lewontin called a spandrel. In ``An Especially Elegant Evpsych Experiment'' (2009) Yudkowsky allows that the pain of sunk costs may be ``an unfortunate spandrel.'' Later research supports the view that anger is an adaptation; this post gives no evidence for it.}

This historical fact confuses ``all too many people.'' They ask whether I mean that they are trying to have children when someone punches them. \nb{No one who makes this mistake is named.} ``No. No. No. NO!''

As I wrote earlier the same day, the cause, shape and consequence of an adaptation are separate things. A toaster does not know it was meant to make toast. But a toaster is not a mind, so no one is tempted to credit it with goals. A human is a mind, and a human's evolutionary ``purpose'' gets mixed up with its real purposes, the way the meaning of the word ``blue'' gets in the way of naming the color it is printed in, while ``wind'' causes no trouble. A bee and a human are both designs, but only the human is a designer: the bee is ``wind,'' the human ``blue.''

So: ``Cognitive causes are made of neurons. Evolutionary causes are made of ancestors.'' \nb{Biologists call this the distinction between proximate and ultimate causes.} An intention to go to the supermarket, an emotion, an instinct, a suppressed thought all exist physically in the brain, and with a good enough scanner and the code you could see them. Even unconscious motives do. Lord Acton said that power tends to corrupt. Whether or not Stalin thought himself an altruist, something in his brain, it ``seems likely,'' lit up for power, or how could power have corrupted it? Evolution, by contrast, is a statistical fact about ancestors, not ``a little furry thing lurking in an undiscovered forest.'' Wings do not understand flight, bones have no concept of strength, and brains that evolution made able to design had no more concept of evolution than a bird has of aerodynamics. Until the twentieth century no brain represented inclusive genetic fitness. So ``the evolutionary purpose of anger'' must not slide into ``the purpose of anger,'' and then into its cognitive purpose.

Thinking that anger contains a wish to reproduce is like thinking your hand contains a wish to pick things up. You can will your hand to pick up a penny, but no act of will made the hand grow. Likewise, a particular fit of anger can have mental causes, but you did not will the circuitry of anger into your brain. Keep apart the episode of anger, the circuitry, the genes that build it, and the ancestral history that explains the genes. Evolutionary psychology demands ``X-Treme Nitpicking.''

Then a story. A man and a woman meet in a bar. He likes her clear skin and firm breasts, ancestral cues to fertility, here produced by makeup and a bra. She likes his confident smile and firm manner, ancestral cues to status and resources. She plans to use birth control, and her brain, so to speak, is a creationist. He just wants sex. They use a condom. Their detectors do not know what they were for. The main result is to keep the bar, the hotel and the condom maker in business, which was nobody's purpose and has nothing to do with the ancestral regularities that built their brains.

You must hold all of this in mind ``without a single mixup or conflation.''
