\honest{The Hidden Complexity of Wishes}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

A preface, added in 2024: this post is about how complex human values are, and so about what a superintelligence would need to want. It is not about what one could understand. \nb{The body agrees with the preface. Its conclusion is that a safe genie ``must share the same values that led you to make the wish.''}

There are three kinds of genies, I say: those to whom any wish is safe, those to whom no wish is safe, and those too weak to matter. I do not argue that there are only three.

Your aged mother is trapped in a burning building. You have an Outcome Pump, which resets time until a chosen outcome happens, and pushes a chosen quantity as high as it can. You tell it to increase your mother's distance from the building's center. The gas main explodes, and her body flies away from the building. Patch the wish to forbid explosions, and she falls from a window and breaks her neck.

A human helper would not even think of blowing up the building, so ``Get my mother out of the building'' sounds safer than it is. The group selectionists, I add, expected group selection to make animals restrain their breeding, and it produced cannibalism, ``especially of immature females.'' Eating little girls was so ``un-aesthetic'' that Wynne-Edwards, Allee and Brereton ``simply didn't think of it.'' \nb{Cannibalism was one of four traits that changed, and the post quotes none of the three biologists; see ``The Tragedy of Group Selectionism.''}

You wanted your mother alive; you wished for distance. \nb{Norbert Wiener made this warning in 1960: the purpose put into a machine we cannot effectively interfere with had better be ``the purpose which we really desire.'' The post does not mention him.} You also want her healthy, untraumatized and in touch with her family. What of the dog, a murderer, a piece by Bach, a frozen head? The judgments you would make are finite in complexity, but not small.

So: ``There is no safe wish smaller than an entire human morality.'' \nb{The only genie the post examines is built to push its score as high as possible. A genie that optimizes less hard is not considered. Such ``limited optimization'' was later proposed at the author's own institute (Jessica Taylor, 2015), with the warning that it is ``not a silver bullet.'' The argument shows the danger of maximizing; the thesis is about every genie.} The only safe genie shares all your judgment criteria. ``With a safe genie, wishing is superfluous. Just run the genie.'' I do not say how to build one.
