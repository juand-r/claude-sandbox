\honest{Fake Selfishness}{Eliezer Yudkowsky}{2007}

I once met a man who called himself purely selfish and told me that I should be purely selfish too. Feeling mischievous, I told him that religious people, ``at least the ones I meet,'' find a religious reason for whatever they want to do. ``It looks to me like'' a philosophy of selfishness works the same way: it ``has no effect on their behavior,'' because whenever its holders want to be nice to people, they can find a selfish reason. \nb{The post's only case is the conversation that follows: one man's reasons for urging selfishness on the author.}

He disagreed. So I asked why, if he was genuinely selfish, he wanted me to be selfish. \nb{Yudkowsky had made a related point in ``Your Rationality Is My Business'': a truly selfish person would be making money, not arguing. The \textsc{Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy} treats an egoist who recommends egoism as unusual, not inconsistent.}

He answered that if I became selfish, I would see that my rational self-interest lay in playing ``a productive role in the economy,'' not in passing laws against his property. \nb{That is a self-interested reason, and a familiar one. Ayn Rand held that the rational interests of different people do not conflict, so an egoist of her kind can want others to be selfish too.}

I replied that I am already a libertarian, and have taken a job that I expect to benefit many people instead of one that pays more. I asked whether persuading me was ``the most selfish thing'' he could be doing. Then I asked what I really wanted to know: did he want to be selfish first, and decide that converting others served that, or did he want to convert others first, and then look for a selfish reason?

He said, ``You may be right about that last part,'' so I marked him down as intelligent. \nb{The concession shows that the wish to convert may have come first.}

In a footnote I offer more questions for professed egoists. Would you die now to save the Earth, rather than live one more year and die with it? Would you rather stub your toe, or have a stranger tortured for fifty years, if you would never know about the torture? \nb{The last clause meets the egoist's usual reply, that helping others only relieves one's own distress. Daniel Batson's experiments tested such replies and did not support them.} Would you steal a thousand dollars from Bill Gates if no one could ever find out? \nb{This is the test of Gyges' ring, from Plato's \textsc{Republic}.}
