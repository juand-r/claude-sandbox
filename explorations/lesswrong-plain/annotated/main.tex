% ---- Commentary notes -------------------------------------------------------
\newcounter{cnote}
\newcommand{\pendingnotes}{}
% \cnote{kind}{text}: mark the text with a number and queue the note.
\makeatletter
\newcommand{\cnote}[2]{%
  \refstepcounter{cnote}\textsuperscript{\footnotesize\thecnote}%
  \edef\cnote@head{\noexpand\printnote{\thecnote}}%
  \expandafter\g@addto@macro\expandafter\pendingnotes\expandafter{\cnote@head{#1}{#2}}}
\makeatother
\newcommand{\printnote}[3]{%
  {\footnotesize\RaggedRight\color{notecolor}\textsuperscript{#1}\textsc{#2}.\ #3\par}}
% Print queued notes beside the paragraph just finished, then realign the columns.
\newcommand{\flushnotes}{%
  \switchcolumn\pendingnotes\gdef\pendingnotes{}\switchcolumn*}

\newcommand{\cstyle}[1]{\cnote{style}{#1}}
\newcommand{\clogic}[1]{\cnote{logic}{#1}}
\newcommand{\cfact}[1]{\cnote{fact}{#1}}
\newcommand{\ccut}[1]{\cnote{cut}{#1}}
\newcommand{\cauthor}[1]{\cnote{author's footnote}{#1}}

% ---- Post header ------------------------------------------------------------
% \post{title}{author}{year}{url} starts a post; \closepost closes it.
\newcommand{\post}[4]{%
  \clearpage
  {\noindent\Large #1\par}\vspace{0.3ex}
  {\noindent\small #2, #3.\quad \href{#4}{Original on LessWrong}\par}\vspace{1ex}
  \begin{paracol}{2}}
\newcommand{\closepost}{\flushnotes\end{paracol}}

The left column reproduces each post as published on LessWrong. I have changed nothing except to convert web formatting to print: links become plain text, and the authors' italics and bold stay as they were. The numbered marks are mine.

The right column holds my comments. Each starts with a tag:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textsc{style}: the writing could be clearer or shorter. Many of these cite principles from Joseph Williams, \textsc{Style: Lessons in Clarity and Grace}: put the characters in the subjects and the actions in the verbs; put old information before new; save the end of a sentence for what matters most; cut hedges, intensifiers and redundant words.
\item \textsc{logic}: the argument has a gap, an unstated assumption, or a claim that goes beyond its support.
\item \textsc{fact}: a factual or arithmetic claim that is wrong, or that needs a source.
\item \textsc{cut}: the passage does no work and could go.
\end{itemize}

This is a personal study copy. The posts belong to their authors.
