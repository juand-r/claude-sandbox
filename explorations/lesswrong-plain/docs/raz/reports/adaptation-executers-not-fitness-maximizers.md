# Report: adaptation-executers-not-fitness-maximizers ("Adaptation-Executers, not Fitness-Maximizers", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-11-11)

Book order 140 (The Machine in the Ghost, The Simple Math of Evolution). Batch 3a.

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Following Tooby and Cosmides, organisms do not pursue genetic fitness; they execute
adaptations that were shaped by ancestral conditions, which is why taste buds tuned to scarce
sugar and fat now lead people to harmful ice cream. The distinction is explained with a
screwdriver: its cause (the designer's mind), its shape (the only thing in the tool), its
consequence (what it actually does) and its meaning (what users take it for) are four different
things, and the same four headings applied to the taste bud separate evolutionary history,
mechanism, modern effect and value. Even "people do what would have helped their genes among
hunter-gatherers" is not quite right, since no one asks that question and many modern things have
no ancestral counterpart.

## 3. Arithmetic

- 2763 generations: the linked post, `data/originals/evolutions-are-stupid-but-work-anyway.md`
  line 25: "if the population size were 1,000,000 ... it would require 2763 generations for a
  gene conveying a 1% advantage". Formula (line 21): 2 ln(N)/s. 2 x ln(1,000,000) / 0.01 =
  2 x 13.8155 / 0.01 = 2763.1. Matches.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. Reserved words: none in the notes, afterword or honest section.
2. The Response's main point is credit-type information (Mayr's terms, "evolutionary
   mismatch") presented as "the reader is not told". The post does not claim novelty, so the
   notes do not say "presented as new". If the editor thinks naming the standard terms is not a
   fault at all, the second paragraph of the Response could be shortened to one sentence.
3. The Mayr point is made in full here (first in book order) and only as a pointer in
   "Evolutionary Psychology" (order 141), per STANDARDS 2.5.
4. The last note and the Response's third paragraph place the slogan in a debate
   (Smith et al. 2001; Wikipedia). This is information, not a charge that the thesis is wrong. It
   could be read as importing a general dispute about the field; I kept it because the post
   states the slogan as settled ("Therefore it is said"). Cut if the editor judges it outside the
   brief's instruction on general criticisms.
5. The Response is mild (mostly credit). I found no fault in the post beyond the missing
   names and the settled tone, and did not invent one.
