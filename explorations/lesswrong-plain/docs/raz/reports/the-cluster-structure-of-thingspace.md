# Report: the-cluster-structure-of-thingspace

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Any object can be pictured as a point in a "thingspace" with a dimension for every property,
and uncertainty about the object as a cloud of probability in that space. Categories such as
"mother" or "bird" are radial: the intension is a glow brightest at the typical centre, and the
extension is a cluster of points with dense central sub-clusters and dimmer outliers; the
author holds that the glow was learned from the clusters. Because real clusters need have no
set of necessary and sufficient properties, a definition only has to lead the listener to the
right cluster, and objecting to its exceptions is nitpicking.

## 5. Items not verified

- Lakoff (1987) itself: not read; the attribution rests on Wikipedia ("Cognitive semantics") and
  on the book's title in the previous post's references.
- DNA: "millions of quaternary coordinates". A bird genome is about a billion base pairs (chicken:
  "approximately one billion base pairs", PubMed 15592404, `pubmed_15592404_chicken_genome.txt`),
  so "billions" would be closer. Left out of the notes: "millions" is loose rather than false (a
  billion is a thousand millions) and nothing depends on it.
- Quantum configuration space ("infinite-dimensional configuration space, and a single point in
  that space describes the location of every particle"). For n particles the configuration
  space is R^3n (`wiki_config_space.txt`); the infinite-dimensional space is the space of wave
  functions (`wiki_wave_function.txt`: "an in general infinite-dimensional Hilbert space"). I
  dropped the note because a fair defender can answer that the universe may hold infinitely many
  particles, or that field theory has an infinite-dimensional configuration space.
