# Report: original-seeing

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The post is a passage from Robert Pirsig's Zen and the Art of Motorcycle Maintenance,
introduced by two sentences saying that the story is true whether or not it happened. In
the passage a student who cannot write about Bozeman, or about its main street, writes five
thousand words once told to start from one brick of one building. The teacher concludes she
was blocked because she was trying to repeat what she had heard, and narrowing the subject
forced her to see for herself.

## 6. Judgment calls

- The \clogic on "either way, it's true". A fair defender could say "it's true" is an idiom
  meaning "the lesson rings true", offered for illustration. I kept the note because the post
  asserts truth in its own voice and offers nothing else; the note does not say the lesson is
  false. The word "invented" appears only as "If the story may be invented", a conditional,
  not an accusation.
- Quoting the fictional-evidence post: its topic is using fiction in forecasting. I quoted
  only its general question ("argued in its own right"), which fits this case.
- The note that the post "leaves the connection to the reader" (to "Cached Thoughts") is
  descriptive, not a fault.
- The post is a quotation, so there is one counted paragraph; notes on the quotation are
  placed inside the quote environment and print beside it.
