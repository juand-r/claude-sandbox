# Report: my-best-and-worst-mistake

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Having seen definitions of intelligence abused in AI, and simple elegant math confine AI to
toy systems, the young Yudkowsky refused to define intelligence and disbelieved in any
mathematics of it, overreacting to others' mistakes. This was a best mistake because it led
to broad study of the cognitive sciences and a long period of study before proposing
anything, and the author draws the moral that what you end up doing matters more than the
clever reason you had for doing it. It was a worst mistake because informality meant low
standards: reasoning at the level of "that orangey-bright thing" is needed on the way to
knowledge, but must not be trusted, and the young author put weight on it and "stepped onto
a loaded trap".

## 6. Judgment calls

- The main critical note: "loath to put any definition on 'intelligence' at all" and "I
  refused to define it at all" are stronger than the archive, which has informal
  definitions (Staring 1996/1999; Plan 1999-2000). I used "stronger than the record" and
  "overstates", not "false": the post's first claim (no mathematical definition) stands,
  and its later "not to precisely define" fits. The fair-defender reply "loath means
  reluctant" is real; the third paragraph's flat "I refused to define it at all" is the
  firmer target. This point is in the In short line, since it is the one place where the
  memoir's account of its own earlier writing does not match them.
- The Legg and Hutter note is information (credit to the post's later view), not a charge;
  it sits in the Response as a short paragraph.
- No note charges the "screens off" moral or the "anthropic principle" wording; I judged
  both a fair use.
- Reserved words: "wrong" only in paraphrase of the post ("right actions for wrong
  reasons", "would also have been wrong").
