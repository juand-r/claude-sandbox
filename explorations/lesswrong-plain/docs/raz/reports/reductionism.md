# Report: reductionism ("Reductionism", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-03-16)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Reductionism, in the author's sense, is obviously correct: physics describes a 747 and a
collision of gold nuclei with the same fundamental laws, and we use different, simpler
models for the airplane only because a fundamental model would be impossibly expensive to
compute, so the use of different levels of description is a fact about our maps, not about
the territory. A fundamental model would contain all the facts about wings and lift
implicitly, and would predict better; the feeling that the airplane is made of many
interacting levels is how an efficient multi-level model in the mind feels from inside.
Reductionism is therefore not a positive belief but a disbelief that the higher levels of
our models exist in the territory, which dissolves the question of how the airplane can
lack "real" wings when one can see them.

## 6. Judgment calls

1. The Matthew C note (quote block) and Response paragraph: "reads as sarcasm" is my
   reading of the full comment ("rocks thrown at anyone who would challenge ... fools or
   liars", link to a book on extrasensory perception). I do not say the post misquotes: its
   cuts are marked. The point is that the excerpt hides the tone. A defender may say the
   author saw the sarcasm and "deemed it legitimate" anyway; the note does not claim
   otherwise.
2. P7 note: the "when ... falsified" conditional. I do not say the conditional is refuted,
   only that the post does not show its condition holds for views about reduction.
3. P8 note: "one claim, stated two ways" (absence of belief vs "only the most basic level").
   I dropped a sentence about burden of proof as my own argument.
4. The antireductionist note (P20) and Response paragraph: a scope point. The post takes a
   side ("obviously correct"), so naming the multiple-realizability tradition as unaddressed
   seems within STANDARDS; but Fodor-style token physicalism agrees with much of the post,
   and the editor may prefer to cut the scope sentence or move it out of the In short line.
5. The chromodynamics cfact: a slip whose point survives; kept out of the In short line.
6. Credit: Anderson (1972) as prior statement of the post's key distinction and of the
   consensus; SEP's representational/ontological distinction. The In short line says "close
   to what Anderson set out in 1972"; Anderson also says "at each level of complexity
   entirely new properties appear", which some read as more than a claim about maps, so
   "close to", not "the same as".
7. Pronouns: Anderson (historical figure), the gunner ("he" in the post).
