# Report: leaky-generalizations

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Almost every generalization above the level of fundamental physics has exceptions, and
generalizations about what to do inherit the exceptions of the world: instrumental values
often have no description that is both short and stated only in terms of the actions, as
a box opened by a machine with many keys shows. But the complexity of actions says nothing
about the complexity of goals: killing anyone, even Hitler, is a large negative terminal
utility, and shooting Hitler is still right because of the lives it saves. To infer from a
positive expected utility that the terminal utility of the death must be positive is the
type error of "Terminal Values and Instrumental Values", a kind of double counting; whether
utilities themselves are leaky is a separate question.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. Need-for-closure clogic: the post's "can" is respected ("fits stakes of the first kind").
   A fair defender may say "stakes" meant urgency; the note allows that.
2. The cpara "No description is both. That is true of the example because it is built so":
   an invented example is not a fault (STANDARDS 2.2.4). The note says what the example
   shows and where the "often" rests; it does not call the example a fault. Cut if it reads
   as one.
3. Circumcision clogic: two readings given; only the "different terminal values" reading
   is said to go unanswered, using the author's own hedged words from the previous post.
4. Particularism clogic and Ross cpara: prior work given as information. The Ross note
   supports the author's view (credit); the particularism note says who actually holds the
   rejected view, and that the post's closing concession leaves room for it. "argue from a
   general claim about reasons, not from the consequences of particular cases" is my
   summary of SEP's "true for reasons in general".
5. "The speaker is a composite and unnamed": the recurring unnamed-opponents point, made
   briefly (full versions in Say Not Complexity and elsewhere earlier in the book).
6. Considered and dropped: "trigger the button" (no button was introduced; a slip);
   the joke name for Thanksgiving (described neutrally, no judgment); whether "double
   counting" is the exact analogue of Pearl's problem (the post's "resonance" matches Fake
