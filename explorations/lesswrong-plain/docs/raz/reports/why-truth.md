# Report: why-truth ("Why Truth?", Yudkowsky, posted 2006-11-27)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

People seek truth for three reasons, morality, practical use, and curiosity, and each
reason sets different priorities among questions. Morality as a motive is risky because
it turns rationality into a custom people defend instead of learning from mistakes;
practical use gives feedback on which ways of thinking work; curiosity is pure but may not
check its answers, and history shows science began with truth as an instrument. All three
matter, and some rule-like norms are needed, but the author names curiosity as the most
foundational.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- Xenophanes cfact. "No one realized that anything was wrong with that" has no time frame. A fair defender could say it meant prehistory, before the Greeks. The note therefore says "Loosely worded, and overstated if it covers the Greeks the paragraph has just named." B11 is a moral objection, and B15 and B16 are closer to an epistemic one; the note cites both. The Response repeats this in one sentence. If you think the conditional reading is too generous or too harsh, this is the note to look at.
- Spock cpara: "It does not show what the previous paragraph claimed, that holding rationality as a duty produces such mistakes." The post's own sentence ties Spock to "how many people conceive of the duty to be 'rational'", which is about people being put off, not about duty producing errors. I think the note is fair, but the post's Spock paragraph does two things and a defender could say I picked the one it does less well.
- Propriety cpara (paragraph 15). I say the post "does not say what keeps the new norms from the trouble it described there". The post hedges ("things that look like norms", "quasi-moral injunctions"), and a defender could say the hedge is the answer. I kept "does not say", not "contradicts".
- Final cpara (curiosity "most foundational"). I first wrote that the choice "goes against" the post's reasons, and softened it to "point elsewhere" and "not supported by the post's own reasons". "Foundational" is undefined, and the author may mean foundational for them personally. The note says the answer is offered as the author's own ("if you were to ask me").
