# Report: the-failures-of-eld-science

## 1. The argument in three sentences

A story, not an argument: Jeffreyssai asks his five students how the scientists of the
"Eld" era (ours) failed to see, for over thirty years, what quantum physics implied, and
rejects the easy answers (religion, too few physicists, administrative burdens) while
drilling Brennan to answer by thinking rather than by fitting a pattern. His own answer is
that Eld science thought it acceptable to take thirty years, since its process aimed at the
truth "eventually"; with arithmetic on minutes and five-minute insights he argues that a
major problem might be solved in a month. Put on the spot, Brennan adds that Eld scientists
were trained to use known science, not to resolve a major confusion, which they met once a
lifetime and faced as amateurs without probability theory; the teacher leaves the class a
one-day assignment to find a further flaw he has in mind.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. Fiction (STANDARDS 2.2 test 3). Most notes describe. Three notes check the history the
   story alludes to: Schrödinger 1952, "once per lifetime" against Einstein's record and the
   old quantum theory, and "no probability theory" against Jeffreys 1939. These are
   characters' claims; the teacher accepts them ("Good", "Acceptable"), so I treated them
   as the story's diagnosis. A fair defender could say the characters are guessing about a
   distant era. The Response says "sits uneasily", not "wrong".
2. The premise (the founders failed to see many-worlds) is inferred from context, not
   stated. The note says the talk of a "universal law" "points to" many-worlds. The
   Response's first paragraph relies on this inference.
3. Reserved words: "never names it" (checked by grep); "wrong task" and "never have faced"
   are paraphrases of the story's own words.
4. Planck note: points forward to Faster Than Science (order 255), which carries the full
   point. In book order this story is the first allusion; for the whole-book pass the full
   point could move here, but Faster Than Science quotes Planck by name and this story does
   not, so I left it there.
5. Density: 49 \cpara on 79 paragraphs; the one-line dialogue lines have none.
6. The honest section is 455 words, near the top of the range, because the story has many
   beats.
