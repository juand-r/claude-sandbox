# Report: not-for-the-sake-of-happiness-alone

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Against ethicists who argue as if all desires reduce to the desire for happiness, the post
asks whether we should care about the things that make us happy apart from the happiness
they bring, and replies that doing what makes us happy does not make happiness the only
reason (it would make caring for others hard to explain, and an act can have several valued
consequences). Using a pill that simulates the joy of discovery, an unseen screensaver and
a holodeck with a forgetting pill, the author reports an intuition that gives full
value only when both an objective component (a real discovery, a real person) and a
subjective one (someone's joy) are present, and a value on freedom from external
manipulation even when no one would know. So the author's values are not reducible to
happiness or to subjective states: they are many terminal values (art, science, love,
freedom...), and the author prefers a really complicated life to a pleasure center in a vat.

## 6. Judgment calls

- The main criticism (notes on paragraphs 9 and 11, the clogic after "only consequent", the
  Response's second paragraph, the "In short" line): the middle arguments address motives
  (psychological hedonism, egoism), while the question in paragraph 7 is evaluative
  ("whether we *should* care"). A fair defender might say that for the author what we should
  value is fixed by what we terminally value, so the two coincide. The clogic grants that the
  book gives its account of "should" later. I think the point stands for this post as
  written, but it is the note most open to the defender's reply.
- "This argument does not bear on that view" (paragraph 9): the first argument's target is
  "instrumental means of obtaining a warm glow of satisfaction", i.e. egoism. I also say
  "Against that the argument works", so the note is not a dismissal.
- "The post does not consider this reply" (conclusion paragraph and honest n.b.): checked the
  whole post; it has no discussion of whether the intuitions might be biased.
- De Brigard is a small study (24 per condition, undergraduates). The note reports it as
  "Experiments suggest", and the Response as "give it some support". A later literature
  (e.g., Weijers 2014) exists that I did not read.
- Moore, Sidgwick and Nozick are given as credit and context. The post does not claim the
  cases as new, which the Response says.
- The "Which means" clogic: mild; the post's link to Thou Art Godshatter is acknowledged.
- Reserved words: "never" in the Summary is the post's own word ("I'll make sure never to
  take them"). No other reserved words. The pronoun flag is "his" inside a Moore quotation.
- Honest section is 518 words, slightly over 500 with six n.b. notes.
