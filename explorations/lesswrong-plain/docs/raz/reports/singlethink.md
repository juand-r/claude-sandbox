# Report: singlethink ("Singlethink", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-10-06)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

At fifteen the author realized that a pleasing childhood memory, of refusing a camp spanking game out of a principled refusal to hurt others and to play a negative-sum game, was untrue: the child had refused only to avoid being hurt, and the author had always known this and looked away from it. Having caught the feeling of pushing an unwanted truth into a corner of the mind, the author resolved to notice it every time and named the skill "singlethink", after Orwell's doublethink: notice that you are forgetting, and remember. The mechanisms of confirmation bias go deep, but this first skill cleared many such corners.

## 6. Judgment calls

- Paragraph 6 note (Prisoner's Dilemma rules out only the negative-sum framing). I think this
  is exact against the text: paragraph 5 gives two meanings ("refused to take joy in hurting
  others"; "refused to play a negative-sum game"), and paragraph 6's evidence touches only
  the second. A defender could say "I didn't want to get hurt" was simply recalled, not
  inferred from the PD point; the next note (paragraph 7) addresses exactly that: the recall
  is introspection.
- Paragraph 7 note and Response: "could be wrong" (reserved word "wrong", modal use). The
  post itself says the first reading "wasn't true", so "could be confidently wrong" is the
  post's own finding. "Flattering" (motive flag) describes the memory the post calls
  "pleasantly self-righteous"; not a motive reading.
- Paragraph 13 note ("rests on the author's word"): the post gives no second corner. I read
  the whole post for any other example; there is none.
- I did not note the post's use of "confirmation bias" for motivated forgetting (a loose but
  common broad use); judged a wording nitpick.
