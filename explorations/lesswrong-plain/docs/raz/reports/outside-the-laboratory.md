# Report: Outside the Laboratory (order 189)

Agent: batch 4a. Slug: `outside-the-laboratory`. Posted 2007-01-21.

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The proverb that scientists are no wiser than anyone else outside the laboratory is partly true,
and that should alarm us, because a scientist who applies experimental rules only in the lab,
like a shepherd who can count sheep but not apples, has learned a ritual without understanding
why it works. The reason for experiments is that to map a territory you must interact with it
causally; a scientist who holds a religious belief "without evidence" or on inner experience
shows that they treat the principle as a social convention, since spirit voices would be a causal
interaction subject to probability theory like any other. Reality is one unified process under
simple laws, as Newton showed for planets and apples, so rationality should apply in everyday life
as much as in the laboratory.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- Economist note: "It assumes that a weekly ticket fails the test of expected utility." The post
  asks a question ("Does this person really understand expected utility...?"); the assumption is
  what makes the question pointed. A defender could say it is only a question. I kept it because
  the question is offered as the analogue of the shepherd who cannot count.
- The conclusion note ("If, outside of their specialist field ...") and the Response say the
  diagnosis rests on an imagined case and no evidence about real scientists. The post's hedges
  ("probably", "to me", "that does make me wonder") are quoted, and the claims are attacked as
  hedged.
- The Pew survey is 2009, two years after the post. I present it as information that fits both
  the premise and its hedge, not as a correction.
- Considered and left out: (a) "significant at the 0.05 confidence level" should be "significance
  level" (a wording slip; nitpick); (b) "Reality ... a single unified process governed by
  mathematically simple low-level rules" stated as learned, while "Is Reality Ugly?" (2008) says
  current theories "are not perfectly integrated with each other" and calls math "by far the best
  guess"; the post's point (no separate magisteria) survives, so no note; (c) "prophets and priests
  follow the ancient human practice of making everything up" is polemic without evidence; described
  neutrally; (d) "It is only scientists who really believe it" is marked "stated without evidence"
  in the Newton note, a mild call.
