# Report: resist-the-happy-death-spiral

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Some ideas really are that good (Bacon's scientific method is the example), so the cure for
a happy death spiral cannot be refusing to admire anything, nor a biased hunt for negatives,
nor confining the idea to a narrow box, since science does bear on nearly everything. The
cure is to treat each additional nice claim as a burdensome detail and ask for the specific
causal chain behind it, so that the halo stays subcritical; claims that are merely
"unproven" should not add to one's happiness. Stuart Armstrong's advice, to split the Great
Idea into independent parts and judge each separately, serves the same end, and the post
closes with a list of what does and does not work.

## 6. Judgment calls

- \cpara on the paragraph that drops the ethical-responsibility example: the main criticism.
  It rests on the post's own words ("not beyond dispute", and later "avoid being happy about
  unsettled claims"). A defender could say the absurd examples are only illustrations and the
  method is general. The note says only that the method is shown on no disputed claim.
- \cfact on Bacon: I first wrote "a timetable that did not come true" and "wrong in others";
  I weakened both because Bacon's timetable is conditional ("If we had but any one who
  could..."). Now: "Four centuries of answered questions have not completed that work."
- \clogic on the cached thoughts: the post's motive reading is hedged ("Probably"), and I keep
  the hedge. The GAC is evidence that scientists voiced the hydrogen-bomb worry early; it does
  not show who first said "air conditioning ... hydrogen bomb". The note says "prominent early
  versions", not "originators".

  "far less likely" is the author's addition; Armstrong wrote "If we can do that, we should be
  safe".

  nice claim", "wrong ways"). Pronouns: "he/his" for Bacon, "her" for Rothblatt.
