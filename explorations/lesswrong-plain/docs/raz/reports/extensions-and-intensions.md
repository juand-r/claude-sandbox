# Report: extensions-and-intensions

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Words can be defined by other words ("intensional definition," which risks an endless
regress, as in Harnad's Chinese/Chinese dictionary) or by pointing to examples
("extensional definition"), and Peirce's recipe for finding lithium is an intensional
procedure that leads to an extensional example. The concept itself is different from
either kind of definition: its "actual intension" is a neural membership test and its
"actual extension" is everything one would call by the word, and neither can be fully
conveyed, so definitions are "treasure maps, not treasure." Therefore you cannot define a
word any way you like: you cannot program concepts into other people, and you cannot
freely change both a concept's test and its members, because the test is applied below
awareness; this is why "by definition" arguments are popular.
