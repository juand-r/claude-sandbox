# Report: anthropomorphic-optimism ("Anthropomorphic Optimism", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-08-04)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Anthropomorphism is expecting a process whose causal structure differs from a human brain's
to do what your own brain would predict; the group selectionists, who expected group selection
to produce voluntary restraint in breeding when Wade's experiment produced cannibalism, are the
example. The author's explanation is that the brain searches only among solutions that rank high
in its own preferences, so it never generates solutions like cannibalism, while evolution ranks
options by a different criterion; the biologists then disguised this by rationalizing why
evolution itself would favor their preferred outcome, an instinct humans may have evolved for
political argument. The same error underlies optimism in general, and (as "subtext") optimism
about what an AI will do: ranking outcomes by your preferences and taking the top one as a
prediction.
