# Report: anthropomorphic-optimism ("Anthropomorphic Optimism", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-08-04)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Anthropomorphism is expecting a process whose causal structure differs from a human brain's
to do what your own brain would predict; the group selectionists, who expected group selection
to produce voluntary restraint in breeding when Wade's experiment produced cannibalism, are the
example. The author's explanation is that the brain searches only among solutions that rank high
in its own preferences, so it never generates solutions like cannibalism, while evolution ranks
options by a different criterion; the biologists then disguised this by rationalizing why
evolution itself would favor their preferred outcome, an instinct humans may have evolved for
political argument. The same error underlies optimism in general, and (as "subtext") optimism
about what an AI will do: ranking outcomes by your preferences and taking the top one as a
prediction.

## 6. Judgment calls

1. The main clogic (the biologists' reasoning is inferred from their conclusion; no record
   quoted). It is a claim about what the post does, not about the biologists' minds. It adds
   Wynne-Edwards's own account and says it "does not settle the question either way".
2. cfact on "quiet peace": uses a 1980 retrospective summary of the 1962 theory. A fair defender
   could say the phrase describes the insects' hoped-for outcome, not Wynne-Edwards's words. The
   note says only that it is not how he described his theory.
3. Pronouns: "he/his/him" for Wynne-Edwards (historical figure, documented) are flagged by
   raz_check; allowed by STANDARDS 2.6. The author is never "he".
4. "honestly" flag: it occurs only inside the post's own quoted words.
5. The Response's point that the post does not "look at the cognitive history" uses the post's
   own last paragraph; it is not a charge of hypocrisy and is worded as a fact about the example.
