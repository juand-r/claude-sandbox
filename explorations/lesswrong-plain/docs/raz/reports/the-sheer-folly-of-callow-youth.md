# Report: the-sheer-folly-of-callow-youth

Batch 6a (Book VI, Yudkowsky's Coming of Age, order 303). Posted 2008-09-19.

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The author's early "mysterious answer" concerned morality as well as consciousness: in 1997 the
young author argued that the case "life is meaningless" cancels out of any expected-utility
calculation, so one may act as if life has meaning and build a superintelligence to find that
meaning, and treated the "ought" problem, existence and qualia as linked "hard problems" needing
new physics. The author diagnoses the errors as treating utility as an absolute quantity,
treating "should" as whatever compels any mind (and equating that with "right"), and using the
"proof" as a reason not to define "intelligence" or "meaning", and says the young author did not
turn on this position the refutation skill used on others. The lessons: no justification for
relaxing rigor protects you, because Nature grades only whether you meet the standard; one may
think with vague concepts but not put one's weight on a "best guess" or build on an interim
understanding; and confusion cannot be worked around, only dissolved.
