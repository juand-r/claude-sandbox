# Report: thou-art-physics ("Thou Art Physics", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-06-06; book order 245)

Agent batch 4c. Scratch folder: `data/sources/4c_thou-art-physics/`.

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

People who hear that physics is deterministic picture "Me" and "Physics" as rival causes of
the future, so that if physics settles the future there is no room for them; the right
picture puts "Me" inside physics, and the gap goes unseen only because the levels between
thoughts and quarks cannot be visualized the way fingers making up a hand can. The author's
position, "Requiredism", is that choice, control and responsibility, sensibly understood,
require determinism (at least patches of it), since one cannot plan and act amid utter chaos;
if the future were not determined by physics, it would have to be determined by something,
and that something would include you. Physics includes our decisions and does not explain
them away; confusion comes from our cognitive algorithms, not from physics.

## 6. Judgment calls

- Note on "Or perhaps I should say" (agent causation). The post's weaker formula "determined by something" is accepted by agent-causal libertarians, and only "some law, some order" excludes them. A defender could reply that the paragraph as a whole means lawful order. The note concedes that the clause excludes them and says only that it is asserted.
- Note on "utter chaos". I say the libertarian views in the literature "do not posit chaos" and that the post "does not address these views". The post never mentions libertarianism; the note is scope, but it bears on the post's argument, since the argument against indeterminism is the post's own. I gave the luck objection in the SEP's words (accounts "often thought to be vulnerable"), so the note does not take sides in the dispute.
- "Requiredism is a form of compatibilism": logic (if X requires determinism, X is compatible with it), and consistent with the Wrong Questions notes. The post does not claim novelty; the note says so.
- The note that the brain-network claim is "stated as fact, with no evidence": the paragraph has no hedge ("It just seems like the way things are" describes the feeling, not a hedge on the claim).
- "The future is determined by physics" note: information, not a charge. It could be cut as a nitpick if the editor thinks the post's "determined" is loose on purpose.
- Reserved word flag: "false" appears only in the SEP-derived definition of libertarianism.
- Pronoun flags: all in quotations (Hume, Anscombe) or for historical figures (Lewis; "she" for Anscombe in the honest n.b.).
