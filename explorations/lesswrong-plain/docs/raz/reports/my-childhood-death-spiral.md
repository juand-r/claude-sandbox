# Report: my-childhood-death-spiral

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The author's parents dismissed arguments from a bright child by appealing to experience,
which left the young Yudkowsky convinced that intelligence mattered above everything; the
author now sees that the parents lost those arguments not because a child was smarter but
because they used their intelligence against itself. The young Yudkowsky of 1995 guarded
against the obvious failures (no claims of superiority, everyone needed to be smarter), but
on meeting the idea of the Singularity in 1996 fell into a "happy death spiral" around
intelligence, which led to false beliefs such as that the lightspeed limit would not bind a
superintelligence. The real wrong turn was answering "intelligence has nothing to do with
being good" by concluding that superintelligence would necessarily imply supermorality; a
postscript says what has changed in the author's view since.

## 6. Judgment calls

- The "No line drawn between himself and others" note quotes the 1996/1999 essay on both
  sides ("just how dumb I am"; "an actual enhanced human such as myself"; its "blind spots").
  The essay is later than the 1995 self the paragraph describes, and the note says so. The
  editor may judge this a reach; I kept it because the brief asks for descriptions of the
  early self to be checked against the archive, and it states no motive.
- The Principles' "(probably true)": I read the parentheses as the author's verdicts at the
  time of writing (the parallel "(unknown)" supports this). On that reading the belief was
  still held as probable in 2000 or 2001. If the editor reads it otherwise, the sentence
  "so it was still held as probable in 2000 or 2001" should go (note and Response).
- An earlier draft said the archive held the belief "at 95 per cent rather than as a
  necessity". I withdrew it: the Principles says "I was sure", which fits the post's
  "necessarily".
- The "We are the cards" paragraph: I note only the ambiguity of whose view it is and the
  postscript's revision; I did not charge the unfairness ranking or the reading of others'
  motives ("one way of turning away"), which the post hedges.
- Reserved words: "false" and "wrong" appear only as the post's own terms ("false happy
  beliefs", "wrong turn"). The pronoun flag ("himself") is inside a quotation from the post.
- Credit note (clogic on "supermorality"): the case fits the halo effect and supplies a real
  example missing from Resist. This is my observation; it is marked as what the case does,
  not as the post's claim.
