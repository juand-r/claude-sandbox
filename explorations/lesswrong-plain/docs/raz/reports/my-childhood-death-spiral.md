# Report: my-childhood-death-spiral

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The author's parents dismissed arguments from a bright child by appealing to experience,
which left the young Yudkowsky convinced that intelligence mattered above everything; the
author now sees that the parents lost those arguments not because a child was smarter but
because they used their intelligence against itself. The young Yudkowsky of 1995 guarded
against the obvious failures (no claims of superiority, everyone needed to be smarter), but
on meeting the idea of the Singularity in 1996 fell into a "happy death spiral" around
intelligence, which led to false beliefs such as that the lightspeed limit would not bind a
superintelligence. The real wrong turn was answering "intelligence has nothing to do with
being good" by concluding that superintelligence would necessarily imply supermorality; a
postscript says what has changed in the author's view since.
