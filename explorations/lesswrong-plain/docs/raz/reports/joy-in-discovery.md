# Report: joy-in-discovery

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Discovering something first is said to be a tremendous high, and the author asks why a discovery should be worth less because someone else knows the answer, weighing three readings of the joy: long struggle and deep understanding, effort justification, and status. If the joy requires being first, then in a universe large enough to hold other Newtons (spatially infinite, inflationary, branching) it is never available, which the author treats as a reductio. The consistent solution is to treat mystery as personal: if you do not know, it is a mystery to you, which restores wonder to everything you do not personally understand, while being first for one's civilization remains a separate, one-shot reward.
