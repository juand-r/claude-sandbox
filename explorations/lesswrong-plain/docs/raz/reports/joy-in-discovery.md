# Report: joy-in-discovery

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Discovering something first is said to be a tremendous high, and the author asks why a discovery should be worth less because someone else knows the answer, weighing three readings of the joy: long struggle and deep understanding, effort justification, and status. If the joy requires being first, then in a universe large enough to hold other Newtons (spatially infinite, inflationary, branching) it is never available, which the author treats as a reductio. The consistent solution is to treat mystery as personal: if you do not know, it is a mystery to you, which restores wonder to everything you do not personally understand, while being first for one's civilization remains a separate, one-shot reward.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. The wine clogic ("does not fit the reading it illustrates"). The post lists the wine result under valuing something "just because it took more work to get it". A fair defender could say price is a kind of cost. In the study the price was only a label the tasters were told; the note says that and no more.
2. The Standard Model cfact: "Loosely worded". The argument survives (it needs only infinite space, which Tegmark counts as part of the concordance model), so per the Book III lesson it is kept out of the "In short" line.

The post itself frames scarcity "one person per civilization" before the physics; I read the reductio as aimed at the universe-wide version. Please check this is not "you read my term against its meaning".
