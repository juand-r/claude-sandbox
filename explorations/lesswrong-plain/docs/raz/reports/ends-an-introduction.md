# Report: ends-an-introduction

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Value theory, the study of what people care about (including what we wish we cared about),
matters because humans do not act as they wish they would, are not consistent enough to have
utility functions, and misjudge even what they wish; philosophers disagree about what we
want, what we ought to want and what "ought" means, and the history of moral theory is a
record of failed master principles. Book V, *Mere Goodness*, collects three sequences (Fake
Preferences, Value Theory, Quantified Humanism), of which the last, applying theory to
practice, is called the most important, since a normative theory's worth is how well it
guides practice, and the answer to when to trust intuition over theory is case by case.
The introduction then poses the book's two recurring questions: where we should go (fun
theory, transhumanism, hedonism against eudaimonia, the neglect of utopia-planning) and how
ends relate to means (journey against destination), ending by asking what we mean by
"valuable".

## 6. Judgment calls

- Skeleton hand fix: the original's second footnote paragraph begins with a bare "######"
  (a Markdown h6 marker with no number), which md2tex printed as "\#\#\#\#\#\#". I removed
  those six characters from the skeleton line; check_verbatim then prints OK. The same
  pattern occurs once elsewhere (an-intuitive-explanation-of-bayes-s-theorem.md, line 350,
  an empty "###### "). md2tex is unchanged.
- The value-theory note says the definition is the book's own stipulation. This is
  information, not a fault; the editor may think it a nitpick for an introduction.
- The pragmatist note ("stated, not argued"; "close to the pragmatist view"): the "In short"
  line repeats "does not argue". It is a premise the book's ranking rests on, so I kept it in
  the verdict. An editor might treat it as a preface convention (STANDARDS 2.4, item 7) and
  drop it from the "In short" line.
- "Without a source" on utopia-planning's neglect: the claim is about the state of a field,
  so I noted it. The "In short" line carries it; it could be moved out of the verdict as minor.
- Popper: given as information about the other side, not as a charge. The honest n.b. says
  "a well-known case", matching the note.
- Sandel: the introduction's gloss is loose rather than wrong; the note says "gives the last
  part". Could be cut as a nitpick.
- No reserved words in my text. No pronouns for Bensinger or Sandel.
