# Report: the-simple-truth ("The Simple Truth", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-01-01)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

A belief is true when it matches the world, as an empty bucket of pebbles matches an empty
pasture, and the match is produced by a causal process in which the world controls the
belief (sheep cause pebbles), not the other way round. The match is worth having because it
lets you act without loss (stop searching and lose no sheep), and it works whether or not
anyone believes in it; changing the pebbles does not change the sheep. The objections put in
the mouth of Mark (truth is subjective or cultural, magic comes from coherence among buckets,
there is no reality but only beliefs, truth is a tool of power) are shown to be confused,
self-refuting or admitted excuses, and the dialogue ends with the world overruling Mark when
he steps off a cliff.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- Scope of criticism. The post says it is "not meant as an encyclopedic reference" and aims at mailing-list arguers. I say so in cpara line 15 and in the Response, and the critical notes are framed as "as satire this is fair; as a reply to the named positions it does not reach them". A fair defender could still say the post never claimed to answer Rorty or Putnam. My reply is that the dialogue uses the literature's names ("coherentist theory", "reflexivity problem", "many adherents and an extensive literature"), so the reader is invited to take Mark as that literature's spokesman. If you think this is outside what the post claims, the notes at lines 127, 311, 367, 379 and the Response's last paragraph are the ones to soften.
- Pragmatism. The brief suggested checking pragmatism. The post does not name pragmatism anywhere. I did not say the post misrepresents pragmatism. Instead: Mark's "really really believe" resembles Rorty (a pragmatist); the shepherd's definition of reality is close to Peirce's (the founder). Net point: the shepherd is nearer pragmatism than Mark. Mark's line about truth as what is "advantageous to your own social group" resembles the popular reading of James ("the true is only the expedient"), which the SEP pragmatism entry says Russell attacked and calls "unfair"; I left this out as too far from what the post says.
- Realism versus correspondence (clogic line 307, Response paragraph 2, In short). This is the main substantive charge. The post does present a correspondence view, and its pebble model is a correspondence model; the charge is only that its arguments against Mark establish realism, which deflationists share. A defender could say: the post never claimed to refute deflationism. The Response says "defends ... against people who deny reality, not against competing theories of truth", which I think is accurate and not a charge of error.
- Scope objection (clogic line 109). "does not take up those other domains" is true of the text; the defence "not encyclopedic" applies. I kept it because the aim sentence is unrestricted.
- Credit notes (lines 153, 179, 313, 321): "names none of this work" / "names no one". The post does not claim novelty, and the Response says so explicitly ("though it does not claim them as new"). Line 179's link to Searle is an identification of a phrase, not a claim that the post targets Searle.
- Reserved words: "false" twice, both in "a false proposition can cohere with some set of propositions" (a statement of Russell's objection, not a verdict on the post). No other reserved words.
- Motive flags: "inference" twice, both about logical inferences, not motives. Line 389 says Darwin's test "is not meant to persuade Mark": a reading of a character's intent, supported by Darwin's "you shall both be happy".
- Pronouns: he/his for Mark, Autrey, Darwin (the post's own). Rorty ("he does not hold") and Stove ("his prize") are historical figures with documented pronouns. Hales restructured. The shepherd, the author's first-person narrator, gets no pronoun. The post's author gets none.
- "It reaches none of them" (Response) and "do not reach anyone who holds these views as a position" (line 367): strong, but conditional on the dialogue's own framing (Mark says his statements are excuses, "not so much a model"). Could be softened to "it does not engage them" if you prefer.
