# Report: money-the-unit-of-caring ("Money: The Unit of Caring", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-03-31)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

From Omohundro's resource balance principle, the post argues that any approximately rational
system has a common currency of expected utilons, and that in our society this currency is
money, "the measure of how much society cares about something." Its central example is the
lawyer who volunteers an hour at a soup kitchen instead of working an extra hour and paying
for five hours of a specialist's work: by comparative advantage and specialization, giving
money gets more done, except for the underemployed or for large blocks of the volunteer's own
specialized skill, and volunteering to stay motivated is fine if money is given too. So money
is the unit of relative caring, giving it is not a lesser form of caring, and someone never
willing to spend any money on a thing does not care about it.
