# Report: money-the-unit-of-caring ("Money: The Unit of Caring", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-03-31)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

From Omohundro's resource balance principle, the post argues that any approximately rational
system has a common currency of expected utilons, and that in our society this currency is
money, "the measure of how much society cares about something." Its central example is the
lawyer who volunteers an hour at a soup kitchen instead of working an extra hour and paying
for five hours of a specialist's work: by comparative advantage and specialization, giving
money gets more done, except for the underemployed or for large blocks of the volunteer's own
specialized skill, and volunteering to stay motivated is fine if money is given too. So money
is the unit of relative caring, giving it is not a lesser form of caring, and someone never
willing to spend any money on a thing does not care about it.

## 3. Arithmetic

- Lawyer example: one hour of the lawyer's pay buys five hours of a hired worker only if the lawyer's (after-tax, net of
  any deduction) hourly pay is about 5 times the worker's wage. Stated in the note as "about five times"; taxes ignored.
- Dates: this post 2009-03-31; "Helpless Individuals" 2009-03-30 ("the previous day's post"); "Rationality: Common
  Interest of Many Causes" 2009-03-29; "Purchase Fuzzies" 2009-04-01 ("the next day's post").

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- Reserved words: "Overstated" (Benkler note on "the only way that anything ever gets done in this world"). The claim
  is italicized as the post's thesis, so I read it as meant, not as a figure of speech. The note limits itself to the
  general claim and says Benkler's granularity condition may leave the lawyer example standing. "never" appears only
  in paraphrase of the post's own last line.
- The aggregation note ("Loosely stated for a society") uses the post's own "up to a positive scalar factor" plus a
  secondary source (Wikipedia on cost-benefit analysis). A defender could say the next paragraph ("It's not as if you
  thought 'society' was ... sane") already disclaims this. I think it disclaims wisdom, not weighting by wealth, so I
  kept the note, and the Response names the concession.
- "Calls the point ... No one who denies it is named." Mild; could be cut if the editor thinks it faults what any essay
  does. The Response groups it with the unhedged "mere reflection of a mind" diagnosis and the hedged "may be
  sufficient by itself" claim, and keeps the hedge.
- Credit notes: Omohundro's own words, Prelec and Loewenstein (pain of paying), Unger 1996, Ricardo, Singer 1972. The
  Response credits Singer and Unger plainly and does not say the post fails to name them.
- Hindsight: the 80,000 Hours 2015 note and the Response paragraph are marked "For information, with hindsight" and
  are kept out of the In short line.
- The Armstrong note ("explains the same attitude differently, as status-seeking") sets two explanations side by side;
  it does not endorse Armstrong's.
