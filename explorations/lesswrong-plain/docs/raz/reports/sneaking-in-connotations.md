# Report: sneaking-in-connotations ("Sneaking in Connotations", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-02-19)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

A dictionary lists the few visible features by which members of a category are recognized, not
the many properties (connotations) that people infer from the word; the invented word "wiggin"
(green eyes and black hair) shows a word picking up connotations its definition lacks. When
someone argues "by definition" from a dictionary, they are generally trying to carry over such a
connotation, because a definition that included the property could not be applied before the
property is observed, and one that leaves it out cannot deliver it. The test: an argument that
really stayed inside the definition ("he's a wiggin, therefore he has black hair") would feel
empty, so the felt force of these arguments comes from the smuggled connotation.
