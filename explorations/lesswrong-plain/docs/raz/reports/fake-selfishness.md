# Report: fake-selfishness

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The author tells of meeting a man who called himself purely selfish and urged the author to
be selfish too, and offers the hedged suspicion that a philosophy of selfishness, like a
religion, has little effect on behaviour, because its holders can rationalize whatever they
already want to do. The author asks why a selfish man would want others to be selfish, rejects
his answer (that selfish people would not pass laws against his property) on the grounds that
the author is already libertarian and works for others' benefit, and asks whether converting
others is really the most selfish use of his time. The man concedes that he may have started
from wanting to convert people and only then justified it as self-interest; a footnote lists
further questions (die to save humanity, a stubbed toe against a stranger's torture, an
undetectable theft) meant to show that professed egoists do not act or choose as egoists.

## 6. Judgment calls

- Pronoun flags: "her" in the SEP definition quotation (SEP's wording) and "her" for Ayn Rand
  (well documented); "he/him/himself" for the man in the story (the post uses "himself", "him").
- The P1 clogic and the Response say the conversation concerns one act and the man's reasons.
  I first wrote that it "says nothing about how he treats people", then softened it, because a
  fair defender could say that urging selfishness on the author is itself conduct.
- The P6 note and the Response say the concession does not show the wish to convert was
  kindness, and add "the post does not claim that it does". I think the thesis ("whenever they
  want to be nice to people") invites that reading, but the post never says it; hence the clause.
- The "most selfish thing" clogic credits the question against ethical egoism and limits it
  against psychological egoism. The man professed both; I thought this split was fairer than
  either verdict alone.
- Credit notes (Rand's view, Batson, Gyges) are information, not faults; the Response calls the
  footnote questions "well made". Praise words ("fair", "well made") are deliberate credit.
- Honest section is 511 words, slightly over the 500 guide.
