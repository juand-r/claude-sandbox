# Report: savanna-poets

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Feynman asked why poets can write about Jupiter as a god but not as a sphere of methane and ammonia; the author answers that poetry moves us through human-like agents, and the strongest emotions, found in all cultures, are built into us, so a Jupiter that cannot love or weep is harder to write about. But the Great Stories do not need Jupiter, since humans still exist to enact them, and these stories are not timeless, because the species is not; the author would like some of them to change. Poets who can write only the classic human-shaped stories are "savanna poets"; the true story of the rainbow or of Jupiter is something new, and respecting strangeness means telling its story truly.
