# Report: reversed-stupidity-is-not-intelligence

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Foolish or evil people are not reliably wrong, so their believing something is little or no
evidence that it is false: flying saucer cults would exist whether or not there were
flying saucers, and anyone wrong 99.99% of the time would need to know the truth to manage
it. The halo effect's converse makes people treat anything said by someone evil (Stalin) as
false, but Stalin also believed that 2 + 2 = 4. Corollaries follow: argue against an
idea's strongest advocates, ignore lunatics and ad hominem, notice that affiliation moves
belief, and do not expect that reversing a failed approach will hit the solution.

## 6. Judgment calls

- \clogic on "how much less should human evil anticorrelate": says the post "gives no reason"
  and that a deliberate liar knows the truth. This uses the post's own premise, not an outside
  source. A defender might say "evil" here means malice, not lying; the note only says the
  a fortiori step is unargued and that the Stalin example supports a narrower claim.
- \clogic on "Ad hominem argument is not valid": framed as a clarification (true in the
  logician's sense), not a charge. The Response calls it broader than the principle "as a rule
  about what to believe". Reserved words: "false" appears only in "does not show the claim false".
- \clogic on "Eliezer Yudkowsky post-2003": only says the ranking and the 2003 cut-off are
  unexplained. No motive is assigned. The editor may think this too small for a note.
- \cfact on footnote 3: "the wrong year" in the Response is a reserved word; the evidence is
  the LessWrong postedAt date (2009-03-14) against the footnote's "December 2, 2018".
- Mill notes: the "old idea" point is made as context, not as a charge of uncredited
  borrowing; the post does not claim novelty.
- Pronouns: "his side" (Stalin) and Pirsig's own "he" inside a quotation only.
- Considered and dropped: the Soviet origin of "2 + 2 = 5" (no bearing on the argument); a
  note that "All perceived negative qualities correlate" overstates the horns effect (wording
  nitpick); a note that Piper's scenario makes cults evidence for aliens (the post's "isn't
  less than" allows it).
