# Report: reversed-stupidity-is-not-intelligence

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Foolish or evil people are not reliably wrong, so their believing something is little or no
evidence that it is false: flying saucer cults would exist whether or not there were
flying saucers, and anyone wrong 99.99% of the time would need to know the truth to manage
it. The halo effect's converse makes people treat anything said by someone evil (Stalin) as
false, but Stalin also believed that 2 + 2 = 4. Corollaries follow: argue against an
idea's strongest advocates, ignore lunatics and ad hominem, notice that affiliation moves
belief, and do not expect that reversing a failed approach will hit the solution.
