# Report: hand-vs-fingers ("Hand vs. Fingers", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-03-30; book order 218)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

When you can see how a higher level reduces to a lower one, as you can see a hand as fingers,
thumb and palm, the two levels stop seeming like separate things, and it becomes plainly
silly to ask whether the hand or the fingers pick up the cup. A reasoner who only knows the
fact of reduction (from a scanner showing separate dots, or from being told) can still
imagine the hand moving off without the fingers, but that conceptual possibility is a fact
about the reasoner's map, not a logical or physical possibility, just as 235757 can seem
possibly prime to someone who has not factored it. Philosophers who reason from such
imaginings, whether to deny that hands exist or to posit contingent bridging laws that make
fingers evoke a hand, commit the Mind Projection Fallacy: they take their intuitions for
direct information about reality.

## 6. Judgment calls

1. P9 note and Response paragraph on "physical identity" / composition as identity. The post takes
   the identity view in passing ("of course"), and SEP calls the view "by no means
   uncontroversial". I present it as a stronger claim than the argument needs, not as an error.
   The editor may judge this a dispute the post does not really take up and cut it to a neutral
   credit (Lewis's "same portion of Reality").
2. The eliminativism note: "the parody changes what it claims". The post says "you can imagine
   philosophers", so a defender may say it is a hypothetical. I kept the note because the name
   "eliminative fingerists" maps onto a real position and the brief asks for rival positions to
   be stated fairly where the post argues against them.
3. The bridging-law clogic: "a real counterpart for consciousness only" rests on Chalmers's
   naturalistic dualism (SEP "Zombies") and his water/genes sentence. I say the book takes the
   question up in the zombie posts (order 225 onward), not that this post is wrong.
4. Chalmers's sentence is hedged ("it seems that"); all three texts keep the hedge.
5. Response and In short: "made on the hand, where it is not disputed". This is a scope point;
   the post does not claim to have settled the mind case. I kept it out of the "wrong" family of
   words.
6. Pronouns: none for the author; Putnam and others are named.
