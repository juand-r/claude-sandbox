# Report: hand-vs-fingers ("Hand vs. Fingers", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-03-30; book order 218)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

When you can see how a higher level reduces to a lower one, as you can see a hand as fingers,
thumb and palm, the two levels stop seeming like separate things, and it becomes plainly
silly to ask whether the hand or the fingers pick up the cup. A reasoner who only knows the
fact of reduction (from a scanner showing separate dots, or from being told) can still
imagine the hand moving off without the fingers, but that conceptual possibility is a fact
about the reasoner's map, not a logical or physical possibility, just as 235757 can seem
possibly prime to someone who has not factored it. Philosophers who reason from such
imaginings, whether to deny that hands exist or to posit contingent bridging laws that make
fingers evoke a hand, commit the Mind Projection Fallacy: they take their intuitions for
direct information about reality.
