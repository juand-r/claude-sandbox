# Report: if-many-worlds-had-come-first

## 1. The argument in three sentences

After a short history of Everett's 1957 proposal and its neglect, the post asks whether the
order of discovery drives the popularity of interpretations: if many-worlds had come first and
collapse had been proposed only in 1957, would collapse now be gaining ground? It answers with
a satire in which Huve Erett presents a collapse postulate to Biels Nohr, who rejects it as
unphysical (non-linear, non-local, informally specified, untestable, unsupported by evidence,
and no explanation of the Born statistics). The joke's reversal is that Huve's imagined
"absurd" world, where physicists knew quantum mechanics for thirty years and never thought
there might be more than one world, is our own.
