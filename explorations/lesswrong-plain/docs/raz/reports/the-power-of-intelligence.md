# Report: the-power-of-intelligence

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The human brain looks harmless, and from the viewpoint of an evolution that armed species
with claws and shells over millions of years, the weak-fingered "Squishy Things" would have
seemed incapable of nuclear weapons, moon landings or reshaping DNA; yet intelligence did all
of it, so objections that an AI could do nothing interesting without a robot body are
misplaced. People's first reaction to the word "intelligence" retrieves book smarts, which
leads them to say that intelligence cannot beat charisma, guns or money, forgetting that
all three come from human brains; the archetype of intelligence is a human being. That we
cannot see how one power could do so many things is a fact about our ignorance, not about
intelligence, which is as real as electricity and far more powerful and dangerous.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. The "hands" note (cpara on "Now explain to me again"): the parable's creatures had
   fingers; the AI in the conclusion has no body; "the post does not say how an AI without
   a body would act on the world". I searched the whole post for a mechanism (money,
   commercialization, guns): none is given. A defender could say the post only rebuts the
   claim that an AI "can't do anything interesting" without a body, and that intelligence
   acting through others is implied by the money example. I judged that implication is not
   stated.
2. The "widening the word" note (charisma): the post states its wide sense openly in the next
   paragraph, and the note says so. The point is that the wide definition answers the AI
   question by definition. Check for fairness.
3. The "species, not one agent" note (guns and money): it is my analysis of whether the
   replies meet the sayings, not an outside alternative explanation. It is in the Response.
   I first placed a version of it at "one trick cured smallpox ... tamed fire" against "one
   fell swoop", and moved it, because the post links "one fell swoop" to "one process", so
   the idiom may mean a single kind of cause rather than a single moment.
4. The MIRI revision note is neutral, as STANDARDS 2.5 allows. The Response gives it one
   sentence.
5. Reserved words: "wrong" only inside the post's quoted "just sounds wrong".
6. The "hundreds of eons" line (there are four geological eons) is a figure of speech; no note.
