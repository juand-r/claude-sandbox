# Report: Do Scientists Already Know This Stuff? (book order 252, batch 4d)

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Answering the commenter poke, who said that forming good hypotheses is learned in scientific
apprenticeship, the author says that many eminent scientists (Penrose, Bohr, the physicists who
reject many-worlds, scientists who talk about the future or about religion) make the same mistakes
as the author's younger self, and that no standard warning against them (mysterious answers, the
conjunction fallacy, the exact probability that has no room for whims) is taught. Standard training,
the post says, is traditional rationality books plus frequentist statistics and paradigm techniques;
great mentors may pass on more, but only rarely and without being able to put it into words. So the
harder skills of reasoning on sparse evidence are not a secret part of standard science, and the lax
social standard of Science, taken as a private standard, lets people believe far too much.

## 6. Judgment calls

- Penrose note: says the post does not mention Penrose's Gödel argument. A defender could say the post's
  point is about consciousness being left mysterious, which the Gödel argument does not answer. I kept
  it as information about why Penrose holds the view, since the post explains the view by a missing
  warning ("I expect").
- Polanyi \clogic: "This takes one side" framing was removed; the note now credits Polanyi's agreement
  and states his contrary view. The Response says the post "takes a side without naming the other".
- Hamming/Feynman note: "had been published, the second in similar words". Hamming's wording is close
  ("What are the important problems of your field?"); Feynman's is related (reporting everything that
  might invalidate an experiment; Millikan), not identical to "guard your expectations from influencing
  the experiment". The Response says "in similar words". The post's claim that mentors have not
  published is conditional ("if the great mentors haven't..."); I wrote "suggested".
- Note on the method paragraph: "the post offers no survey of what scientists are actually taught".
  True, and the Response balances it with the supporting evidence.
- "Believe they believe": I say the post gives no evidence about scientists for it. The author could
  reply that "Belief in Belief" argues it for believers generally; the note names that post.
- Reserved-word flags ("never") were in paraphrases of the post and were changed to "not"/"ever".
