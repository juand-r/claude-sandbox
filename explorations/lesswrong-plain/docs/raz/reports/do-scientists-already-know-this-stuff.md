# Report: Do Scientists Already Know This Stuff? (book order 252, batch 4d)

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Answering the commenter poke, who said that forming good hypotheses is learned in scientific
apprenticeship, the author says that many eminent scientists (Penrose, Bohr, the physicists who
reject many-worlds, scientists who talk about the future or about religion) make the same mistakes
as the author's younger self, and that no standard warning against them (mysterious answers, the
conjunction fallacy, the exact probability that has no room for whims) is taught. Standard training,
the post says, is traditional rationality books plus frequentist statistics and paradigm techniques;
great mentors may pass on more, but only rarely and without being able to put it into words. So the
harder skills of reasoning on sparse evidence are not a secret part of standard science, and the lax
social standard of Science, taken as a private standard, lets people believe far too much.
