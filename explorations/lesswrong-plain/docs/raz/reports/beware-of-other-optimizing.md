# Report: beware-of-other-optimizing ("Beware of Other-Optimizing", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-04-10)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

People who try many self-improvement methods and finally find one that works for them come to
think they have found "the real way", and press it on others, although the long run of methods
that failed for them should have told them that people differ in what works. The author, a
frequent target of such advice, learned that a trusted friend's confident tip is no more likely
to work than a blog post by any intelligent writer, so the right attitude, until someone finds
the deep laws of what works for whom, is to be willing to take "No" for an answer. This matters
most when the adviser holds power or leverage over the other person, because power is easy to
abuse without noticing, people underestimate how different others are, and the ability to tell
laziness from real limits is rare.
