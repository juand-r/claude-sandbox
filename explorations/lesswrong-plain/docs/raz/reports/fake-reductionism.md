# Report: fake-reductionism

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The author guesses that Keats did not understand the rainbow's explanation himself, but had only heard that science had explained it, citing the toast "Confusion to the memory of Newton". Such a person has a "fake reduction": told that something was reduced, but without seeing how, so the explanation only moves the rainbow into the "literary genre" of science, labelled boring by authority. This, the author suggests, accounts for much of the existential emptiness said to accompany reductionism, and is how anti-reductionists experience it.
