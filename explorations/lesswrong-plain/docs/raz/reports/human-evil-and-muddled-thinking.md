# Report: human-evil-and-muddled-thinking

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Orwell saw that human evil and muddled thinking go together: political language defends
the indefensible with euphemism, and clear, simple English makes one's own stupidity
obvious, which is the aim of Overcoming Bias. Evil depends on keeping its victims' and
bystanders' revulsion latent, so it sets out to muddle thinking; 1984's villains falsify
records and torture Winston into saying two and two make five, and people like Tyler
Cowen who doubt that overcoming bias is important resemble those Orwell argued against.
Wherever there is evil there are biases shielding it, so the truth has enemies, and the
fight against our own stupidity is a war in which we are soldiers.

## 6. Judgment calls

- \cfact on "planned famines": "go beyond the record as it is usually summarized". For the Soviet
  famine the dispute is explicit in the source; for the Chinese famine the claim is that intent
  to starve is not among the listed causes. An editor may want primary sources here.
- Three Cowen notes (\cfact unimportant, \cfact truncated sentence, \clogic scope insensitivity)
  plus the Chase \clogic. They make different points, but together they are the heaviest part of
  the annotation. "Does not match Cowen's post" in the Response is not a reserved word; I avoided
  "misquotes" because the cut quotation keeps Cowen's words and the dropped clause is a
  qualification ("especially"), not a reversal.
- \clogic on "this is a war, and we are soldiers": "sits uneasily". The note grants that the
  enemy here is "ourselves", which is the fair defender's answer.
- \clogic on "muddled language is muddled thinking": a stronger paraphrase of Orwell. The
  editor may think this is a figure of speech; I kept it because the Response's point (Orwell
  widened) rests on it and on the "In our time" note.
- Pronouns: Winston keeps "him" (character); no pronouns for the author or for Cowen.
- Considered and dropped: the "Great Leap Forward" echo in the last paragraph (wordplay); the
  Inquisition and witches (the Roman Inquisition did try witchcraft cases; no clear error);
  "conjugate strands of DNA" (DNA strands are complementary; a wording nitpick).
