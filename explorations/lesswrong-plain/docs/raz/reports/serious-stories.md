# Report: serious-stories

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Starting from the writing manuals' rule that stories need conflict and pain, and Orwell's
observation that utopias are places no one would want to live, the post considers the
argument that a world without pain would have no stories worth reading and so would not be
worth living in; it grants that a story and a life are optimized by different criteria, but
takes the bad feeling of stories where everything goes right as a reason to ask whether a
life without pain would feel the same. It then argues that human pain is far easier to
produce and far more intense than pleasure, and guesses that pain, hedonic adaptation and
the "new floor" problem could be engineered away, so that David Pearce's abolition of
suffering is "very probably" feasible. Against complete abolition it sets a "path of
courage" (remove the pain that destroys minds, make people stronger, keep lesser sorrows),
admits this appeals to the author partly from conservatism and perhaps from fear, and ends
without deciding: "I don't know."
