# Report: professing-and-cheering

## 1. The argument in three sentences

At a panel on science and religion, a pagan panelist recited the Norse creation myth with an
odd pride, knowing it was scientifically absurd and not trying to be convincing. Neither
Dennett's "belief in belief" nor his "religious profession" (saying the belief aloud) fits
this, because both would require her to pretend to believe more convincingly. The author
concludes that she was cheering for paganism, like a banner at a football game, and that
the absurdity was the point, as marching naked is the point of an outrageous march.

## 6. Judgment calls

- The main criticism (note on the third paragraph, Response, n.b.) is that the post does not
  consider a symbolic reading of the myth. It is an alternative explanation, so under
  STANDARDS 2.4(5) it needs a source: the Wikipedia/Heathenry summary. The sourced claim is
  about Norse cosmology and Ragnarok, not the creation myth specifically; the note says
  exactly that and adds that most Heathens believe the gods exist. We do not know the
  panelist's tradition; the post calls her "a pagan" and she told the Norse myth. If the
  editor thinks the source is too indirect, the note can be cut back to the textual point
  (her two remarks, and the quotation marks around "believed").
- The \clogic on "clearly" is a fairly small point; the fair defender could say her
  explaining the myth by the Vikings' landscape shows her knowledge. The note says only that
  "clearly" stands where evidence would go and names the one view on science she is reported
  to hold. Keep or cut.
- Reserved-word flag: "wrong" appears only in the description of the author's footnote
  ("nothing is wrong with ..."). Pronoun flags: all refer to the panelist (the post's "she")
  or to Ymir ("his body"); none to the author.
