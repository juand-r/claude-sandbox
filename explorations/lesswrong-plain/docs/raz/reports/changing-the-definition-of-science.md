# Report: Changing the Definition of Science (book order 254, batch 4d)

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Quoting a New Scientist article in which the philosopher Colin Howson proposes replacing Popper's
falsificationism with a Bayesian account of science based on degrees of belief, the author says that
the author's own view is less isolated than it seems. The author then lists three differences from
this mainstream Bayesian reformulation of science: the author, outside academia, can say "extreme"
things; teaching probability theory will not be enough without a new synthesis of bias research and
social psychology (shown by Aumann, a Bayesian theorist who is an Orthodox Jew), which the author
says has not been suggested elsewhere; and much better reasoning than science's is possible. The
extreme case is a Bayesian superintelligence that could infer Newton's laws and consider general
relativity from the first falling apple it saw, while humans and academia process evidence
inefficiently.
