# Report: when-anthropomorphism-became-stupid ("When Anthropomorphism Became Stupid", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-08-16; book order 222)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Animism, the belief that trees, rivers and rocks have minds, was not an obviously stupid
mistake for earlier cultures: people reasoned by analogy from their own bodies and wills, and
nothing they could see ruled it out. It became obviously wrong only gradually, once people
knew that thought is done by the brain's tangled neural circuitry, that complex functional
structure comes from natural selection, and that cognition is computation. With that
knowledge one can look at a tree, a river, an atom, a puppy or a rock and give a reason why
it lacks a mind (or moral reasoning), which is why the post ends "Now we know" after the
Zhuangzi story of the happy fish.

   perceive ... they have no mean of contrary qualities" (the theory-of-perception ground).

## 6. Judgment calls

1. The microscope note ("sits uneasily with Aristotle"). A defender can say Aristotle's view
   of plants was a theory, not something "obvious", and the post allows gradual change. The
   note concedes "How obvious this was is a matter of degree the post does not measure" and
   claims only that microscopes were not needed to reach the view. Please check this is not too
   strong. I first wrote that Aristotle's grounds were behavioural; the De Anima text gives
   observation of growth and a theory of perception, so I changed it.
2. The rock cfact ("Aristotle denied that falling bodies move themselves"). Strong wording but
   directly supported by Physics VIII.4. A defender may call "intentions to want" a figure for
   Aristotle's teleology; the note grants the teleology.
3. The Hume credit and the Galen/Herophilus history are offered as information. The post
   hedges its history heavily ("I do not know enough history"), so the notes do not fault it for
   not knowing them; the Response says "The post does not mention this history" once.
4. "Now we know." I did not comment on what it claims (it reads as a joke); the cpara only says
   it is left for the reader. An earlier draft said the post's tests do not address what fish
   enjoy; I cut it as taking a joke literally.
5. Pronouns: he/his used only for Aristotle, Alcmaeon, Galen, Hume (historical figures).
6. Reserved-word flags: "wrong" appears only in paraphrase of the post's own "obviously wrong".
