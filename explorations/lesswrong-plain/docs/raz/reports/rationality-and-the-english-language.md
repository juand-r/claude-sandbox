# Report: rationality-and-the-english-language

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Orwell's writing on language belongs with the classic rationalist texts, because the way a
thing is said shapes what the audience sees: the passive voice and static noun phrases
("Unreliable elements were subjected to an alternative justice process") remove the actor
and keep the facts from outraging anyone. A statement's "public rationality" lies in the
experience its words create, which a writer attends to and a charitable, analytic reader
misses; so "Meaning does not excuse impact," and an applause light cannot be excused by
the rational reading one could construct for it. Orwell's passages on stock phrases and on
letting the meaning choose the word state the same lesson.

## 6. Judgment calls

- \clogic on "Passive voice removes the actor": "Loosely stated". The support is the OED
  definition (via Liberman) and Orwell's own plain version being an agentless passive. I did not
  use "false" or "wrong": agentless passives do remove the actor, which is the post's case.
- \clogic on "Whatever the audience thinks you said": says the post applies a rule for one's own
  words to other people's statements. The author's 2007-09-14 comment confirms the passage was
  about readers; so the move is real. The Response grants that judging public speech by its
  impression "can be a fair standard".
- \clogic on the Progenitorivox sentence: "the author's own impression ... No reader's response
  is cited." A defender might say any essay reports the writer's impression (fair-defender 4).
  I kept it because "impact" is the post's thesis and this is its only evidence for it.
- \cstyle on "are trained": turns the post's rule on itself; supported by the commenter's
  question. The editor may judge it a nitpick (THIRD_PASS 2); it is in the Response as one
  sentence.
- Pronouns: Orwell (historical figure) gets he/his; no pronouns for the author.
- Considered and dropped: Orwell's own high rate of passives (about Orwell, not the post);
  Sniffnoy's 2009 comment making the same agentless-passive point (the Liberman source is better);
  the word "cliches" in the post (no accent) as a nitpick.
