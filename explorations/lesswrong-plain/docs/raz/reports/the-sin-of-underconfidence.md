# Report: the-sin-of-underconfidence ("The Sin of Underconfidence", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-04-20)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Underconfidence is the third of three besetting sins of rationalists: people who have learned
many ways humans err, and who feel that modesty is safer than pride, may correct past the mark
and pass up opportunities. The author's remedy is to test hypotheses about one's own abilities
(the AI-Box games, a proposed unprepared debate), to seek challenges that could humiliate but
not destroy, to keep trying to win while accepting that losing hurts, and to watch for a slow
loss of momentum in which self-doubts are raised and never tested. The general check is to ask
of any habit of thought, "Does this way of thinking make me stronger, or weaker?", since
underconfidence is a "stopping mistake" that prevents the experience that would correct it.
