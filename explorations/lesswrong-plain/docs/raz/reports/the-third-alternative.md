# Report: the-third-alternative ("The Third Alternative", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-05-06)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

A Noble Lie such as Santa Claus is usually defended by showing that it beats doing nothing,
which is a false dilemma: a policy better than the null action need not be the best, and a
Third Alternative (science fiction for wonder, praise instead of bribes) can usually supply
the benefit without the lie. Such false dilemmas persist because people who find a
convenient, defensible policy stop searching at once, while an inconvenient option makes
them keep looking. So beware of defending a policy as "defensible" rather than "optimal",
ask whether you would be glad to find a better option, and spend five minutes by the clock
looking for one.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- Reserved words: "false" appears only in "false dilemma(s)", the post's own term. "correct"
  (credit) is used for the central point, which is standard logic.
- The Deci note (sentence note on "unconditional good behavior") mixes credit (direction
  supported) and scope (tasks, not good behavior; averages). The meta-analysis measures
  free-choice behavior when the experimenter is absent, which is close to "when adults are
  watching"; I said "interest in tasks, not good behavior" because the dependent measure is
  time spent on an interesting task. A fair defender might call this note a nitpick on an
  illustrative example. Cut it if so.
- Plato note, dropped. I first drafted a note that the term's source (Republic, Book III)
  shows a Noble Liar devising a new lie ("How then may we devise one of those needful
  falsehoods"). But Socrates says in the next line "Nothing new, I replied; only an old
  Phoenician tale", so a fair defender could turn it round. Replaced by the plain point that no
  Noble Liar is named.
- "Modern cognitive psychology views..." note uses the author's later post to call the phrase
  "broader than the author's own later account". This is a comparison of two texts, not a
  charge of contradiction.
- The introspection note (paragraph 11) is the Response's main criticism of the advice. The
  post might reply that a "tiny flash of reluctance" is a feeling one can notice even if its
  cause is subconscious. I kept "does not say why a direct question would reach them", which
  is literally true, and credited the five-minute rule as not depending on introspection.
- The proverb: the cpara on paragraph 6 states its usual sense; the "Beware" cpara uses it as
  one of two reasons the warning sits uneasily. I count this as one point made once, with a
  back reference.
