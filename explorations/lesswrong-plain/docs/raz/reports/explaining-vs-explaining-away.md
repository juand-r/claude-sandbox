# Report: explaining-vs-explaining-away

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Keats's lines in Lamia lump together three things "cold philosophy" destroyed: haunts in the air, gnomes in the mine, and the rainbow; but the rainbow was explained (it is still there), while the haunts and gnomes were explained away (they never existed). Anti-reductionists miss this distinction, as the classic objection "your belief in reductionism is the mere result of molecules" shows: a causal account of a belief need not debunk it, since the causal chain can run through the fact believed. The confusion comes from the Mind Projection Fallacy: hearing "there are no fundamental rainbows" as "there are no rainbows", and feeling the removal of a belief from one's map as the removal of a thing from the world.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. The main note (clogic after "has destroyed:") and the Response's second paragraph: the claim is that the quoted lines say the rainbow became common, not that it ceased to exist, while granting that the omitted line (Lamia melting "into a shade") supports the post. A fair defender could say "Unweave a rainbow" itself implies destruction. I wrote "also say" and "some support" to keep both readings visible. Check that this is not a nitpick on a figure of speech; I think it is not, since the post's whole contrast depends on Keats putting the rainbow with the gnomes.
2. Robertson's note ("Not to be taken as a serious expression of Keats's view of life") is used in the Lamia cpara and the Response. It is information, and the Response says it is one editor's reading. Consider cutting it from the Response if it reads as piling on.
3. The anti-reductionists note (SEP). The post's previous day defines reductionism as disbelief in a multi-level territory; under that definition, non-reductive physicalists may count as reductionists. The note says only that the word covers several positions and that the post does not say which it means. The other agent annotating "Reductionism" (202) may make a related point; STANDARDS 2.5 says to make it in full once (202 comes first in book order). Please reconcile.
4. The reader-test clogic ("cannot tell a reader who commits the fallacy from one who reads ... shorthand"). The post hedges with "would seem to argue". I think the note is fair; it could be cut as a nitpick.
5. The scope sentence in the reply cpara ("For a belief reached by argument ... the post does not trace the chain"). One sentence, stated as scope, with credit first.
6. Reserved words: "never" appears only in paraphrases of the post ("there never were any", "they never existed"); "wrong" in the Response ("where the objection goes wrong") is credit to the post.
