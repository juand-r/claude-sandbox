# Report: categorizing-has-consequences

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Blood types are real, medically important categories discovered by Landsteiner, yet in
Japan they carry personality stereotypes like astrological signs; since blood types were
never mysterious and never the lines of a conflict, the author argues the stereotypes "must
have arisen strictly from the mere existence of the labels". The author predicts, without
knowing of an experiment, that labeling non-human things would have the same effect, and
explains the effect four ways (similarity clusters, neural subnetworks, compression, the
ability to make things up about any named category). So drawing a category boundary is never
a neutral act for a human mind, which is one more reason not to believe you can define a word
any way you like.

## 6. Judgment calls

- The main note (fourth paragraph): "stronger than the history allows". A defender could say
  "strictly from the mere existence of the labels" means only that no real differences or
  conflicts fed the stereotypes, and that the post's fourth explanation (people make things
  up about named categories) already covers Furukawa and Nomi. I say exactly that in the note
  ("the post's fourth explanation below") and restrict the charge to the use of the case as
  evidence for labels alone. The source is Wikipedia, cited as such.
- Tajfel and Wilkes: presented first as support (the experiment existed and fits), then as a
  limit on "mere labeling". The limit rests on one abstract sentence and one replication
  abstract. The post's own parenthesis worries about protocols that make labels seem
  significant; the finding is a different point (labels unrelated to length had no effect),
  so I did not tie them together.
- The cfact on "Thus people learn their blood types before they marry": borderline nitpick.
  Kept because it is a stated factual consequence ("Thus") with no source, and the reader may
  take it as fact. I did not add a note on "allergic reaction" / "allergic suppressant"
  (the mechanism is immune, antibody-based, per MedlinePlus); that is terminology with no
  effect on the argument.
- "never" appears twice in the Summary, each paraphrasing the post ("never been mysterious",
  "There's never been a war ... never even been a political conflict").
