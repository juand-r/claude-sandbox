# Report: don-t-believe-you-ll-self-deceive

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Commenters such as Kurige say they practise "double-think" or chose to believe in God while
knowing the choice does not affect whether God exists, which puzzles the author, since
knowing one's map is not correlated with the territory should end the belief. The author
now grants that it may be quite easy not to make that jump, and that the earlier statement
"You cannot make yourself believe the sky is green by an act of will" was partly an attempt
to instill a self-fulfilling prophecy. So the author recommends repeating to oneself that
deliberate self-deception cannot work: believing in your own inability makes you less likely
to fool yourself.

## 6. Judgment calls

- "Kurige ... used the word more loosely": Kurige did write "Orwellian", so a defender could
  say the post read the word as Kurige offered it. The note now quotes Orwell's definition
  and Kurige's "not contradictory" side by side and says only "more loosely".
- The "zero effect" note is a logical point from the author's own account of evidence; the
  Response says the post's question "rests on the word 'chose' alone", which I think is fair:
  "chose ... deliberately" could mean without evidence, and the note grants that the reading
  rests on it.
- "close to what Doublethink argued against": I avoided "contradicts". The post's own defence
  (a self-fulfilling belief) is stated in the same note and in the Response.
- The link slip (Moore's Paradox for Doublethink) is mentioned once, neutrally, in a cpara,
  because a reader following the link would not find the sentence; the editor may cut it
  under 2.4(2).
