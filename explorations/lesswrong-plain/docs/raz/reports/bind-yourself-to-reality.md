# Report: bind-yourself-to-reality

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The posts on joy in the real belong in a sequence on reductionism because taking things
apart can feel like removing magic from the world, and the author wants to leave readers a
line of retreat and to oppose the "Hollywood Rationality" view that understanding the
rainbow subtracts its beauty. The deeper theme is "binding yourself to reality": emotional
energy spent on the impossible (the lottery, flying potions, magic rainbows) should be
redirected, not destroyed, into real and actionable things (a job, an airplane, the physics
of the rainbow), as the lottery post and "Feeling Rational" already suggested. Emotions
that follow from one's best picture of the truth are not irrational, so rationality does
not remove feeling; it points feeling at the lawful universe, and "Science puts the fun
back into life."

## 6. Judgment calls

- The Dawkins note (paragraph 3, Response, honest n.b.) says "The post does not mention the
  book." This is credit to prior work, not a charge of plagiarism; the post does not claim
  novelty and the Response says so. "Joy in the Merely Real" (order 206, annotated by another
  agent in this batch) is the first post in book order to take up Keats's rainbow; if its notes
  already make the Dawkins point in full, mine should become a pointer (STANDARDS 2.5). I did
  not see its notes (none existed when I checked).
- Central charge: "emotional energy" undefined and its transfer between objects unshown
  (paragraph 8 note, Response, honest). I judged this a stated assumption the advice depends
  on, not a speculative alternative. The airplane note ("the easiest case") is a separate point:
  the one example where transfer is not needed.
- Reserved word "never" appears only in describing the Truthsayer ("never lying").
