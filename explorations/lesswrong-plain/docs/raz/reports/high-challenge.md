# Report: high-challenge

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The post takes the prophecy that machines will do all the work, adds computer games to
keep people busy, and follows the logic of making games easier until nothing is left but a
screen saying "YOU WIN", which it compares to the Christian Heaven as a picture of eternal
laziness that only "sounds like good news". It proposes instead that a long-run meaning of
life should consist of activities good to pursue and not only good to finish ("valuations
over 4D states"), grants that some goals like curing cancer are worth only their result,
and rejects passive experiencing (the "Buddhist Heaven") on the ground that the author does
not want to become "a happy blob", which the author counts as a justification. As in the
earlier post on happiness, the author holds that what is valued can include external reality
and not only experience: a race is worth running only if the other racers are real, the
effort is one's own, and a sentient being experiences it.

## 6. Judgment calls

- The one critical point is the clogic on the racing paragraph (and the Response's last
  paragraph, and the last honest n.b.): the post's closing conditions are met by a game against
  real opponents, so its rhetorical "Isn't there a wasted step here?" is broader than those
  conditions. It uses "rigged" in its literal sense (a rigged contest), not as a charge
  against the post. The claim that a race on a track is a game is mine, stated plainly; a
  defender might say the post only doubts computer games as a substitute for work. I think
  the note survives that, since the post names "artificiality and isolation" as the costs,
  which are features of some games, not of games as such. The editor may prefer to cut it.
- The "we" note (P26 cpara, Response, honest n.b.): the caller asked that preferences be
  judged as preferences and generalizations noted. The note says the want justifies the "we
  should" only for those who share it, and points to supportive survey evidence.
- Many notes are credit (Keynes, Suits, flow research, Nozick). The Response presents them
  as support, per the Book V lessons ("prior work that supports the post is credit").
- "Well-known" was removed from the Keynes note; the Response still says "well-known
  precedents" for Keynes, Suits and Nozick together.
- "Reserved" flags: "never" is a paraphrase of the post's prophecy; "rigged" as above.
- Pronouns: Keynes, Nozick and the charwoman ("her heaven", Keynes's words) keep theirs.
