# Report: if-you-demand-magic-magic-won-t-help

## 1. The argument in three sentences

If there were dragons in our world and no zebras, fantasy would be about zebras; people
who wish for magic, most of whom did not become scientists in a world of science, would
lose interest in magic once it was real and had to be studied, like lottery winners whose
excitement wears off. The same holds for the Future (which will be another Now), for a
commenter excited by Sheldrake's theory rather than by Special Relativity, and for New
Agers, whose beliefs would lose their point if their rituals worked. So the capacity to
enjoy the merely real is what matters, "It's not where you are, it's who you are," and
whatever one hopes to do anywhere one may as well do in reality.

## 6. Judgment calls

- Changed during drafting: I first wrote a note that the post's central question treats
  becoming a scientist and becoming a "(superpowerful)" sorcerer as the same choice. I cut it as
  my own speculative disanalogy (the author could answer that science is power, as "Mundane
  Magic" argues), and recast the point as scope: the post states its claim about readers as a
  condition and does not say how many meet it.
- Motive notes: the paragraph on Matthew C. ("A claim about a named person's motives, stated
  as fact, with no evidence beyond the commenter's interest") and the New Age paragraph. These
  describe the post's own motive attributions; they are not motive readings of the author.
  The "In short" line rests on them.
- The protein-folding note uses "Loosely put" and "it seems" (my reading of what the author
  meant by "non-explanation-demanding"). I judged it relevant because the looseness bears on the
  motive claim that follows; the editor may consider it a wording nitpick.
- Lottery note: support and limits in one note (fits the point; no six-month figure; larger
  study found lasting life-satisfaction gains). Kept as information, not a charge; it is not in
  the "In short" line.
- Pronouns: the post calls Matthew C. "he"; our text uses "the commenter".
