# Report: the-tragedy-of-group-selectionism

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Before 1966 some biologists held that animals restrain their breeding to spare their food
supply, and explained it by selection among isolated subpopulations; the idea is possible
but needs conditions (small groups, low cost, strong benefit) that simulations suggest are
rarely met, and observation went against it. When Wade later imposed group selection for
small populations on flour beetles in the laboratory, the beetles evolved cannibalism, not
restraint. The author concludes that the group selectionists started from an aesthetic
wish for harmony and rationalized a mechanism for it, without neutrally asking what that
mechanism would really produce, and that evolutionary biology is good training against
this error.
