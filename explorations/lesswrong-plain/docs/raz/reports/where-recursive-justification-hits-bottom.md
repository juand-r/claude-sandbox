# Report: where-recursive-justification-hits-bottom ("Where Recursive Justification Hits Bottom", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-07-08; book order 270)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Every justification of induction or Occam's razor seems to rest on induction or Occam's
razor, which raises the regress problem (how does the chain of justifications end, and if it
may end in an unjustified assumption, why not assume anything?); Bayesian updating does not
solve it, since the answer depends on the prior, and there are possible minds with
anti-Occamian and anti-Laplacian priors that defend them by their own lights. The author's
answer is to treat "Should I trust my brain / Occam's razor?" as ordinary questions examined
with one's current mind, since there is nothing else to use: this makes the chain of
justification loop rather than stop, and the author holds such reflective loops, which
include a causal story (including evolution) of why one's mind should track the world, to
be different from circular logic like a paper that says "everything on this paper is true",
while a self-certifying source like the Bible fails because its record on other matters
does not hold up. The aim is not to persuade an ideal philosopher of perfect emptiness but
to win: question everything, including one's rules of reasoning, with the full force of the
current mind, and change a principle when it fails that examination.
