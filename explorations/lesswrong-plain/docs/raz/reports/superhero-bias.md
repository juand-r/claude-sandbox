# Report: superhero-bias ("Superhero Bias", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-12-01)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Because of the halo effect, a strong, invulnerable superhero seems more courageous than
an ordinary police officer who takes a real risk, and fame likewise seems to add to
perceived virtue, as when Gandhi, protected in part by celebrity, is remembered over
anonymous marchers who risked more. The author separates two senses of "greater": saving
two hundred beats saving three as an outcome, but risking your life for three reveals
more courage, and deliberately choosing the riskier or smaller rescue to reveal virtue is
itself a moral failure. Real virtue seeks to save more with less risk, so any heroism that
remains after that is truly revealed, and the unpowered officer shows more virtue than
Superman.

## 6. Judgment calls

1. The Gandhi cfact. A fair defender could say the sentence concerns the marches, where police
   violence fell on the marchers, and the assassination attempts were not during marches. The
   note grants the Dharasana case, quotes the post's own later softening, and objects only that
   the post counts the protection fame gave and not the risk it brought. The mob in 1897 and
   the attempts are presented as risks of prominence; that link is my reading of the events,
   stated as "his prominence also made him a target".
2. The clogic "Inferred from the halo effect, with no study ..." The post may intend this as an
   illustration, not an empirical claim; but it names a bias ("superhero bias") on it, so I
   kept the note, worded as "no study", not as a fault of reasoning.
3. The Mishnah note is context, not criticism: the post itself calls the saying "a fine
   applause light" and objects to it only as advice for choosing.
4. Credit: the Response calls the second half "careful moral reasoning" and says the
   revealed-virtue comparison "holds as stated". I checked it: the comparison is between a
   person whose threshold is three and a person whose threshold is two hundred.
5. Reserved word "false" appears only in "false testimony" (describing the Mishnah), not as a
   verdict.
