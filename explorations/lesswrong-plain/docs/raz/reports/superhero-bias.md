# Report: superhero-bias ("Superhero Bias", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-12-01)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Because of the halo effect, a strong, invulnerable superhero seems more courageous than
an ordinary police officer who takes a real risk, and fame likewise seems to add to
perceived virtue, as when Gandhi, protected in part by celebrity, is remembered over
anonymous marchers who risked more. The author separates two senses of "greater": saving
two hundred beats saving three as an outcome, but risking your life for three reveals
more courage, and deliberately choosing the riskier or smaller rescue to reveal virtue is
itself a moral failure. Real virtue seeks to save more with less risk, so any heroism that
remains after that is truly revealed, and the unpowered officer shows more virtue than
Superman.
