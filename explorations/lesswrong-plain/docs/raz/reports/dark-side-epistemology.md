# Report: dark-side-epistemology ("Dark Side Epistemology", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-10-17)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

A lie is out of place in a lawful, interconnected world, so protecting it requires lying about connected facts, then about the general laws that link them, then about the rules of reasoning themselves (creationists lie about evolution and then about what "theory" means); the same happens inside one mind when a self-deceived believer must reject, in turn, the rule that beliefs need evidence and the reasons for that rule. So wherever lies are protected, bad general epistemology ("the Dark Side") comes into being, and some of the "how to think" proverbs we meet, perhaps "Everyone has a right to their own opinion," were made to protect a lie. Most who repeat them are duped rather than deceivers, and the idea must not be used as an all-purpose way to dismiss disliked ideas; ideas must still be refuted on their merits.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- Note on "which is the path taken by most creationists": argues that "lie" here has its ordinary sense because the post turns to self-deception only afterwards, then says the post does not reconcile "most creationists" lying with "more duped than duplicitous". A fair defender could say the post's "lie" covers self-deception throughout ("Whether you're lying to others, or to yourself"). I think the order of the post supports my reading, but this is the note I am least sure of.
- Quine note: says the logical point is Quine's and the post "does not mention Quine". It does not say the post claims novelty. The post does write "it's not widely realized that the Dark Side is out there", which is about the phenomenon, not the logic.
- Proverb notes: the cpara gives the UDHR sense and grants "The post may mean the proverb's use in argument"; the clogic says the treatment "sits uneasily" with the post's own rule (not "contradicts"). The In short line says "in tension with its own rule".
- Protein note: says the protein item "is not explained" and gives Wikipedia on shape changes. It does not claim weak bonds are evidence for design, only that the post gives no reason why they count against it.
- "A single Lie That Must Be Protected can block ...": the note grants it is a claim of possibility ("can") and says no example is given.
- Pronouns: "Quine" used instead of "him"; Kaas has no pronoun.
