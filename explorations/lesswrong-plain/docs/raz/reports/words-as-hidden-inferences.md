# Report: words-as-hidden-inferences

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Guessing that the next egg-shaped object from a barrel is blue is an explicit inference from
a small sample, but recognizing a tiger is the same kind of inference made instantly and
without tracking its assumptions; naming the objects "bleggs" and "rubes" makes it
automatic. So you cannot define a word any way you like: the brain categorizes and infers
whatever your stated definitions say, as the Aristotelians went on recognizing humans by
their looks while claiming that mortality was established by definition. A wrong picture of
your own thinking does not stop quick perception, but it can spoil the deliberate thinking
you use to correct it, for example by making you careless in choosing definitions.
