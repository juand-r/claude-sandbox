# Report: doublethink-choosing-to-be-biased

## 1. The argument in three sentences

To choose a bias rationally ("second-order rationality") you would need an accurate model
of the consequences first, and then you would have to forget it and forget the forgetting,
as in Orwell's doublethink; since we cannot make ourselves believe things by an act of will,
the only real alternative is to stay biased blindly, which is willful stupidity. The driving
example shows the cost: an optimistic bias can feel fine for years and then kill you, and
you cannot weigh that risk without knowing your real skill, while unknown unknowns do not
come to mind. For readers who already know about biases the "happiness of stupidity" is
closed anyway, so they should aim for the happiness a rationalist can have, which the author
thinks may prove greater.
