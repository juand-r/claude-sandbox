# Report: science-as-curiosity-stopper

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Answering "Science!" or "Electricity!" to "how does that work?" gives the listener no
knowledge (no predictions, no way to build one), yet it stops curiosity, because being told
that someone else knows makes the phenomenon feel understood. For your own curiosity, as
distinct from greed or the social use of technology, it should not matter whether someone
else knows; otherwise "somebody already knows" would let you walk past a floating green
elephant. You do not know how your cerebellum, knees or monitor work, so the world is still
full of puzzles for you, and you should not complain that science has emptied it of mystery.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. The main criticism (cpara on "But what does the phrase 'scientifically explicable'
   mean?"): being told something is scientifically explicable does carry information, that
   it breaks no known law and that an answer exists. This is my own argument, not a sourced
   alternative explanation. I think it is a logical point about the sentence "you don't know
   more than you knew earlier" rather than a speculative psychological hypothesis (THIRD_PASS
   rule 5), and I restricted it accordingly: I did not claim that this is why people's
   curiosity drops, only that it is a reason that is neither greed nor spite and that the
   post does not consider. The editor should decide whether it passes 2.4(5).
2. The same point is referred to three times in the notes (definition paragraph in full;
   elephant and "double standard" as brief pointers). I judged the pointers necessary
   because each example is where a reader would raise it; they do not restate the argument.
   Cut them if they count as repetition.
3. "Is this not spite?": I describe it as a charge made as a question with no evidence. It is
   the post's own motive reading of its readers; my note does not read the author's motives.
4. The Joy in the Merely Real clogic is an observation about scope, not a fault. Consider
   cutting if it reads as faulting the post for not being a later post.
5. Reserved words: none remaining. I changed "is wrong" to "is mistaken" in the "In short"
   line.
6. I considered and dropped a note citing "Your Strength as a Rationalist" ("more confused by
   fiction than by reality") as the author's own reason for the "double standard": that
   sentence is about fictional stories, and applying it here risked fair-defender 7.
