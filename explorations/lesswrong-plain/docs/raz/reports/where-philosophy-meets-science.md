Agent batch 4c. Scratch folder: `data/sources/4c_where-philosophy-meets-science/`.

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

The most basic error of early quantum physics was that its founders forgot they were
themselves made of particles, so they did not see that a detector (or their own knowledge)
is just more "particles in different places", and they brought consciousness into a theory
that could not be written as a formal program. The insight they needed looks
"philosophical", yet it was physicists, not professional philosophers, who cleared up the
mystery, and in general science, not the consensus of philosophers, tells us which
philosopher was right. So "philosophy" is the name for thinking not yet taught or reduced to
calculation, and it is done well only from within a science by people closely involved with
it; professional philosophers should learn the science and expect experiment to settle their
arguments.

## 6. Judgment calls

- The von Neumann note ("The diagnosis does not fit him"). Fair-defender test: the post hedges "I'm sure that most of them knew it in theory" and then says "they didn't notice that putting a sensor ... was an example of 'particles in different places.'" Von Neumann did treat the instrument as a quantum system (SEP), so the claim that they did not notice does not fit him. I did not write "wrong": the post names nobody, and some founders may fit.
- "Solved goes further than the author went a month later" (clogic). The post's "the problem" is the consciousness/collapse confusion; the Born rule is arguably a separate question, and a defender could say so. I kept the note because the post says "cleared up the mystery and made everything normal again", which is broader. The editor may prefer to soften to "sits uneasily with".
- The note on "usually science" uses "sits uneasily with", not "contradicts": the post hedges with "usually" and does not say experiment settled quantum interpretation.
- The "within a science" note applies the post's rule to the sequence. It gives both readings of "within" so as not to charge the author on the stricter one. It could be read as a jab; I think it is the post's own test applied to itself (README 5.2). Editor to judge.
- The philosophers-of-physics sentence is credit/information, not a charge: the post's claim about who found the answer is granted ("the origin fits").
