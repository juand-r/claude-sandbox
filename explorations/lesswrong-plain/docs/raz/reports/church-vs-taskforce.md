# Report: church-vs-taskforce ("Church vs. Taskforce", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-03-28)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

If religion disappeared, the author guesses, the largest gap it would leave is not in ideals or morals but in community, and many people who stay religious without quite believing may stay for their neighbours. Churches and friendly workplaces supply community only as a side effect, so a community built explicitly for community would be designed differently: time-shared buildings, welcome for newcomers, practices tested by experiment. The author proposes that rationalist communities form as local "taskforces" around shared work on the world's problems (or other shared goals such as learning), while granting that this is "probably" a dream.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- The Pew note (cfact on the insurance paragraph) and the Response set "nearly all of what people get out of it" against self-reported reasons. The sentence is inside "to the extent that churches provide community", which a fair defender could read as governing the whole sentence. I kept the point because "nearly all" is asserted within that clause, and the note says stated reasons need not measure benefits and that Lim and Putnam point the other way. It reaches the In short line as "though not in the strong form of 'nearly all'". If the editor judges this a slip whose main point survives, move it out of the In short line.
- The Sacred Places note is information that the time-shared building partly exists; it does not claim the post is wrong. Source is an advocacy organization.
- The Lim and Putnam "contingent on a strong religious identity" note: offered as bearing on the taskforce turn, ending "the study does not test" it. Check it does not read as an objection.
- Considered and dropped: a note that the post never lists what a hunter-gatherer band gives its people (the post admits it does not understand this well; scope); a note that "empirically the case" about colleges is unsupported (hedged by "seem"; nitpick); a note on "energy that was once wasted into the void of religious institutions" versus the community functions the post credits churches with (the phrase can be read as about religion proper); church-matchmaking statistics (the bullet is hedged "probably" and no clear standard for "nearly enough").
