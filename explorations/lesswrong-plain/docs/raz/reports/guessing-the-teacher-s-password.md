# Report: guessing-the-teacher-s-password

## 1. The argument in three sentences

As a child the author "knew" that light, sound and matter were waves without knowing what a
physicist means by "wave"; school rewards saying the right words, so students come to treat
a verbal answer ("Waves!", "heat conduction") as true or false in itself. Such an answer is
not a belief about the world but an attempt to guess the teacher's password; only using a
model (putting numbers into the diffusion equation, or even picturing heat flowing and
testing it) connects words to anticipated experience. Students should be drilled that words
do not count, only anticipation-controllers, and shown how a phrase can lead to questions
and tests; otherwise they get stuck on passwords, as the author did at nine and humanity did
for thousands of years.

  It says the post's view of meaning is not defended and that a standard view disagrees; it
  does not say the post is wrong. The defender's reply is "I only said the syllables are not
  right or wrong 'of itself'"; the note is aimed at the post's calling it a "bad habit" to
  think the student "has made a true statement", which is the post's own wording.

  the author "waves". The editor may judge it tangential and cut it (STANDARDS 2.4(2)); I kept
