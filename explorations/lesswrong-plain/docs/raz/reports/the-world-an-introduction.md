# Report: the-world-an-introduction

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Earlier books compared humans as goal-seekers with inhuman purposeful processes; Book IV,
*Mere Reality*, compares humans as physical systems with processes that are not mind-like,
and the introduction previews its seven sequences. It gives background on two debates the
book enters: the hard problem of consciousness (Nagel's bat, zombies, Chalmers's "further
fact"), which "Physicalism 201" will test against a technical account of explanation and
evidence, and the measurement problem in quantum mechanics (Schrödinger versus Born, Bell's
"measurer", the Copenhagen strands). Since neither Yudkowsky nor Bensinger is a physicist,
it points readers to outside sources (Tegmark, Albert, the Stanford Encyclopedia, Epstein)
for checking the many-worlds arguments, noting that many-worlds is popular but still
rejected by a number of physicists.

## 2. Sources checked

Fetched files are in `data/sources/4a_the-world-an-introduction/` (shared by my four slugs).

- SEP "Zombies", https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/zombies/ (`sep_zombies.txt`). Matched:
  "they are exactly like us in all physical respects but without conscious experiences";
  "If zombies are to be counterexamples to physicalism, it is not enough for them to be
  behaviorally and functionally like normal human beings: plenty of physicalists accept
  that merely behavioral or functional duplicates of ourselves might lack qualia.";
  "The most systematic use of the zombie idea against physicalism is by David Chalmers
  1996"; "Zombies were put forward as a counterexample to physicalism in general, and
  arguments devised to back up the intuition that they are possible (Kirk 1974a, 1974b)";
  "some argue that nevertheless zombies are not really conceivable at all (Kirk 2005, 2008,
  2013; Tye 2006)".
- Nagel, "What Is It Like to Be a Bat?", full text at
  https://www.philosopher.eu/others-writings/nagel-what-is-it-like-to-be-a-bat/
  (`nagel_bat.txt`; a transcription, not the journal PDF). Matched: "But when we examine
  their subjective character it seems that such a result is impossible."; "It would be a
  mistake to conclude that physicalism must be false."; "It would be truer to say that
  physicalism is a position we cannot understand because we do not at present have any
  conception of how it might be true." Also, not used in a note but relevant: Nagel's
  proposed "objective phenomenology ... Though presumably it would not capture everything".
  That is why I did not fault the introduction's "can never fully capture": Nagel says
  something close, with hedges. The note targets only the step to "ditch physicalism".
- PhilPapers 2009 survey, https://philpapers.org/surveys/results.pl (`pp2009_results.html`).

- Bell, "Against 'measurement'", Physics World, https://physicsworld.com/a/against-measurement-john-bell-on-our-continuing-struggles-with-quantum-mechanics/
  (`bell_physicsworld.txt`). Matched: "It would seem that the theory is exclusively
  concerned about ‘results of measurement’, and has nothing to say about anything else.

  "measurement" vs "measurements"; "the role of ‘measurer’" vs "the role of the

## 6. Judgment calls

- The Nagel cfact: says Nagel "declined that conclusion" (the introduction's question is
  whether to "ditch physicalism"). The introduction does not say Nagel concluded against
  physicalism; it groups Nagel with Chalmers as arguing that models "can never fully
  capture" consciousness, which Nagel roughly does, with hedges. I considered this an
  attribution worth one note and one Response sentence; the editor may think it a nitpick
  for an introduction.
- The zombie definition note: the introduction states the stronger (physical) version two
  paragraphs later, which the note says. Possibly a nitpick; kept because Physicalism 201
  depends on the physical-duplicate version.
- The polls note: the introduction's statement is hedged and reasonably accurate. The note
  gives numbers, not a charge. The Response says "In each it was held by a minority" without
  calling the introduction's wording wrong.
- The Copenhagen and Mermin notes are information, not faults: SEP itself says the
  consciousness view is "usually associated" with Copenhagen.
- Reserved-word flag: "never" appears only in a cpara restating the introduction's own
  "can never fully capture".
- Layout: two overfull boxes (42pt, 147pt) from bare URLs in the post's own footnotes 3 and
  8; the known md2tex issue in review_log.
