# Report: the-world-an-introduction

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Earlier books compared humans as goal-seekers with inhuman purposeful processes; Book IV,
*Mere Reality*, compares humans as physical systems with processes that are not mind-like,
and the introduction previews its seven sequences. It gives background on two debates the
book enters: the hard problem of consciousness (Nagel's bat, zombies, Chalmers's "further
fact"), which "Physicalism 201" will test against a technical account of explanation and
evidence, and the measurement problem in quantum mechanics (Schrödinger versus Born, Bell's
"measurer", the Copenhagen strands). Since neither Yudkowsky nor Bensinger is a physicist,
it points readers to outside sources (Tegmark, Albert, the Stanford Encyclopedia, Epstein)
for checking the many-worlds arguments, noting that many-worlds is popular but still
rejected by a number of physicists.
