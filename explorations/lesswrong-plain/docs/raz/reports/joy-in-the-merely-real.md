# Report: joy-in-the-merely-real

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Keats's "dull catalogue of common things" holds whatever is known, knowable, lawful: in a word, real. Since mystery is a fact about our ignorance, not about the world, everything that exists is liable to end up in that catalogue sooner or later. So one must either decide that real, explicable things are worth caring about, or suffer an unresolvable ennui; scientists, fascinated by ordinary things, are those capable in principle of enjoying the real universe.
