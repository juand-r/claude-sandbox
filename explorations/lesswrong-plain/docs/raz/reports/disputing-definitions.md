## 1. The argument in three sentences

In the falling-tree riddle, two people who agree on every event in the forest (vibrations
occur, no one hears them) still argue, and the argument drifts from the forest to "by
definition", to the dictionary, to "common usage" and to insult. The post stages this
"Standard Dispute" to show that it produces no feeling of agreement even though there is
nothing factual left to disagree about. The remedy is to describe the event in unambiguous
lower-level terms, or to coin two new words ("alberzle", "bargulum"), while keeping track of
a testable proposition; this settles "a substantial fraction" of such disputes, not all.
