# Report: no-really-i-ve-deceived-myself

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The author recounts a conversation with an intelligent, nominally Orthodox Jewish woman who
knew the evidence against her religion and said she had come to believe in God after
deciding, as a teenage atheist, to act as if she did. From her phrasing ("I believe in
God", never "There is a God"), her talk only of the benefits of belief, and her agreement
that a truth-seeker would not invent God as a hypothesis, the author concludes that she does
not believe in God but believes she has deceived herself into it. Believing that one has
deceived oneself may yield, the author supposes, much the same placebo benefit as real
belief, which would explain why she defended "I believe" and showed no interest in whether
God exists.

  say the sentence reports what she knew. But "She *knows* this" is the author's endorsement,
  and "corresponding old Persian legend" describes the conjecture as an attested legend. The
  note is on background, not on the argument; the editor may judge it a nitpick under 2.4(2).

  sentence denies only a conjunction.

  hypothesis. The note is limited to the phrasing, and the cpara credits the agreement as the
  strongest evidence.
- Pronouns: the woman is "she" in the post; kept.
