# Report: no-universally-compelling-arguments ("No Universally Compelling Arguments", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-06-26; book order 272)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Since mind design space is vast, for any argument there is some possible mind it does not
persuade; the hope for arguments that would persuade any mind comes from picturing a "ghost in
the machine", an irreducible core of reasonableness above the code, whereas physically, for any
system that assents to an argument one can build a similar system that does not, so compulsion
is a property of minds, not of arguments. The same holds for the charge that Bayesian priors are
"arbitrary": the ideal philosopher of perfect emptiness who would be persuaded from no
assumptions is the ghost again, and a mind with all assumptions removed is a rock, so validity,
rationality or objectivity cannot rest on universal compellingness. The worst error in thinking
about Friendly AI is to expect any AI to reach the one true morality on its own (a lesser version
hopes a "perfectly free" AI will arrive at virtue), with John C. Wright as the example.

## 3. Arithmetic

- Laplace's rule of succession after 4 red and 1 white: (4+1)/(5+2) = 5/7. Correct.
- 2/7 from "another mind": the post does not specify the prior. One prior that gives it: a barrel
  known to have held 6 red and 6 white, sampled without replacement; after 4 red and 1 white,
  7 balls remain, 2 red: 2/7. (The linked "Priors as Mathematical Objects", fetched to
  `data/originals/priors-as-mathematical-objects.md`, uses a 5-and-5 urn, which would give
  (5-4)/(10-5) = 1/5; the post does not claim to reuse that urn.)
- "two to the trillionth chances": minds specifiable in at most 10^12 bits number about
  2^(10^12+1); the post's figure is a round statement of that count. No note depends on it.

## 6. Judgment calls

1. The Kant clogic (on the conclusion paragraph) and the Response's main criticism: the post's
   argument is descriptive (some mind is not moved), while Kant's claim is normative. I first
   wrote "the main traditions"; I cut that to Kant alone, since I checked only Kant. The post
   itself says validity "cannot rely on" compulsion and promises to return to the theme, so the
   notes say the post "leaves [the normative question] for later", not that it fails.
2. The Bayesian-priors clogic calls the SEP account "the standard form of this worry". The post
   names no philosopher; I compare its paraphrase with SEP's description, and note that the post
   itself raises the question of reasonable priors ("perhaps, a less "reasonable" one").
3. The motive cpara ("The post assigns a motive to those who hold it"): the post's words are
   "they dream of commands that no sufficiently advanced mind can disobey" and "the dream of
   absolute command". Stated as what the post does; no reading of the author's motives.
4. The Wright cpara: "the post does not say how it bears on the argument in the novel". The
   closing line may be a joke; I describe it rather than calling it an ad hominem.
5. The orthogonality credit (Bostrom 2012) may overlap with notes on The Design Space of
   Minds-In-General or Ghosts in the Machine by other agents; if an earlier post carries it in
   full, mine should become a pointer. The CAIM notes point back here for Bostrom.
6. "Reserved" flag: "false" is the post's own word ("chances to be false"). Pronouns: he/his for
   Kant (historical) and for Wright as the post itself uses "his".
