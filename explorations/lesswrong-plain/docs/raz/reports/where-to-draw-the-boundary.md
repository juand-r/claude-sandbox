# Report: where-to-draw-the-boundary ("Where to Draw the Boundary?", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-02-21)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

A word such as "art" has no meaning floating in the void to be discovered by finding the right
definition; the real task is to find which things cluster together or share a common cause,
that is, to carve reality at its joints ("electromugnetism", which lumps lightning, compasses and
animal magnetism and leaves out light, is a wrong word). Given a list of things called art and
not art, one can propose an intension (the author's: deliberate effort meant to produce
aesthetic emotion), and others can dispute either the intension or the list itself, on the
presumption that some underlying pattern generates the list. Both intensional and extensional
definitions can therefore be wrong, as a "fish" list that includes dolphins is wrong, and one
should admit that one's definition-guesses can be mistaken.

## 6. Judgment calls

1. cfact "The quotation is reworded" (not "misquotes"). The difference changes the meaning: the
   added clause is the attitude the post then criticizes. A fair defender could say "The one
   comes to you and says" is a stylized, parable-like frame that signals a retelling (test 3).
   The post uses a block quotation and links the comment, so a reader will take the words as the
   commenter's; I kept the note, stated only as fact.
2. clogic "same kind of answer as the commenter's". The post's contrast is between looking for a
   word's meaning and describing a cluster. My claim is that, apart from the added clause, the
   comment is a cluster description of the same form as the author's. The note says "It gives no
   reason ... apart from the opening words it added", not that the post contradicts itself.
3. cpara on the extension dispute (art has no settling finding). Scope, not error: the post calls
   the underlying curve a "presumption". A fair defender might cite the fMRI remark as a possible
   test; the post says that remark only emphasizes "that emotions are not ethereal", so I did not
   count it as a test.
4. Reserved word "wrong" appears many times, always in the post's own sense ("wrong word",
   definitions "can be wrong") or describing the post's argument; never as my charge.
5. Credits: the 1784 commission and Linnaeus support the post's two scientific cases; given as
   information.
