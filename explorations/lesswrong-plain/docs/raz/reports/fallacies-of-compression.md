# Report: fallacies-of-compression

## 1. The argument in three sentences

A map must be smaller than the territory, and a brain is small, so things that are distinct
in reality get compressed into one point on the mental map; from the inside this feels not
like compression but like seeing one thing. The dangerous case is not two known things under
one label but having no idea that two things exist (sound as vibration and as experience; a
suspect who is really two twins), and seeing the split is a scientific achievement, often
marked by coining new words. "Consciousness" is the modern example (wakefulness, reflectivity
and subjective experience under one name), and the confusion drives bait-and-switch arguments,
so prying a concept apart, helped by the rationalist version of Taboo, is part of the skill
of inquiry.
