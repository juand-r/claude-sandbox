# Report: heat-vs-motion ("Heat vs. Motion", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-04-01; book order 220)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

The reduction of heat to motion is a simpler example of reductionism crossing an apparent
difference in kind: someone who, like Carnot in 1824, knows a great deal about heat and about
mechanics as separate subjects will feel that heat and motion are independent properties, and
may read "where there is heat, there is motion" either as a claim that the two words mean the
same (Thinker 1) or as a contingent bridging law between two things (Thinker 2). Thinker 1
confuses quotation and referent: different words or beliefs can have the same referent, which
is found by looking at the world, not inside one's head, like the morning and evening star;
Thinker 2 mistakes an inferential rule for a causal law and then conflates conceptual
possibility with logical possibility, which Putnam's water/H2O passage also discusses (though
the author thinks it uses "water" in two senses). Only detailed knowledge of how the reduction
works makes a world where heat and motion come apart inconceivable, so "reductionism is easy,
reduction is hard", though being a reductionist helps in looking for a reduction.
