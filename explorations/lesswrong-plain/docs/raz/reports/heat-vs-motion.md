# Report: heat-vs-motion ("Heat vs. Motion", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-04-01; book order 220)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

The reduction of heat to motion is a simpler example of reductionism crossing an apparent
difference in kind: someone who, like Carnot in 1824, knows a great deal about heat and about
mechanics as separate subjects will feel that heat and motion are independent properties, and
may read "where there is heat, there is motion" either as a claim that the two words mean the
same (Thinker 1) or as a contingent bridging law between two things (Thinker 2). Thinker 1
confuses quotation and referent: different words or beliefs can have the same referent, which
is found by looking at the world, not inside one's head, like the morning and evening star;
Thinker 2 mistakes an inferential rule for a causal law and then conflates conceptual
possibility with logical possibility, which Putnam's water/H2O passage also discusses (though
the author thinks it uses "water" in two senses). Only detailed knowledge of how the reduction
works makes a world where heat and motion come apart inconceivable, so "reductionism is easy,
reduction is hard", though being a reductionist helps in looking for a reduction.

## 6. Judgment calls

1. The Putnam note and Response paragraph are the main criticism: the post's reading ("seems to
   be saying ... water becomes automatically redefined to mean H20") is at odds with Putnam's
   paper, which says the meaning did not change. The hedge is quoted in the note and the
   Response. I use "at odds with" and "rejects", not "contradicts". The In short line names it.
   The post's "different position" is presented as one option ("you could coherently hold ..."),
   so I call it "the alternative the post mentions" and "close to" Putnam's account, not "the
   post's proposal".
2. The Kripke note: "does not mention Kripke" is a credit point. The post says "I believe the
   above has already been said as well?", so the Response frames all the prior work as showing
   the argument is standard, not as a charge of taking credit.
3. The heat/kinetic energy note ("Loosely stated"): a slip whose point survives; kept out of the
   In short line. A defender could say the post, like Kripke, uses the everyday identity.
4. The first-reading note (caloric theorists held caloric conserved): information, since the post
   offers the bridging law as a possible interpretation, not as history.
5. The Descartes parallel for Thinker 2 is credit via Chalmers's list of predecessors; the post
   does not mention the mind here, and I do not import the zombie dispute.
6. Honest section is 564 words, above 500, for a 1569-word post.
