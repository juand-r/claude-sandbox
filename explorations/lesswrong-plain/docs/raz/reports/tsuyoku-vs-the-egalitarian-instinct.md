# Report: tsuyoku-vs-the-egalitarian-instinct

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Hunter-gatherer bands are egalitarian, and their successful hunters downplay their kills to
avoid envy; but anyone who aims to do their best will eventually aim above the average, and
if you are ashamed of doing better than others the median becomes a wall. Pride in beating
others may even be a useful motivator, and in any case life is not graded on a curve, so run
as fast as you can without worrying about pulling ahead. You may find it wise to downplay
your successes in public, but privately keep saying "stronger, stronger" and set a higher
target, without shame.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. The envy/pride clogic. The post says the hunter downplays the kill "to avoid envy"; Lee's
   informants give pride as the reason and describe others enforcing the modesty. The note
   says "In Lee's account", one group only. Could be judged a nitpick; I kept it because it
   bears on the Response's point that the custom is a public, enforced modesty.
2. The main charge (cpara on the "concrete wall" paragraph and the Response): the opening
   example shows public downplaying, which the post itself later allows, not the private
   shame it argues against, and no example of the latter is given. A fair defender might say
   the example only shows that an egalitarian instinct exists. The title does call it an
   instinct, and the Response notes the only link is "the ancestral environment".
3. "sits uneasily" for the two defenses (pride as motivator; not graded on a curve). They are
   not logically contradictory (one is about motivation, one about grading), so I did not use
   "contradicts". The post itself flags "despite my principles".
4. Pronoun flags: all refer to Lee's hunter and informants, characters of a quoted account.
5. "Wikipedia's article summarizes": secondary source, cited as such.
