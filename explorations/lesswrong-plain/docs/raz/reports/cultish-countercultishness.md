# Report: cultish-countercultishness

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Cultishness is not an essence that a group has or lacks but an attractor, a set of ordinary
group failure modes (polarization, halo effects, evaporative cooling) that any cause slides
toward unless it keeps resisting them. The nervous question "This isn't a cult, is it?" goes
wrong in several ways: it treats cultishness as all-or-nothing, it seeks reassurance instead
of evidence, and (the author suspects) it is driven by fear of weirdness, the fear of lonely
dissent that cults themselves exploit, rather than by fear of real harms. The remedy is to
look at a group's reasoning processes directly, accept that some doubts stay unresolved, and
treat unusual beliefs and lofty goals as risk factors to watch, not as the disease.
