# Report: making-history-available

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The inverse of generalizing from fictional evidence is failing to be moved enough by
historical evidence, because history reaches us as ink on paper and is processed like
fiction. The author, realizing after the mysterious-answer episode that vitalism's rise and
fall had happened to real people, resolved to think as if everything in the history books had
happened to them personally, since books and eyes both carry causal traces of events. Doing
so makes the world seem older and more fragile, shows how often our biases have failed us,
and, if we remember how many times the world changed in ways we did not guess, should make us
less shocked by the strangeness of the future.
