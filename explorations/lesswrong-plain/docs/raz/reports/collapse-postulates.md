# Report: collapse-postulates

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Collapse postulates arose, the post says, from reading squared amplitudes as probabilities
and concluding that knowledge (Heisenberg) or later an objective process removes all but one
part of the wavefunction, at a time when no one thought more than one world could exist.
The post asks why collapse theories insist on one survivor and on collapse before humans are
superposed, suggests (then disowns as ad hominem) that the motive is to keep the intuition of
a single self, and notes that experiments keep confirming superposition in larger systems
while no collapse theory has experimental support. Finally it lists eight respects in which
collapse would be the only non-linear, non-unitary, discontinuous, configuration-nonlocal,
CPT-violating, Liouville-violating, random and faster-than-light phenomenon in physics, and
asks what more it must do before physicists reject it.

## 5. Not verified

- The 50-micrometre experiment could not be identified (see above).
- Donadi et al. 2021 (Diósi–Penrose test) and the molecule experiments were not opened; both
  are taken from the SEP entry.
- Items 4 (non-local in configuration space) and 6 (Liouville, many-to-one) were checked only
  informally: a von Neumann projection is many-to-one and acts on the whole wavefunction at
  once, so both are accurate for the textbook postulate. For GRW, a single hit (multiplication
  by a Gaussian, then renormalization) is invertible for a given hit centre, so "many-to-one"
  is less clear-cut there; I made no note because I could not source it.
- Item 7 (random) is accurate for collapse; no note.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- The first cpara says the post's history ("before it occurred to anyone that the laws ...
  might apply universally") is "loosely stated". A defender could say Everett (1957) was the
  first to apply the laws without exception, including observers, which is true; von Neumann
  kept the "abstract ego" outside. The note's point is narrower: applying the laws to the
  apparatus was considered and produced the measurement problem, so collapse was added because
  of that result, not for lack of the idea. The SEP collapse entry supports this.
- Heisenberg note: "Overstated", not "false". Heisenberg did tie the change of the probability
  function to knowledge, so half the post's attribution holds; he denied the conscious-awareness
  half in the same passage.
- The cfact on "devised in a time when it simply didn't occur to any physicists": the sentence
  is in a paragraph answering why "collapse theories considered in modern academia" keep one
  survivor, so I judged it applies to those theories. A defender could say it refers to the
  original postulate only; the note grants that it is true of that.
- The cynic cpara: "does not withdraw it, so the reading stays in the post" is a description of
  the text, modelled on the "Forget for a moment" note in "Something to Protect". It is close
  to a motive reading of the author; the editor may prefer to cut the last clause.
- Item 8 note: "This holds only if many-worlds, or some other way around Bell's theorem, is
  assumed." I chose "some other way around" to cover superdeterministic or retrocausal
  options without naming them.
- The final clogic (the list counts the costs of collapse only) is a scope point with a
  pointer to order 246; kept to one sentence plus the quotation, as the brief asks.
- Pronouns: "he" for Heisenberg (historical figure).
