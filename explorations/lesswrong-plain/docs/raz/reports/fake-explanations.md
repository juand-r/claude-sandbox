# Report: fake-explanations

## 1. The argument in three sentences

A classroom joke: physics students, finding the side of a plate next to a radiator cool and
the far side warm, offer "heat conduction" and similar answers instead of saying "I don't
know"; the instructor had turned the plate around. An answer like "because of heat
conduction", used so that it could explain either outcome, controls no anticipation and is
no better than "because of magic"; the two differ only in literary genre. Real physics
would measure temperatures and use the diffusion equation, and the students' deeper error
was to think that saying science-sounding words is doing physics.

## 6. Judgment calls

- The main logic note (paragraph 6) says the sentence "If "because of heat conduction" can
  also explain the radiator-adjacent side feeling cooler, then it can explain pretty much
  anything" is "loosely stated", because the post's own physicist explains the cooler near
  side by heat conduction plus a starting condition. The defender's reply is the next
  paragraph's "used in such fashion"; the note acknowledges it. The editor should decide
  whether "loosely stated" is still fair given that qualifier. I think it is, because the
  genre comparison with "magic" treats the phrases as equals.
- The cstyle note on "maximum entropy" and "anticipation-isomorphic" is a wording note. I kept
  it because the undefined terms carry the paragraph's conclusion; it could be cut under
  STANDARDS 2.4(2) if the editor sees it as a nitpick.
- Response credits the post with a "good joke" and a "sound point" (one sentence).
- Reserved words: none used in notes, Response or n.b.
- Pronouns: "he/him" refer to Feynman only (historical figure).
