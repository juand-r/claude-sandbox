# Report: 2-place-and-1-place-words ("2-Place and 1-Place Words", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-06-27; book order 275)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

The pulp artist who thinks a bug-eyed monster will find a human woman sexy treats sexiness,
a function of two arguments (admirer, entity), as a function of one (entity), which is a
variable question fallacy or mind projection fallacy. An "equally valid" alternative is that
each speaker's "sexy" names a different one-place function that makes no mention of the
speaker (Fred's is Sexiness_20934, Bloogah's Sexiness_72546), and currying unifies the two
views: the two-place word eats an admirer and returns a fixed one-place function; the same
analysis dissolves the Twin Earth debate about "water", which, the post says, rested on
instinctively currying or uncurrying concepts. One must train oneself to be aware of the
curried and uncurried forms, because the brain by instinct labels its own function
"Woman.sexiness"; the analysis will be used later to taboo "objective", "subjective" and
"arbitrary".

2. SEP, "Moral Relativism" (`sep_moral-relativism.txt`). Matched: "moral contextualism
   (commonly attributed to Harman 2000a and Dreier 1990 among others) is the view that the
   semantic content of moral sentences depends on the context in which they are expressed

   are expressed".
3. SEP, "Moral Sentimentalism" (Kauppinen; first published 2014, revised 2021),
   https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/moral-sentimentalism/ (`sep_moral-sentimentalism.txt`).
   Matched: "Simple Dispositionalist Subjectivism improves on it by indexing moral properties to

   another."; "A basic objection to these views is the Missing Disagreement Problem (Moore

4. SEP, "Two-Dimensional Semantics" (Schroeter; first published 2010, revised 2021),

   criticisms, targeting its commitment to apriori conceptual analysis, its claim that
   1-intentions capture a type of meaning, and its internalist approach to assigning contents."
   ("1-intentions" is spelled so in the SEP sentence; elsewhere the entry writes "1-intension".
   I do not quote the word; the note says "which the entry calls the 1-intension".)

## 6. Judgment calls

1. The "whole confusingness" cpara ("claims more than the post shows") and the Response
   paragraph on it. The post's sentence is unhedged and unargued. The evidence is that the
   two-level answer existed and drew substantive criticism (SEP). I do not say the post is wrong
   about the debate, only that it asserts more than it shows. The Response adds that the post
   "does not engage those criticisms"; this is a scope sentence and could be cut.
2. The credits (hidden-parameter relativism, response-dependence, contextualism, Kaplan's
   character, rigidification) are given as support, not as "the post fails to credit". Five
   credits in one post may be dense; the editor may merge the response-dependence and
   relativism notes.
3. The Missing Disagreement note (last paragraph) and the Response paragraph on costs for moral
   words: information for the book's later use, since this post does not apply the analysis to
   morality. I avoid saying the post faces the problem; the Response leaves it to the later posts.
4. Reserved words: "contradict" appears only as SEP's term for the missing-disagreement problem,
   not as a claim about the post. Pronoun flags are for Fred, a character the post calls "he".
5. The rigidification clogic says the post's move is "similar" to Wiggins's; SEP's
   rigidification fixes the starting point of idealization to actual sentiments, the post fixes
   Fred's current function. The similarity is that both keep the function from changing with
   later tastes.
