# Report: an-alien-god

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Nature is full of apparent purpose, but its purposes conflict (foxes and rabbits) and are
often cruel (ichneumon wasps, starving elephants), which fits one benevolent designer
poorly; evolution explains both because there is no "Evolution Fairy" deciding what is
helpful, only genes whose effects cause their own copies to become more frequent, separately
in each population and without foresight. Hence people who imagine what would be good for
organisms predict wrongly, and evolution yields designs a human engineer would call stupid
(the backward retina) and cruelties that would cost nothing to remove, because there is no
one in the system to argue with. Evolution is closer to a god than to random noise, and
godlike in being bodiless, omnipresent and unmade, but it is mindless, divided and not
nice, Lovecraft's Azathoth; religionists who say they believe in a vague deity, or await
science's discovery of God, should have recognized it when Darwin found it.

## 5. Items not verified

- Both George Williams quotations (*Adaptation and Natural Selection*, 1966). The archive.org
  copies are lending-only (401) and the Google Books API was over quota. The notes say they
  could not be checked. In particular I could not check whether Williams "suggests" the
  rattle diverts attacks from the head.
- The Damien Broderick quotation: no source in the post; I did not find one. Said in the note.
- Paley's watch: Paley's heath becomes a "desert" in the post. Paraphrase, not in quotation
  marks; no note (nitpick).
- "Hence the phrase 'natural selection'": Darwin chose the name to mark its relation to
  breeders' selection. I did not fetch the *Origin* passage and made no note (the line reads
  as wordplay).
- Spontaneous generation from "straw and dirty shirts" is van Helmont's recipe (a Catholic,
  not an atheist); but some materialists did hold spontaneous generation, and the post does
  not say all its believers were atheists. No note.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. The two Hume notes. "Probably not" is hedged, and the Manichaean sentence is only "I
   wonder". The notes answer the post's own question with a source; they do not call the
   post wrong. For Hinduism, the note says Philo argues from shared labour, not from
   conflict, so the post's specific argument from conflict is not claimed to be Hume's. The
   In short line says "whose main moves Hume had made ... where the post guesses that no one
   had". Check that this is fair to "Probably not" (a defender could call it a joke; the note
   is still information).
2. The vague-deity note (clogic at "when Darwin said "Aha!""). It uses the post's own "Evolution
   is not a God" and "the Maker has no mind". A defender could say "vague deity" means only
   "whatever made us". The note says the charge needs such a notion; it does not say no one
   holds it. The Response says the ending "sits uneasily with" the post's own account.
3. Credit given: "closer to God than it is to pure random entropy" is called "the post's best
   reason for calling evolution godlike" (cpara) and "a correct and useful way" (Response).
4. Pronouns: "his" for Darwin, "he"/"He" for Philo (a character in Hume's dialogue). None
   for Yudkowsky.
5. Reserved words: none.
6. "Restates the main point, for the third time in a few paragraphs" (cpara): counted. The
   "no Evolution Fairy" point appears in "There isn't an Evolution Fairy...", "The key
   realization is that there is no Evolution Fairy", and "There's no Evolution Fairy that
   reaches in from outside" (the paragraph noted).
