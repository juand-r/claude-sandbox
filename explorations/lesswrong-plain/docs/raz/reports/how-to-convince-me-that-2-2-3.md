# Report: how-to-convince-me-that-2-2-3

## 1. The argument in three sentences

A commenter (Cihan Baran) said that since no situation could make 2 + 2 = 4 false, the
belief may be held unconditionally; the author answers that what could make a fact false
and what could change a belief are different questions. The author describes a situation
that would convince them that 2 + 2 = 3 (earplugs that add to three, a mental picture that
agrees, calculators and books that agree) and says this is the same kind of evidence that
now supports 2 + 2 = 4, a belief that must have got there by a process entangled with
reality, since otherwise it could be right only by coincidence. So unconditional facts do
not require unconditional beliefs, the author's belief is not blind faith, and a footnote
asks Christians what situation would convince them of Islam.

## 6. Judgment calls

- The main line of criticism (the post states a disputed empiricism as plain fact, and its
  arguments are the ones Frege, Kant and Quine address) is a philosophical judgment built on
  SEP summaries. I kept each claim about a philosopher to what the SEP text says, and marked
  SEP summaries as such. The fair defender could say an essay replying to a blog comment need
  not survey philosophy of mathematics (STANDARDS 2.2.4). My answer: the notes do not fault
  missing citations; they fault the arguments offered (acquisition vs justification; earplugs
  vs arithmetic; unconditional fact from experience), each with a source for the objection.
- "The Stanford Encyclopedia's entry ... The failure counts against the claim about liquids,
  not against arithmetic": the last sentence is my gloss on the entry's pairing, not a quote.
- "Quite easy" \clogic contrasts the post's small scenario with Quine's "not something we can
  imagine in any detail". A defender could say a thought experiment need not imagine all of
  science; the note states the contrast and makes no charge. Could be cut.
- Footnote \clogic: "the expected answer has already been given" reads the footnote's
  "Presumably". It is framed as a question and invites other answers, which the note says.
- Reserved words: "false" appears only in the non-judging sense (a fact being false; the
  liquids claim being false, per SEP). "Rejected" (Response, In short) for Quine rests on SEP's
  "No particular experience confirms" and "not advancing"; for Kant on B14; for Frege on
  "derided".
- Pronouns: "he"/"his" occur only inside the SEP quotation of Hawthorne's point (generic).
- Credit: the Response opens with one paragraph saying the reply to the commenter is correct.
