# Report: how-to-convince-me-that-2-2-3

## 1. The argument in three sentences

A commenter (Cihan Baran) said that since no situation could make 2 + 2 = 4 false, the
belief may be held unconditionally; the author answers that what could make a fact false
and what could change a belief are different questions. The author describes a situation
that would convince them that 2 + 2 = 3 (earplugs that add to three, a mental picture that
agrees, calculators and books that agree) and says this is the same kind of evidence that
now supports 2 + 2 = 4, a belief that must have got there by a process entangled with
reality, since otherwise it could be right only by coincidence. So unconditional facts do
not require unconditional beliefs, the author's belief is not blind faith, and a footnote
asks Christians what situation would convince them of Islam.
