# Report: arguing-by-definition ("Arguing \"By Definition\"", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-02-20)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Arguments "by definition" start from visible features that fit a dictionary definition, but
the visible features are rarely what the dispute is about; the legitimate move from visible to
hidden properties is a probabilistic inference from the cluster structure of the world, which no
definition changes. Saying "by definition" amounts to refusing to condition on further evidence
(Socrates seen with herbologists, the coin already seen to show heads), and people reach for the
phrase exactly when the default inference has been called into doubt, or when X does not resemble
the central members of Y and a connotation of Y is to be sneaked in ("atheism is, by
definition, a religion"). By the author's informal tally over thirteen years, "by definition"
outside mathematics is among the strongest signals of a flawed argument, so readers should drop
the phrase.
