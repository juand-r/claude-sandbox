# Report: raised-in-technophilia

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The author grew up a technophile, taught by Pournelle and a father who saw regulators as
the enemies of progress, and still holds that this childhood view was not "too wrong" about
who the Bad Guys were: the historical record, including an estimate that slow FDA approval
kills far more people than it saves, justifies an allergy to "technology has risks as well
as benefits". The mistake was to think that avoiding everything the Bad Guys did made one a
Good Guy, which made any departure from full speed ahead feel like joining the
middle-of-the-road "pretenders to Deep Wisdom". So when a 1997 or 1998 debate about
nanotechnology made the young author see that extinction was possible, the young author
kept the old plan (race to AI) under a new reason instead of rethinking, which shows that
even a sharp Traditional Rationalist falls short of what is needed.

## 3. Arithmetic

- FDA: post, per year: 5,000 prevented against 20,000 to 120,000 caused, ratio 4 to 24.
  Gieringer as quoted, per decade: 5,000 (or 10,000 worst case) against 21,000 to 120,000,
  ratio 4.2 to 24 (2.1 to 12 against the worst case). Per-year figures are ten times the
  per-decade ones; the ratios are unchanged, which is why the note says the comparison survives
  and the slip stays out of the In short line.
- Thalidomide: 10,000 worldwide against 17 in the US (post) matches Wikipedia's "over 10,000"
  and 17.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- Reserved words: none left in my own voice. "Wrong" appears only in quotations of the post
  ("too wrong", "usually wrong").
- The cpara on "And trying to play the middle": "``In defiance of brute historical fact''
  overstates the case" rests on a reading of "neither good nor evil of itself" (worth depends on
  use). A fair defender might say this is the childhood view being described; the adult author
  endorses the Bad Guy description later ("this isn't such a bad description"), which is why I
  kept it. It could be cut as a nitpick.
- The cpara "rests on the one case" (middle way "usually wrong"). Mild; could be cut.
- The Sandberg note (opponent's aim "more modest") is information, not a charge, and is hedged
  ("the one thread I read"). The editor may think it argues with the memoir; I kept it because
  the brief asks to check the author's descriptions of 1990s debates against the archive.

  1996 AI-danger post as leaving an Earth-born intelligence alive. That reading is mine.

  Future"); it is not in a note as such. The notes support its parts (FDA note; the
