# Report: raised-in-technophilia

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The author grew up a technophile, taught by Pournelle and a father who saw regulators as
the enemies of progress, and still holds that this childhood view was not "too wrong" about
who the Bad Guys were: the historical record, including an estimate that slow FDA approval
kills far more people than it saves, justifies an allergy to "technology has risks as well
as benefits". The mistake was to think that avoiding everything the Bad Guys did made one a
Good Guy, which made any departure from full speed ahead feel like joining the
middle-of-the-road "pretenders to Deep Wisdom". So when a 1997 or 1998 debate about
nanotechnology made the young author see that extinction was possible, the young author
kept the old plan (race to AI) under a new reason instead of rethinking, which shows that
even a sharp Traditional Rationalist falls short of what is needed.
