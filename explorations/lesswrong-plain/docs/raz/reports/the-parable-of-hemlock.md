# Report: the-parable-of-hemlock

## 1. The argument in three sentences

A student of philosophy insists that Socrates must die of hemlock because "all men are
mortal by definition," and when Socrates does not die, concludes by the same certainty that
he is not a man. The post argues a dilemma: if "all humans are mortal" is an empirical
generalization, the conclusion is a guess; if it is part of the definition, you cannot know
Socrates is human until you see him die. More generally, a valid syllogism is valid in every
possible world, so its validity is no evidence about which world we are in; logic can only
show what our uncertain premises already predict (the "Litany Against Logic").
