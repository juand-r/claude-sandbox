# Report: superstimuli-and-the-collapse-of-western-civilization ("Superstimuli and the Collapse of Western Civilization", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-03-16)

Book order 143 (placed after three November 2007 and 2009 posts, though written earlier). Batch 3a.

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Products can be engineered to match evolved preferences more strongly than anything in the
ancestral environment (candy bars, retouched models, video games that people play until they
die), and because competitors each try to be a little more tempting than the rest, markets will
push such superstimuli past the point of harm. People cannot simply say no, because willpower is
a limited evolutionary compromise and resisting temptation spends an exhaustible supply of mental
energy, perhaps faster against superstimuli. Whether government should act is left open, and the
post ends, by an avowed argument from fiction, with the suggestion that falling birth rates may be
the market supplying ever more tempting alternatives to children.

## 3. Arithmetic

- "57 hours" (paragraph 2) and "58 hours straight" (paragraph 6, of World of Warcraft) do not match
  any of the three linked cases: about 50 hours (StarCraft), "several continuous days" (World of
  Warcraft; Guinness, per a search result, says three days, not checked), seven days. 58 vs 50 is
  16%, under the one-fifth threshold, and the post's argument does not depend on it; no note.
  The World of Warcraft label on the 58 hours probably conflates the Korean StarCraft case with the
  Chinese World of Warcraft case; not noted.
- "5% more addictive ... 50% of your competitor's customers": illustrative ("may"); nothing to
  compute.
- Seven months: post 2007-03-16; "The Logical Fallacy of Generalization from Fictional Evidence"
  posted 2007-10-16 (`data/originals/the-logical-fallacy-of-generalization-from-fictional.md`).

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. Reserved words: "wrong" appears twice, both paraphrasing the post's own "doesn't mean it isn't
   going wrong". No reserved word is used as a judgment.
2. The Funk quotation. I say "I could not find this sentence in the novel" and give the nearest line;
   I do not say "misquotes", because the sentence could come from another version or text (the post
   is dated a year after the February 2006 text). The Response says it "does not appear in the text of
   the novel that its author offers for download", which is exactly what I checked.
3. The ego-depletion note cites later replications (2016, 2021) against a 2007 post. The model posts
   do the same (Scope Insensitivity's 2021 replications). The Response says the failures do not show
   that resisting temptation costs nothing, only that the named mechanism was not found. The note
   omits the Vohs et al. exploratory result (d = 0.08, significant, when exclusion criteria are
   ignored); add it if the editor thinks the omission overstates the failure.
4. The Tinbergen note is credit-type information (the idea's origin), not a charge of taking credit:
   the post does not claim the term as its own.
5. The title: the Response says the collapse is argued only in the last paragraph with "Perhaps" and
   a novel. The title may be partly a joke; I did not call the title misleading.
