# Report: a-priori ("A Priori", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-10-08; book order 223)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Occam's razor cannot be justified without appealing to it, and the post pictures
philosophers as calling a truce over such unarguable beliefs under the label "a priori
truth". Against this, it argues that thoughts are physical brain events, so knowing
1 + 1 = 2 "by pure thought" is in effect observing one's own brain, no different as a way of
knowing from watching another brain compute the same thing: there is nothing known "a
priori" that could not be known equally well by observation, and the brain's bias toward
simplicity is built, not arbitrary. Occam's razor works because the world is simple, and a
mind that lacks Occam's razor or modus ponens cannot be argued into them, any more than a
rock can; brains were built by selection, not argued into existence, so rationality should
be understood as engines that work rather than arguments that convince.

## 6. Judgment calls

1. The central cpara ("``In effect'' moves from the process that produces a belief to what
   justifies it"). This is the main criticism. A defender will say the post's point is exactly
   that justification is the process (externalism). The note says so ("The step needs the view
   that justification lies in the producing process. That is a position in epistemology"), and
   the next notes show that this view does not by itself exclude the a priori. I avoided
   "wrong".
2. The hunter-gatherer cpara ("do not engage the views they target"). The paragraph is partly
   rhetorical; I credited the demand in its first sentence and faulted only the two examples.
3. The testimony clogic (Burge/Owens). The post's case is observing another brain's neurons,
   not being told; I treat "copies into itself the resulting pattern" as close enough to
   receiving testimony and say "can be granted and run the other way", not that the post is
   refuted.
4. The truce cpara: SEP partly supports the post; I present that first, then that the
   position was by 2007 "one view among several" (SEP: shift away in the second half of the
   20th century).
5. Several credit notes (Hume, Goodman, Carroll, externalism, Chater and Vitányi) are
   information, not faults. The Response states them in one paragraph of credit.
6. Pronouns: he/his only for Hume.
