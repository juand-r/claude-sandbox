# Report: the-scales-of-justice-the-notebook-of-rationality ("The Scales of Justice, the Notebook of Rationality", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-03-13)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

People treat debate like a sport with two teams, so every point against one side counts for the other, and a study of the affect heuristic shows that people merge a technology's risks and benefits into one good or bad feeling. But facts such as a reactor's waste output and its meltdown risk are logically distinct questions with separate answers, and letting one move your estimate of the other gives wrong answers. Scales suit only a single factual question with two possible answers, where (as Jaynes teaches) evidence moves probability between the two hypotheses; for anything more complex, a reasoner should keep a notebook of facts that are on no one's side.

## 2. Sources checked (outside quotations and figures)

**Finucane, Alhakami, Slovic and Johnson (2000), J. Behav. Dec. Making 13:1-17** (the post's footnote).
URL: https://stanford.edu/~knutson/jdm/finucane00.pdf -> `finucane2000.pdf/.txt` (pdftotext; "fi"/"ff" ligatures print as ® and are silently restored in our quotes).
Matched:
- Abstract: "people seem prone to using an `affect heuristic' which improves judgmental efficiency by deriving both risk and benefit evaluations from a common source — affective reactions to the stimulus item." We quote "from a common source".
- p. 3: "Though distinct, risks and benefits generally tend to be positively correlated." and "Although risk and benefit may be positively correlated in the environment, numerous studies have shown them to be negatively related in people's minds."
- Study 2 design: "A mixed, 4 (affective information: high risk/low risk/high benefit/low benefit) × 3 (technologies: nuclear power/natural gas/food preservatives) design"; ratings "on 10-point scales"; "Ratings of risk and benefit before and after the information manipulation"; "the nonmanipulated attribute generally changed in the direction affectively congruent with the manipulation (the only exceptions were Food LB and Nuclear LB)". Hence "generally moved the other way".
- Appendix, high-benefit vignette: "nuclear power does not depend on a diminishing supply of fossil fuels". High-risk vignette: "the nuclear fuel cycle produces radioactive hazards and waste at every stage" (waste as a risk).
- No experiment about waste versus meltdown probability, or about a particular reactor design, appears in the paper.

**Jaynes, Probability Theory: The Logic of Science (Cambridge, 2003)**, full text PDF at https://jhanley.biostat.mcgill.ca/bios601/GaussianModel/JaynesProbabilityTheory.pdf -> `jaynes_full_mcgill.pdf/.txt`. Matched: "Unless the observed facts are absolutely impossible on hypothesis H0 , it is meaningless to ask how much those facts tend ‘in themselves’ to confirm or refute H0 ." ## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- The cfact "The paper did not test this." The post introduces the reactor as a hypothetical ("Suppose that I first tell you") but then states "If you tell people a reactor design produces less waste, they rate its probability of meltdown as lower" after "studies such as the above show". I read the second as a claim about findings. A defender could say it is the same hypothetical restated. The note grants that the finding runs in the same direction.
- The clogic on "relinquish her scales or relinquish her sword". The post might mean only that facts should not be weighed against each other when estimating them. The note says "Broader than the post supports" and keeps to that; it does not say the post is wrong.
- "wrong" appears only in paraphrases of the post's own claim ("get physical questions wrong").
- The cpara on the sports paragraph calls it "a caricature, marked as one by ``obviously''". I did not fault it for lacking evidence, since the next paragraph supplies the study.
