# Report: science-as-attire

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The X-Men use "evolution" and "mutation" only as the literary genre of science: a mutant who
throws lightning after one mutation would falsify evolutionary theory, and a theory that
covered it would explain anything. Many people who believe in evolution likewise do not know
what it permits and forbids; they use "evolution" for planned progress or for things outside
biology, probably to belong to the scientific in-crowd, and critics of superhuman AI reject
it by comparing it to apocalyptic literature rather than by any model. The test for whether
a scientific belief is yours is which future experiences it forbids; anything else is
probably passwords or attire.

## 6. Judgment calls

- Paragraph 2 cpara credits the Storm argument ("the argument holds"). This is a biology
  judgment: several coordinated complex adaptations from one mutation would conflict with
  gradual selection. I did not cite a source; it is standard.
- The AI note (paragraph 6) could be read as a burden-of-proof complaint. The linked post
  itself argues that asserting impossibility without grounds is "unscientific", so a
  defender could say the post is consistent. The note is limited to what is on the page: a
  model is demanded of the critics and none is given for the outcome.
- The paragraph 3 cpara says no evidence is given for the comic writers' purpose ("purely").
  This is mild; it could be cut if the editor reads "purely ... what they conceive" as a
  figure of speech.
- The "evolution" dictionary note: the defender might say the people meant biology. The post
  gives no example of anyone saying so; the note says only that the ordinary sense makes no
  biological claim.
- No reserved words. Pronoun "her" refers to Storm, a character.
