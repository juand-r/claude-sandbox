# Report: Einstein's Speed (batch 4d, order 256)

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Individual scientists sometimes rationally know things before Science confirms them, and not
only by spotting regularities in data: Einstein reached General Relativity by armchair
reasoning from principles (relativity of motion, equivalence of inertial and gravitational
mass), using Mercury's perihelion only as a final check. In Bayesian terms this is still
induction, with a simplicity prior over the possible "characters of physical law", updated on
the other known laws, so his confidence before the 1919 eclipse (and against Bohr) was
justified. The feat is possible but hard and rare, history over-remembers the winners, and
ordinary people should use every scrap of evidence, though they may train themselves to
reason from scanty evidence where they will later learn whether they were right.

## 3. Arithmetic

- Potential Einsteins: one in a million of a 2008 world population of about 6.7 billion is
  6,700, so "at least six thousand" holds for any population above 6 billion.
- Newtonian deflection 0.87 = half of 1.75 (Kennefick gives both values).
- Mercury: Entwurf 18 of the 43-45 seconds of arc per century missing, "less than half".

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. Reserved word "wrong", five uses: the Entwurf theory "wrong" (it failed Mercury and was
   abandoned by Einstein as "entirely untenable"); Einstein "wrong" about a static universe
   (1917 use of the constant). I think both meet the word.
2. The cosmological-constant note (cfact). The post's parenthesis is half a joke; the note
   argues from the post's own rule. It could be cut as minor if the editor thinks it a nitpick.
3. "Mach's principle" misnamed (cfact). It is a naming error, but in a post about the history
   of physics a reader would be misled about what Mach's principle is; and the next note (the
   1993 poll) depends on the correct name. Kept short.
4. The Eötvös note on "almost no experimental data": the post later concedes Einstein "used the
   data he already had", and the note says so. I kept it because the concession is general and
   the equality of masses is the specific gravitational datum the post's own account uses.
5. The Brush note: Brush's abstract speaks of scientists' weighting, the post of when the
   theory was "blessed as the public knowledge of humanity". I phrased the note as "fits the
   publicity better than the physicists" rather than calling the post wrong.
6. The evolutionary-psychology note imports a field dispute; I kept it because the post takes a
   side ("The most sane thinking I have seen in any scientific field") and gives a reason
   ("do later find out") that is the disputed point. Hedge ("perhaps") kept.
7. The Bayesian paragraph note ("describes the case in Bayesian terms; it does not derive
   anything that could be checked") is my judgment; the editor may find it a scope complaint.
8. Credit notes: Einstein's 1919 principle-theory passage and the 1952 Born letter both support
   the post; the priority-bias paragraph is credited as answering the survivorship point made
   against Faster Than Science.
9. Honest section is 534 words, a little above 500.
