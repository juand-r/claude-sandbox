# Report: the-proper-use-of-humility

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Good science needs humility, but of a particular kind: taking actions against your own
possible errors (fail-safes, double-checking), not the creationist's selective doubt, the
lazy student's "I can never be certain", or social modesty about one's status. Vague
notions of humility can justify anything and usually serve as excuses to shrug (motivated
skepticism), and humility is easy to profess while changing nothing, as Dennett's "belief
in belief" and Galbraith's "busy on the proof" illustrate. So judge a "humble" line of
thought by whether the actions it recommends make you stronger or weaker: to be humble is
to take specific actions in anticipation of your own errors.
