# Report: on-expressing-your-concerns

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Asch's experiments show that conformity is powerful, that one dissenter frees others, and
that the dissenter's effect lasts only while the dissent continues; an anecdote about a
project meeting shows how politeness lets a group reassure itself into overconfidence
("pluralistic ignorance"). For rationalists, who accept that common knowledge of disagreement
means someone is irrational, the key lesson is to distinguish "expressing a concern" from
"disagreeing", since ideal reasoners converge by sharing evidence. But socially the two are
the same, voicing a concern cannot be undone and will not be thanked, so the reader must weigh
these costs and benefits alone.
