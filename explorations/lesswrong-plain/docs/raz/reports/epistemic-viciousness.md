# Report: epistemic-viciousness ("Epistemic Viciousness", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-03-13)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Gillian Russell's essay on "epistemic viciousness" in the martial arts lists how bad beliefs
take hold there: the art is treated as sacred, practitioners are invested in their
techniques, beginners must trust a teacher, historical masters get deference, and data are
too poor to test techniques or training, while practitioners act as if they could afford to
believe anything. The author adds, from memory, that martial arts declined once real fights
stopped sorting good schools from bad, citing videos of black belts and a ki master beaten by
real fighters. Every one of these risk factors, the post says, carries over to any attempt
at a "rationality dojo", and it asks readers what can be done about it.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- "transfers straight over" note (clogic): the post asserts that every factor transfers. I
  noted only that Russell's reason for one factor (no learning from a book) may not hold for
  rationality, which the author teaches in writing, and phrased it as "does not say whether".
  A defender could reply that a dojo means in-person practice, where trust in the teacher
  returns. The editor may prefer to cut it; I kept it out of the In short line's wording
  beyond "asserts without examining".
- The decline note ("videos ... do not show that things went downhill"): one sentence of
  logic against the post's only evidence for a historical claim. Could be seen as a nitpick.
- Russell's partial answers are given as information for the reader (Book II lesson: omitted
  help is not a fault). They are in the notes and the Response, not in the In short line.
- Pronouns: I avoided pronouns for Russell (the essay describes the author as an
  "eleven-year-old girl", but that is not a pronoun statement). Yanagi keeps "he/him" as in
  Skeptoid.
