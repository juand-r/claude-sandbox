# Report: absolute-authority

## 1. The argument in three sentences

People who treat knowledge as a matter of authority read science's admission that it may
be wrong as a confession that it cannot be trusted; the author guesses (with "I suspect",
"I fear") that the habit comes from religion and from school, and offers four short lines
for a public debate. In private, the first step is to see that one can make moral and
factual distinctions without certainty: the "Argument from the Argument from Gray" (without
certainty the moral relativists win) fails because certainty is sufficient but not necessary
for choosing, and because the relativist chain breaks where all grays become one shade.
The learner also needs a culture where admitting error is not shameful, the habit of looking
rather than being preached at, and probability as calibration: people's "absolute"
certainties are often wrong, 99.9999% is already one error in a million statements, and
probability 1 is infinite certainty, so scientists might say "not infinitely certain".
