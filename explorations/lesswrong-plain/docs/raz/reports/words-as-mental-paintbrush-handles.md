# Report: words-as-mental-paintbrush-handles

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Asked to picture a "triangular lightbulb", the mind produces and revises an image without
words, which shows that words call up rich, wordless concepts that can draw pictures in the
visual cortex. The decades-long debate over whether people really have mental images or
only symbol-like labels was "spectacularly silly", a legacy of behaviorism (the author
thinks), and was settled by mental-rotation experiments and then by neuroimaging (Kosslyn).
So a word is only a short label or pointer to a concept far larger than the word, and
people get into trouble with words when they forget how much lies behind them.

## 6. Judgment calls

- The "silly" note uses the post's own "used to be harder to answer" plus the Reisberg
  finding (via SEP). A defender could say the author thinks introspection should have
  settled it for everyone; the Reisberg finding is the answer to that, but it is reported
  secondhand.
- "Legacy of behaviorism" note: I kept the author's "I think" and wrote "the sources point
  the other way", not "wrong". The case rests on SEP's history plus the fact that the
  propositional side posited internal representations, which traditional behaviorism
  tried to avoid.
- Behaviorism note (\cfact): Skinner only. Watson did reject imagery; I did not claim all
  behaviorists accepted thoughts. The "reflex" part of the sentence is left alone.
- Neuroimaging note and Response give credit: the picture view is the one the current SEP
  entry favors. The criticism is only that the debate was presented as closed in 2008.
  Using a 2015 paper title as evidence of that is indirect; the 2003 Pylyshyn abstract is
  the direct evidence.
- Aphantasia note: framed as a limit, not an error; the post's own list of concept
  functions is credited. Editor may judge it scope-only.
- Reserved words: none used as charges. Pronouns: "He" appears only inside the SEP quote
  about Skinner (historical figure); "himself" for Kosslyn removed.
