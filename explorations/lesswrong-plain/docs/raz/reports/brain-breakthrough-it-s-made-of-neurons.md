# Report: brain-breakthrough-it-s-made-of-neurons ("Brain Breakthrough! It's Made of Neurons!", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-04-01; book order 221)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Posted on April 1st as an instance of the "Amazing Breakthrough Day" the author had proposed a
week earlier, the post is a mock press release reporting, as if new, the old discovery (Cajal,
Golgi's stain) that the brain is an enormously complicated network of cells. The release
gathers the history of neuroscience (Alcmaeon, Broca, Nemesius's ventricles, Galvani and
Helmholtz, Babbage's engine, Darwin) to argue that finding a complicated material system where
the mind should be is strong evidence that mind and body are one substance, that intelligence
is not ontologically fundamental, and that this weighs against every religion. Its joke is
that this is shocking news that has been available for a century, so that the reader should
face its implications as if it were announced today.

## 6. Judgment calls

1. Judged as satire, as the brief asks: the Response opens with what the piece does well and says
   its history checks. The criticisms are limited to the release's philosophy (Spinoza) and one
   sweeping clause.
2. The Spinoza note and Response: "not the materialism the release goes on to state" depends on
   the SEP's adopted reading (thought and extension as distinct essences). The SEP mentions a
   rival reading on which God is material; the note says "on the reading the SEP adopts". The
   release does not itself call Spinoza a materialist; the note says the release's next conclusion
   is not Spinoza's view, which I think is fair.
3. The religions clause: I first called it "unhedged"; it sits under "strongly weighs against", so
   I changed this to "reaches further than the rest ... assumes every religion makes the mind
   fundamental, and the release discusses none". The editor may consider this any-satire-does-that.
4. The Eccles note is information about the rival position (a leading neurophysiologist who knew
   the evidence and stayed a dualist). I dropped a sentence of my own arguing that complexity
   fits interactionism, since I had no source for it.
5. The Golgi irony note is information, not a fault.
