# Report: a-prodigy-of-refutation

## 1. The argument in three sentences

In 1996 the young author, primed by an affective death spiral around intelligence, by
technophilia and by science fiction's broad in-group, assumed that a superintelligence would
know what is right, and on a transhumanist mailing list easily refuted the objections
people raised. Because Traditional Rationality pictures science as a fair fight in which
the better arguer wins, and because finding flaws in others' arguments is easier than
getting the facts right, winning those arguments felt like being right; the author's own
"Twelve Virtues" warns that beginners win arguments and overrate themselves. The lesson is
that no one else will argue you out of your mistakes, that one should take Nature rather
than human beings as the opponent, and that the young author's need for a full solution
before accepting an objection measured the young author's level.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- Reserved words: "wrong" and "refuted" occur only where they describe the post's own claims
  (Eliezer1996 thought the objections "obviously wrong"; an argument "he could not refute").
- "My reading" on "the full solution" (note and Response): the post does not name it. I take it
  to be the author's metaethics of July and August 2008. The charge is modest: the post calls
  the field open while measuring its younger self against an unnamed full solution. A fair
  defender could say the metaethics posts are that solution and the post need not repeat it;
  the note does not dispute them, only places them as one side of a debate the post itself says
  is open.
- The logic note on objections 1 and 2 ("close to the author's later view") and the matching
  Response paragraph are presented as support for the post's lesson, not as a fault. "Close" is
  a judgment: objection 1's premise (morality is arbitrary) is not the author's view, only its
  conclusion that a superintelligence would not simply share our morality.
- The In short line says the lesson is "from the author's own ``Twelve Virtues of
  Rationality''". The post quotes it openly; I mean this as description, not a charge of
  borrowing.
- Pronouns: the post calls Eliezer1996 "he"; I used no pronouns for any stage of the author.
  List members are not named, and none gets a pronoun.
