# Report: a-prodigy-of-refutation

## 1. The argument in three sentences

In 1996 the young author, primed by an affective death spiral around intelligence, by
technophilia and by science fiction's broad in-group, assumed that a superintelligence would
know what is right, and on a transhumanist mailing list easily refuted the objections
people raised. Because Traditional Rationality pictures science as a fair fight in which
the better arguer wins, and because finding flaws in others' arguments is easier than
getting the facts right, winning those arguments felt like being right; the author's own
"Twelve Virtues" warns that beginners win arguments and overrate themselves. The lesson is
that no one else will argue you out of your mistakes, that one should take Nature rather
than human beings as the opponent, and that the young author's need for a full solution
before accepting an objection measured the young author's level.
