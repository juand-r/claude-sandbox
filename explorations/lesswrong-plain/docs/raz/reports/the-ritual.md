# Report: the-ritual

## 1. The argument in three sentences

A short story, not an argument: a woman of another "Conspiracy", described as a domain
expert, tells the Bayesian master Jeffreyssai that he is probably wrong about a long-held
premise of his life, and he concedes that he must hold the ritual for considering it. He
spends the day idling his mind and blocking every thought he has had before, then descends
to a bare white room with plaques of maxims and a tally of past outcomes (two changes of
mind, five not), and rehearses his principles, techniques and past oversights. The story
ends as he asks himself the question; it dramatizes the preparation prescribed in "Crisis of
Faith" (posted the day before) and never states the premise or the outcome.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. Fiction (STANDARDS 2.2 test 3). The notes describe; none faults the story for lacking
   evidence. The Response's main point is structural: the story stops before the premise,
   the reasoning and the outcome, so it shows preparation, not results. A fair defender could
   say "that is the point of the story". The Response says only that the reader cannot see
   whether the preparation leads anywhere, which is true either way.
2. The tally paragraph note and the Response credit the story for making plain that a kept
   belief is not a failed doubt, and contrast this with the Aumann example in Crisis of Faith.
   This is a cross-post judgment; check it against the Crisis notes (they agree).
3. Reserved words: "never" remains in the Sun-reflection note (paraphrasing the story's
   "could never alter") and in the Response ("never changing at all", paraphrasing the
   tally rule). "false" appears only in describing Crisis of Faith's premise.
4. The first-paragraph note on book order (names met without explanation) is a cost note, not
   a fault of the 2008 story.
