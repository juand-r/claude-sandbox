# Report: leave-a-line-of-retreat

## 1. The argument in three sentences

A belief is hard to evaluate fairly when its falsehood (or the truth of a feared belief) is too
painful to contemplate, so before weighing the evidence you should visualize in detail the
world in which the feared thing is true and plan what you would do there, as Sun Tzu leaves
an enemy an escape route. Visualizing a possibility as a thought experiment takes less
courage than admitting it is likely, and once done it makes the honest assessment easier; the
theist who pictures a godless world realizes they would not start "slaughtering babies", and
people adapt to bad outcomes (quadriplegics) more than they expect. The technique needs only
the self-honesty to name which ideas scare you and to accept the premise for real, and the
author says it is used by the author personally.

1. Reserved words: "false" and "wrong" appear only in descriptive senses ("even if it is
   false", "objectively right or wrong"). No verdict word.
2. The morality note (\clogic after "slaughtering babies") is the strongest charge. A fair
   defender could say: "The woman meant behaviour; my footnote says the topic came up." The
   note grants the footnote and limits itself to "the argument as others make it"; the post's
   own sentence is general ("all the people who say"). Consider softening if the editor reads
   the post's claim as only about the conversation.
3. The Seneca note gives the precedent and credits the post's new use. It is not a charge of
   copying; the post does not claim originality beyond "one notion I haven't covered here yet".
4. The quadriplegic note: the post's "etc." and informal tone could count as a figure of speech
   (test 3), but the claim is factual and used as support, so I kept it, with the direction
   credited.
