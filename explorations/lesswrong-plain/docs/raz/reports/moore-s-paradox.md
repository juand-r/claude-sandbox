# Report: moore-s-paradox

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Many people may not distinguish believing a proposition from endorsing it, because the word
"belief" has both senses ("I believe in democracy") and no one is taught what actual belief
feels like. So the woman of the earlier posts felt warmth toward the quoted belief "people
are nice", took that feeling for belief, and, seeing that people are not so nice, said
"I believe people are nicer than they are", a real-life case of Moore's paradox. The
commenter Kurige's "I believe that there is a God ... I also believe [in evolved morality]"
is diagnosed the same way, and Kurige is told to state both claims flatly and notice that
this is harder to swallow, which is the difference between endorsing beliefs and having a
single model of the world.

  In short). I read the whole post twice: the paradox is named three times ("I think I
  understand Moore's Paradox", "this real-life case of Moore's Paradox", title) and never
  stated.
- The main criticism ("if right, her sentence is not a case of Moore's paradox as
  philosophers pose it") is my own argument, not a sourced one. It is a logical point: the
  paradox concerns "believe" in the sense of belief (SEP: readings p & B~p, p & ~Bp); on the
  post's own explanation her "believe" means endorse. A fair defender could say "I explained
  a real-life utterance of a Moorean sentence, not the paradox". The note words it that way
  ("shows how a person can utter a Moorean sentence ... It says nothing about the paradox
  itself").

  Kurige's own reply; the note says the test "does not by itself separate", not that it
  fails.
