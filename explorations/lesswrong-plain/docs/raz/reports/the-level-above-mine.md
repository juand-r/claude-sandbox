# Report: the-level-above-mine ("The Level Above Mine", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-09-26)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

E. T. Jaynes's book gave the author a sense of mathematical "formidability", a level of
mastery the author aspires to and admits not yet having in any field; people cannot easily
tell apart levels far above their own. Asked who seemed more natively intelligent than the
author, Marcello Herreshoff named John Conway, and the author accepts, as a real and
ego-damaging possibility, being strictly dumber than Conway. The only humility the author
credits is specific effort predicated on that possibility (blogging so that a younger mind
can overtake, leaving a trail), not modest words, and the author refuses the consolation
that lesser brilliance still allows important work.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- Per the instructions, no remark on the author's abilities. The notes describe the self-assessment and its hedges; the only critical note is on the closing line, and it is answered by the author's own later post.
- The Jaynes-paradox note is information that qualifies an impression the post hedges ("the sense I get", "Maybe because"). I kept it because the image of "blasting them to pieces" states something about the record of a named person. Wallstrom sides with Jaynes conceptually; I quote both halves.
- The posthumous-book note: the post's caveat ("when you write a book, you get a chance to show only your best side") assumes Jaynes shaped the book. I give the fact without drawing a conclusion (it could cut either way: an unfinished book shows less polish, and an editor chose what to include).
- Considered and dropped: a tension between the closing line ("that's not how it works") and "Einstein's Superpowers", where the author suspected that what separates brilliant people from Einstein "is no innate defect of brilliance". The two can be reconciled (that post is about people already at genius level; this one, as "My Bayesian Enlightenment" explains, is about the mastery the author thought Friendly AI required), so a note would fail the fair-defender test.
- Considered and dropped: a style note on "making a mockery of deference" (unclear phrase, but no consequence for the argument).
- Pronouns: Jaynes and Conway keep "his" (historical figures, well documented); the post's author gets none.
