# Report: schools-proliferating-without-evidence ("Schools Proliferating Without Evidence", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-03-15)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Drawing on Robyn Dawes's House of Cards, the post says psychotherapy is a field unbound from
evidence: the Rorschach survives despite failing hundreds of tests, therapy schools do not
differ in outcome, experience brings no gain, and talking to a college professor works as
well as seeing a therapist, yet therapists were licensed, paid and believed without
experimental support. Without evidence to decide between them, schools multiplied, because
prestige came from founding a school on a charismatic story rather than from a tested
technique. Hence any organized practice (the implied target is a rationality school) needs
measurement and controlled testing of its techniques.

- **Lilienfeld, Wood and Garb (2000)**, PMID 26151980: "Although projective techniques continue
  to be widely used in clinical and forensic settings, their scientific status remains highly
  controversial. ... We conclude that there is empirical support for the validity of a small
  number of indexes derived from the Rorschach and TAT. However, the substantial majority of
  Rorschach and TAT indexes are not empirically supported." (A 2003 critique by Hibbard,
  PMID 12763700, argues the evidence for the Rorschach is "more robust than Lilienfeld et al.
  represented"; not cited in the notes, since it only strengthens the "Overstated" point.)
- **Dodo bird verdict.** Wikipedia (raw, `wp_dodo.txt`): "The conjecture was introduced by
  [[Saul Rosenzweig]] in 1936". Luborsky, Singer and Luborsky (1975), PMID 239666: "For
  comparisons of psychotherapy with each other, most studies found insignificant differences
  in proportions of patients who improved (though most patients benefited)." Marcus et al.
  (2014), PMID 25238455: "Wampold et al. (1997), the most recent comprehensive meta-analysis
  to test the Dodo bird hypothesis, yielded consistent evidence of treatment equivalence."
  (Wampold 1997's own abstract was not available: Semantic Scholar elides it.)
- **Experience / paraprofessionals.** Christensen and Jacobson (1994), Crossref
  10.1111/j.1467-9280.1994.tb00606.x abstract: "Research suggests that paraprofessional
  therapists usually produce effects that are greater than effects for control conditions and
  comparable to those for professional therapist treatment". Goldberg et al. (2016), PMID
  26751152: "a very small but statistically significant change in outcome was detected
  indicating that on the whole, therapists' patient prepost d tended to diminish as
  experience (time or cases) increases."
- **Professors.** Strupp and Hadley (1979), PMID 475546: "Highly experienced psychotherapists
  treated 15 patients drawn from a relatively homogeneous patient population (male college
  students ...). A comparable patient group was treated by college professors chosen for their
  ability to form understanding relationships. Patients treated by professors showed, on the
  average, as much improvement as patients treated by professional therapists. Treated groups
  slightly exceeded the controls. Group means, however, obscured considerable individual
  variability." The identification of the post's claim with this study is mine (the post
  cites nothing); it is the well-known study matching "college professor from another field".
- **CBT.** Tolin (2010), PMID 20547435: "From a computerized literature search through
  September 2007 ... CBT was superior to psychodynamic therapy, although not interpersonal or
  supportive therapies, at post-treatment and at follow-up. ... The superiority of CBT over
  alternative therapies was evident only among patients with anxiety or depressive
  disorders."
- **Hedonic psychology.** Sandvik, Diener and Seidlitz (1993), Crossref
  10.1111/j.1467-6494.1993.tb00283.x abstract: "Conventional single-item and multi-item
  self-report measures correlated highly with alternative measures, with theoretical
  correlates of SWB".

  clinicians use it.

  may say the sentence is historical (licensing preceded good trials). The note allows this
  and says the post gives no dates. The In short line lists it among overstatements; the
  editor may think that too strong.

  research already had" controlled testing; what failed was acting on results) is my reading
  of the post's own material, not an outside source. I think it is fair because the post says
  "psychotherapists just can't bring themselves to believe the vast mounds of experimental
  evidence" and calls its findings "experimental results". It is in the In short line.
