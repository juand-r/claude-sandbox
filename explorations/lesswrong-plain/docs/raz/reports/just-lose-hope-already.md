# Report: just-lose-hope-already ("Just Lose Hope Already", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-02-25)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Casey Serin ran up $2.2 million of debt on eight houses bought with fraudulent mortgage applications and still refuses to give up, and Long-Term Capital Management, whose partners included the Nobel laureates Merton and Scholes, kept borrowing more to exploit shrinking margins after its edge had gone. Both show what happens when you refuse to lose hope. Although each profession has its own skills, not turning little mistakes into big ones seems to the author to be a common art, and one of its keys is to be ready to admit you lost.

## 3. Arithmetic

- Serin's age: born 1982-09-10; post dated 2007-02-25; age 24. Matches.
- LTCM equity: post $4.72 billion; Wikipedia $4.7 billion; Lowenstein via Richmond Fed
  $4.67 billion at the start of 1998. Differences under 2%; no note.
- Debt-to-equity: 124.5 / 4.72 = 26.4, matching Wikipedia's "over 25 to 1".
- "in 1998 the inefficiences ... had started to vanish": sources date the start to 1997;
  the post says that by 1998 they "had started" to vanish, which is consistent. No note.
- "Addicted to 40% annual returns": 1995 and 1996 returns 42.8 and 40.8 percent after fees;
  1997 was 17 percent. Figure of speech; no note beyond the paragraph-6 description.

## 6. Judgment calls

- The paragraph-7 `\clogic` gives the post support it does not use (Meriwether's belief that
  the trades would pay off). Leaving it out would have made my later point look like a
  rebuttal of "LTCM refused to lose hope", which the record in fact supports.
- The last `\clogic` and the Response contain a marked inference ("My inference ...: what
  sank the fund was the borrowing, which left it unable to wait"). It rests on the uncited
  Wikipedia sentence above; the editor may want to soften it further or drop it.
- The Merton/Scholes paragraph note is factual, not a charge. The post says "LTCM refused to
  lose hope", not that Merton and Scholes did, so the Response paragraph on this only states
  the facts (partners; founder Meriwether; Scholes uneasy). The editor may cut it as minor.
- Pronouns he/his/him refer to Serin, a character the post calls "he".
- "notably" flag is inside a quotation from the Richmond Fed article.
