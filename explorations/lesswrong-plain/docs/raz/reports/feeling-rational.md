# Report: feeling-rational ("Feeling Rational", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-04-26)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Rationality does not oppose emotion: the author labels an emotion "not rational" when it
rests on mistaken beliefs, and rational when it follows from correct ones, so calm is no
more rational than fear by default. Because emotions arise from beliefs about the world,
the standard of rationality spreads from the belief (a goblin tied my laces) to the
feeling (anger at the goblin), and becoming more rational can make feelings stronger as
well as weaker. Polite society has long treated strong feeling as embarrassing, but there
is nothing wrong with it; "it is rational to feel."

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- Paragraph 7 cpara (proportion). "By the test as written, any feeling that a true belief evokes is rational, however large." This is the standard objection that emotions can be unfitting in size even when the belief is true. I cite no philosopher for it, so it rests on the post's own wording: "an emotion that is evoked by correct beliefs or truth-conducive thinking is a 'rational emotion'", plus its concession "not just about how-the-world-is". A defender could say that the size of a feeling follows from beliefs about how much a thing matters. The post, though, treats values as outside how-the-world-is. I think the note is fair, but it is the main charge in the Response.
- Paragraph 4 clogic (feelings that outlast belief). I use the last paragraph's "Despite all my philosophy I am still embarrassed to confess strong emotions" as the post's own report of such a feeling. A defender could reply that the embarrassment rests on a true belief about listeners ("you're probably uncomfortable hearing them"). I therefore avoided "contradicts" and said only that the post reports a feeling that "persists 'Despite all my philosophy.'"
- Last cpara and Response ("follows from the label", "settles the conclusion in advance"). The post does say "I label", so the note describes the text and makes no charge. My first In-short draft said the definition was "reported as knowledge". I replaced it because it read as a jab.
- Socrates cfact: "overstated", on a Wikipedia source. The post hedges the start date ("at least, and probably long before") but not the continuity. The honest edition keeps the hedge "at least".
- Strange-looks clogic ("sets it aside without a reason"). Minor. Cut it if the margin is crowded.
