# Report: belief-in-belief

## 1. The argument in three sentences

In Sagan's parable of the invisible dragon, the claimant excuses in advance exactly the
tests that would come out negative, so somewhere in their mind they must expect no dragon.
The author explains this with Dennett's "belief in belief": a person can fail to anticipate
X while believing that believing X is good, and cannot admit this to themselves, so the
belief in belief usually shows up as flinches and self-image rather than as explicit
sentences. Belief should be understood widely, including wordless anticipations, and
excuses made in advance are the sign that belief and belief in belief have come apart.
