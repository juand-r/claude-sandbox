# Report: motivated-stopping-and-motivated-continuation

## 1. The argument in three sentences

In real decisions we search for options and evidence and must decide when to stop, and by
analogy with Gilovich's motivated skepticism and credulity, the author proposes that hidden
motives bias that decision: we stop early when the current option is one we want, and keep
searching when we want to avoid a conclusion. R. A. Fisher's insistence, while a tobacco
consultant, that smoking had not been shown to cause cancer is offered as a possible case
of motivated continuation, and demands for more evidence are not virtuous in themselves,
since evidence is costly and slow. Suspect motivated stopping when cheap evidence remains
ungathered after a comfortable conclusion, and motivated continuation when you demand
expensive, slow evidence before doing something uncomfortable.
