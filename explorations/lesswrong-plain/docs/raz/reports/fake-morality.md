# Report: fake-morality

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Religious fundamentalists say that without fear of hell and hope of heaven nothing would
stop people from murdering one another; but a person who had no objection to murder apart
from divine punishment would be relieved, not frightened, if the punishment were withdrawn,
as anyone would be relieved if Omega withdrew a threat against using the bathroom. So the
fear itself shows a moral compass independent of God, which a believer who loses faith can
keep steering by, and the fact that no one argues "what would stop people eating pork?"
shows that believers do not really treat all divine rules alike. The same test applies to
two invented philosophers: the one who argues for selfishness on grounds of benefit to
society and the one who argues for altruism on grounds of personal happiness reveal, by the
criteria they use, values opposite to what they recommend, though which is the altruist is
settled by what each actually does.

## 6. Judgment calls

- Main critical point (P3 clogic, Response, In short, honest n.b.): the opening question is about
  what would stop "people" from murdering; the reply shows only that the frightened believer has a
  motive of their own. I tied this to a real, sourced position (Paley on sustaining motivation)
  rather than to my own argument, and stated it as scope ("does not take up"), because the post's
  advice is addressed to the believer and fits there. The editor may judge it too strong for the
  In short line; the post's thesis survives it.
- The Talmud note says the assumption ascribed to fundamentalists "is not a religious view as
  such". The post says "religious fundamentalists", not all believers, so this is information about
  whose view it is, not a charge of strawman.
- Euthyphro is credited as a relative, with the difference stated (what makes an act right vs what
  moves the believer).
- Reserved word "wrong" appears only in "objectively right or wrong" (describing the metaethical
  claim). "hypothetical philosophers" replaced "invented" in the Summary; the notes say "the
  author's inventions", which the post itself makes plain.
- The cstyle on "s/soul/morality/" is explanatory for readers, not a fault.
