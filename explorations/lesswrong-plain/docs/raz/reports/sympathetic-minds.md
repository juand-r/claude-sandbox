# Report: sympathetic-minds ("Sympathetic Minds", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-01-19; book order 282)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Humans predict other minds by empathy, running their own brain as a model of the other, and on
top of this they have sympathy, feeling what the other feels, which the author suggests evolved
from kin care and reciprocity because the mirroring machinery was already there and a general
urge to share friends' states wins more allies than specific urges. An AI need not work this
way: it can model another mind directly as a separate hypothesis, and an expected paperclip
maximizer would treat humans as machines with levers, managing their reactions without feeling
anything for them. Sympathy is therefore not something to expect of every alien; aliens without
it might trade, keep treaties and even cooperate with us, but they could never be friends.

## 2. Sources checked

Abstracts from PubMed efetch (script `data/sources/5b_sympathetic-minds/pubmed.py`, files named
after the study), 29 Sept 2026. Other files in the same folder.

1. Gallese, Fadiga, Fogassi, Rizzolatti 1996, Brain 119:593 (`gallese1996.txt`): "all of which
   became active both when the monkey performed a given action and when it observed a similar
   action performed by the experimenter."
2. Iacoboni et al. 1999, Science 286:2526 (`iacoboni1999.txt`): "normal human participants were
   asked to observe and imitate a finger movement".
3. Mukamel et al. 2010, Curr Biol 20:750 (`mukamel2010.txt`): "We recorded extracellular activity
   from 1177 cells in human medial frontal and temporal cortices ... A significant proportion of
   neurons ... responded to both observation and execution of these actions."
4. SEP, "Empathy" (`sep_empathy.txt`): "sympathy—or what some authors also refer to as empathic
   concern—is not an emotion that is congruent with the other's emotion or situation"; "sympathy
   consists of "feeling sorrow or concern for the distressed or needy other,"".
5. de Waal 2008, Annu Rev Psychol 59:279 (`dewaal2008.txt`): "Empathy is an ideal candidate
   mechanism to underlie so-called directed altruism, i.e., altruism in response to anothers's
   pain, need, or distress. Evidence is accumulating that this mechanism is phylogenetically
   ancient, probably as old as mammals and birds. Perception of the emotional state of another
   automatically activates shared representations causing a matching emotional state in the
   observer." "The dynamics of the empathy mechanism agree with predictions from kin selection and
   reciprocal altruism theory."
6. Preston and de Waal 2002, Behav Brain Sci 25:1 (`preston_dewaal2002.txt`): "Proximately, the
   perception of an object's state activates the subject's corresponding representations"; "The
   Perception-Action Model (PAM)"; lists "mother-infant responsiveness".
7. Nowak and Sigmund 1998, Nature 393:573 (`nowak_sigmund1998.txt`): "Cooperation pays because it
   confers the image of a valuable community member to the cooperating individual."
8. Carter and Wilkinson 2013, Proc R Soc B 280:20122573 (`carter2013.txt`): "Several authors have
   since suggested that food sharing is maintained solely by indirect fitness"; "Food received was
   the best predictor of food given across dyads, and 8.5 times more important than relatedness."
   Wilkinson (1984) itself not found on PubMed; my note avoids citing it.
9. Henrich et al. 2010, Science 327:1480 (`henrich2010.txt`): "Using three behavioral experiments
   administered across 15 diverse populations, we show that market integration ... positively
   covaries with fairness"; "These results suggest that modern prosociality is not solely the
   product of an innate psychology, but also reflects norms and institutions that have emerged over
   the course of human history."
10. Jacob and Jeannerod 2005, Trends Cogn Sci 9:21 (`jacob_jeannerod2005.txt`): title "The motor
    theory of social cognition: a critique"; "we question the motor theory of social cognition and
    give reasons for our skepticism."
11. Hickok 2009, J Cogn Neurosci 21:1229 (`hickok2009.txt`): "It is argued that there is no
    evidence from monkey data that directly tests this theory, and evidence from humans makes a
    strong case against the position." ("this theory" = "mirror neurons provide the basis of action
    understanding".)
12. Lamm and Majdandžić 2015, Neurosci Res 90:15 (`lamm_majdandzic2015.txt`): "three key issues
    which are often linked to empathy, but which at the same time might obscure our understanding
    of it. These issues are: ... (2) the causal link of empathy to our presumed mirror neuron
    system".
13. Merabet et al. 2008, PLoS One 3:e3046 (`merabet2008.txt`): "sudden, complete and prolonged
    visual deprivation (five days)"; "an increase in BOLD signal within the occipital cortex in
    response to tactile stimulation after five days ... This increase in signal was no longer

14. Prather et al. 2008, Nature 451:305 (`prather2008.txt`): "a certain class of neurons in the
    swamp sparrow forebrain displays a precise auditory-vocal correspondence"; "These neurons
    display nearly identical patterns of activity when the bird sings the same sequence". The

15. Card, Speaker for the Dead, ch. 2, via two secondary pages (curl): Shmoop quote page and Duck

    causes make them act." Plural "ramen" confirmed in the Shmoop quote "If we are ramen". Book

16. Heyes and Catmur 2022 (`heyes_catmur2022.txt`) read but not used in the final notes.

## 6. Judgment calls

1. Mirror-neuron clogic: "Stated as fact, and in 2009 it was a disputed hypothesis." Support:
   Jacob and Jeannerod 2005 (social cognition) and Hickok 2009 (action understanding, a narrower
   claim, which the note labels as narrower). The 2015 Lamm and Majdandžić review is later and is
   in the note and Response only as "still lists"; it is kept out of the "In short" line, which says
   "disputed" and relies on the 2005 and 2009 sources.
2. The alien-friendship clogic is the main criticism. It rests on the post's own sentence about "a
   more abstract desire". A fair defender could say "unsympathetic" was defined by the woodsaw
   exercise; the note states the conditional both ways rather than calling the conclusion wrong.
3. Henrich 2010 postdates the post and uses fairness in economic games, not sympathy; the note says
   both. It is kept out of the Response as information.
4. The terminology note (SEP on sympathy) is information, not a fault; the post flags its own
   usage with "what I would call".
5. "The bat example has support" and "The evidence fits the hedge 'quickly'" are credit, per the
   Book II lesson.
6. Reserved word "never": only in paraphrase of the post's "would never be friends" and in "never
   had mirroring".
7. Pronouns: de Waal is referred to by name only; no he/his for anyone in our text.
8. raz_check "motive?" flag on "empathic inference" is a false positive (the post's own term).
