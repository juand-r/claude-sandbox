# Report: when-none-dare-urge-restraint ("When None Dare Urge Restraint", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-12-08)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

On the morning of 11 September 2001 the author predicted that the overreaction would be ten times worse than the attack, because everyone would compete to express horror and no one would dare to urge a proportionate response. This is the "spiral of hate", the mirror of the happy death spiral: attacking the Enemy earns credit and correcting any negative claim about the Enemy counts as treason, so even false claims (the hijackers were "cowards") go unchallenged. The US response did more damage to the US than the terrorists could, ignoring the attack would have been better, and once restraint became unspeakable, within two days, fury and folly could only rise.

## 3. Arithmetic

- Post: "6,000 deaths is 1/8 the annual US casualties from automobile accidents". 6,000 / 42,196 = 0.142, about 1/7; 1/8 of 42,196 = 5,275. The post's fraction is about 12% low (0.125 vs 0.142). Under the one-fifth threshold, and the line is a hypothetical politician's; no note (the cpara says "close enough").
- 4,000 / 2,977 = 1.34 ("under two"). 80,000 / 2,977 = 26.9 ("far over ten", honest n.b.).
- 14 September is three days after 11 September ("three days later").
- Gallup: 57% (30 Nov-2 Dec 2007) vs 23% (24-25 Mar 2003). The post is dated 8 Dec 2007: "a week before the post".

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- Reserved words: none.
- Lee note: the record partly confirms the post (420-1, "traitor", death threats), so the note says "The record fits the prediction closely, with one exception." A defender could say one dissenter out of 421 is the exception that proves the rule; the note grants this.
- The Iraq figures: the post names no war. The notes say "If it means the war in Iraq". The editor may think this reading is obvious and the hedge unnecessary, or that the notes should not bring in Iraq at all. I kept them because the brief asked for checkable claims and the post's figures ("thousands of soldiers' lives") can only be checked against a named war.
- P3 clogic uses CNN's Iraqi-death estimates dated March 2008, three months after the post; stated in the note.
- Gallup cpara: a defender could say the law is conditional ("Once restraint becomes unspeakable") and public opinion turning means the condition lapsed. The note says the post "gives no way for a spiral to end", which I think is fair, and does not say the law is refuted.
- "the reader has only the author's memory for it" (P2): a mild point; the Response repeats it in one sentence. Could be cut if the editor thinks it faults an anecdote for being an anecdote (STANDARDS 2.2.4). I kept it because the post uses the recalled prediction as evidence of foresight.
