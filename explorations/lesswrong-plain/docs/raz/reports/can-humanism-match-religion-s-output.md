# Report: can-humanism-match-religion-s-output ("Can Humanism Match Religion's Output?", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-03-27)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

The Catholic Church, perhaps the largest voluntary institution, can mobilize its members for
charity far better than atheist leaders can, so until "rationalists" match it, gains for atheism
are partly hollow; the post asks whether the average Catholic does more gross good than the
average atheist simply by being more active. It grants that false beliefs such as fear of hell
may motivate more than any true belief, but argues that hell is an imperfect motivator, that
rationalists who cared a lot might match it, possibly with techniques from CBT and meditation,
and suspects the real source of the Church's output is people physically meeting at church.
It proposes regular in-person meetings for motivation, norms that applaud caring and expect
useful work, and a norm of giving at least 5% of income to real causes, on the guess that the
Church spends more than half its money on religious activity.

## 3. Arithmetic

- Brooks: 2,210 / 642 = 3.44 ("more than three times as much").
- The post's 5% norm: 10% tithe x (1 - more than 50% spent on religion) = less than 5% to real
  causes, so 5% to real causes matches or beats a full tithe's real-cause share. Consistent.
- "Half the effort, twice as much done": with Catholic useful share f < 0.5 and rationalist effort
  C/2, output (C/2) x m = 2 f C requires an efficiency multiplier m = 4f < 2. So on the post's own
  guess, the claim needs better targeting to at most double effectiveness per unit of effort;
  consistent with "better targeting on more efficient causes". Not noted.
- Dates: Pope's remarks 17 March 2009; post 27 March 2009 (Posted line 2009-03-27): ten days
  (honest n.b.). "Church vs. Taskforce" Posted 2009-03-28 (the "I'll post tomorrow" cpara).
