# Report: sorting-pebbles-into-correct-heaps ("Sorting Pebbles Into Correct Heaps", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-08-10; book order 274)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

A parable: a species cares above all about sorting pebbles into "correct" heaps, cannot say
why a heap is correct, and yet makes what looks like progress (Biko's heap of 91 is later
scattered; a war over heaps of 1957 ends when someone shows heaps of 103 and 19); the reader
can see that "correct" means prime, a fixed criterion the characters use without stating.
Against the Heap Relativists, who read the disagreement over large heaps as proof that
nothing makes a heap correct, the story shows that disagreement and historical change are
compatible with a fixed criterion; against the majority, who expect a superintelligent AI to
"see at a glance" which heaps are correct and to rewrite a wrong utility function, it shows
that the criterion belongs to the species, not to intelligence. So the trend "smarter minds
equal smarter heaps" holds among Pebblesorters only because they share the criterion, and
would break for a mind built another way; the post leaves the application to human values to
the reader.
