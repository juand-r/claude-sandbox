# Report: sorting-pebbles-into-correct-heaps ("Sorting Pebbles Into Correct Heaps", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-08-10; book order 274)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

A parable: a species cares above all about sorting pebbles into "correct" heaps, cannot say
why a heap is correct, and yet makes what looks like progress (Biko's heap of 91 is later
scattered; a war over heaps of 1957 ends when someone shows heaps of 103 and 19); the reader
can see that "correct" means prime, a fixed criterion the characters use without stating.
Against the Heap Relativists, who read the disagreement over large heaps as proof that
nothing makes a heap correct, the story shows that disagreement and historical change are
compatible with a fixed criterion; against the majority, who expect a superintelligent AI to
"see at a glance" which heaps are correct and to rewrite a wrong utility function, it shows
that the criterion belongs to the species, not to intelligence. So the trend "smarter minds
equal smarter heaps" holds among Pebblesorters only because they share the criterion, and
would break for a mind built another way; the post leaves the application to human values to
the reader.

## 6. Judgment calls

1. The relativism clogic ("tells against the Heap Relativists' denial of progress ... more than
   against relativism in general"). It is scope information: the post's target is named
   "Heap Relativists" and characterized by denying progress. The SEP reply is hedged ("it might
   be pointed out"); I say only that the reply exists. Also in the Response and the honest n.b.
2. The Response says the second point is "stipulated by the story and argued elsewhere". This
   is my reading of how a parable works, and is repeated in the In short line. The post itself
   makes no claim to prove it, so I phrase it as description, not fault.
3. Reserved words: "never states" (the post's text contains no "prime"; checked). "mistaken"
   replaced "wrong" in the afterword for the fictional camps. "absurd in their remedy" is a
   description of the satire.
4. Motive: the cpara on the Heap Relativists notes the narrator's own motive reading
   ("seem to take a tangible delight"), hedged, and makes no reading of the author's motives.
5. Credit sentences in the Response ("works because it withholds its rule", "fair and funny",
   "well-made fable"): kept because a purely critical account would mislead about a parable
   whose points hold. The editor may trim.
6. Honest section 441 words.
