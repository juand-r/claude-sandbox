# Report: changing-your-metaethics ("Changing Your Metaethics", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-07-27; book order 277)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

People agree far more about first-order moral claims ("murder is bad") than about metaethics
(what makes them true or what they mean), yet many believe that if their own metaethic is false,
morality falls apart; since most of them must be wrong, many will have to change metaethics, and
that is nearly impossible while the fear of losing morality is unanswered. The post therefore
collects the "lines of retreat" set up in earlier posts: you remain responsible for obeying any
moral authority and can act morally without it; explicable things are no less valuable;
arguments need not move every possible mind; reflective loops are not circular logic; a causal
role for evolution is not a justifying role; morality computed in the mind need not be about the
mind; and existential pain may not come from reductionism. It says metaethics matters, although
it "adds up to moral normality", because misunderstanding where morality comes from harms many
people, and it ends on a joke that withholds "the real reason".

## 6. Judgment calls

1. Reserved words: "wrong" and "false" appear only as the post's own subject matter
   (killing is wrong; a metaethic is false). "refute" appears once, in "meant to reassure, not
   to refute", which the post's "as opposed to immediately attacking the plausibility of God"
   supports.
2. "I infer that the big cults are the major religions" (P22): the link on "big ones" goes to
   "Religion's Claim to be Non-Disprovable". Marked as inference. The editor may prefer to cut
   the inference and keep only "asserted without an example of such a teaching".
3. The "explain the difference" clogic and its line in the Response: a fair defender could say
   "explain" does not mean "formalize". I kept it because the post's summary gives no hint that
   the distinction was left to common sense, and it is a pointer to points made in full at 270
   and 271, not a new charge.
4. The Missing Fallibility Problem (P17) is information, placing the worry the post answers.
   The agent annotating "Morality as Fixed Computation" (279) may also place the calculator
   (possibly with the rigidification move, SEP "Moral Sentimentalism", Wiggins 1987); if so, one
   of the two should point to the other. I did not add rigidification here to avoid a clash.
5. The neutral notes on the book's selection and order (P6 clogic, P19) follow the brief's
   rule: they state facts, not faults.
6. Book order: the post is placed before "Could Anything Be Right?" though posted later; the
   CABR afterword does not mention this, only this post's notes do.
