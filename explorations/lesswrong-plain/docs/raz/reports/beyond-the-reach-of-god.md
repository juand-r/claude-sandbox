## 1. The argument in three sentences

A believer who takes God seriously expects some threshold of horror (true death, torture of
the innocent) beyond which God will intervene; but the mathematical answer to "what would
this cellular automaton do, given these rules?" is beyond even God's reach, and in that
what-if world nothing prevents a Khan from torturing innocents or a single conception from
deciding the fate of millions. The author holds that we live in such a world, "driven by the
mathematical laws and nothing else", and that many atheists and self-described rationalists
still think as if some outcomes (the extinction of humanity, a reversal of the positive-sum
trend, a technology doing net harm) are "not allowed", which the author once believed too.
Seeing the world as absolutely neutral is not needed for happiness, but it is needed by
anyone who has something to protect and means to face an uncalibrated challenge of instant
death, and the only prospect of a fairer world is to build one, a task that must be started
from this unprotected one.
