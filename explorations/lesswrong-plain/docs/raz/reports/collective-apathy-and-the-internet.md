# Report: collective-apathy-and-the-internet ("Collective Apathy and the Internet", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-04-14)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Following the previous day's post on the bystander effect, the post suggests that our
coordination instincts, tuned to groups of people who knew each other, fail on the Internet,
and lists eight features of online groups (strangers, unknown size, no eye contact, no real
time, anonymity, no voiced plea and others) that might explain why people react poorly to
problems met online. It says existing online activism tools are mostly petitions, and offers
a handful of design ideas (a video plea, showing the first helpers, video thanks, referral
codes, bounties on verifiable subtasks). It ends by handing readers the problem as open and
unsolved: help groups of strangers form effective task forces online, since, by Shirky's
statistic about Wikipedia and television, a great deal of spare effort is available.
