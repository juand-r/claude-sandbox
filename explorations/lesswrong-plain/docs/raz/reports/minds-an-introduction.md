# Report: minds-an-introduction

## 1. The argument in three sentences

You are a mind running on a lawful brain that evolution built for reproduction in ancestral
environments, and you can study your own workings in order to improve them relative to your
own goals rather than evolution's. Our mental categories do not resemble physical ones, and
even after dualism is rejected our talk of "rationality" and "bias" can stay an unconstrained
"ghost"; so the book will use obviously inhuman purposeful-seeming processes, evolution and
artificial intelligence, as a mirror, and it previews Book III's sequences. The second half
profiles Yudkowsky's AI work (intelligence explosion, Friendly AI, existential risk, debated
and uncertain), and argues that AI belongs in a book on human rationality because it gives
simple illustrations of cognition and because long-term technology forecasting is an
important application of Bayesian rationality.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. The MIRI note (cfact at "until January 2013, MIRI"): it states that MIRI released the
   book and that Bensinger announced it on MIRI's blog. It is meant as context, not as a
   motive reading, and makes no inference. The Response repeats it in one sentence. The
   precedent is the MIRI affiliation note on "On Caring". Cut it if the editor judges it a
   test-9 problem.
2. "It cites no one on the other side of the debate" (cpara on "As of 2015", and the
   Response and In short). A fair defender might say an introduction need not cite critics
   (STANDARDS 2.4(7)). I kept it because the introduction itself raises the debate ("continue
   to be debated") and then names only Bostrom, Yudkowsky's co-author (footnote 7), and a
   textbook passage that reports Yudkowsky's own view.
3. The Bayesian-forecasting point is stated as "holds only in a limited sense" in the In
   short line. The introduction does not claim reliability; the Response says so explicitly.
4. Reserved words: "never" appears once, paraphrasing the post's own "without ever once
   noticing". No other reserved words.
5. Pronouns: "he" for Ryle (historical figure) and inside the quoted textbook sentence
   ("He asserts"). None for Bensinger or Yudkowsky.
