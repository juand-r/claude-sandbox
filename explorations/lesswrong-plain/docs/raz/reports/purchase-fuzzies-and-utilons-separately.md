# Report: purchase-fuzzies-and-utilons-separately ("Purchase Fuzzies and Utilons Separately", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-04-01)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

Following the previous day's lawyer example, the author asks whether a small good deed (telling
a stranger their car trunk is open) can be defended as a use of time, and answers that it is a
"selfish good deed": it restores the author's willpower and keeps the author altruistic, and
calling it selfish does not spoil the effect, at least for the author. The post then recommends
buying warm feelings ("fuzzies"), status and good outcomes ("utilons") separately, because the
most efficient source of each yields little of the others, so a single purchase aimed at all
three does none well; a billionaire should buy fuzzies by personal gifts, status by a showy
donation, and utilons by cold expected-value calculation, spending at least 20 times as much on
utilons as on fuzzies. Ordinary people should get fuzzies cheaply (a soup kitchen, holding
doors) and buy utilons by "shut up and multiply".
