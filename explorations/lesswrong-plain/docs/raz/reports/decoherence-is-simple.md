# Report: decoherence-is-simple

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Occam's razor, made quantitative by Solomonoff induction and Minimum Message Length (which the post says were "proven equivalent"), charges a theory for the length of its laws and for the specific details it must state explicitly, not for the number of objects its laws generate; the conjunction rule explains why stated details cost probability, and the history of an ever larger universe shows that a razor counting objects or computing resources would have failed. By the argument of "Belief in the Implied Invisible", a spaceship that crosses our cosmological horizon keeps existing, because making it vanish needs an extra law. So the objection that many-worlds (which the post calls "decoherence") violates Occam's razor by multiplying worlds is, the post concludes, not weak but "bad math", though other questions about many-worlds (such as the source of the Born probabilities) remain open.

## 3. Arithmetic

- Ten binary details: 2^10 = 1024 stories; a fully detailed one competes with 2^10 − 1 = 1023. Post correct.
- "The woman down the street is a witch; she did it." The(1) woman(2) down(3) the(4) street(5) is(6) a(7) witch(8) she(9) did(10) it(11): eleven. Post correct.
- Hydrogen: 1 g / 1.008 g/mol × 6.022 × 10^23 /mol = 5.97 × 10^23 atoms ≈ six hundred billion trillion (6 × 10^11 × 10^12). Post correct.
- Lottery: $1 ticket, $500,000 prize, 10^6 tickets: expected value $0.50. "poor investment" correct.
- Event horizon needs acceleration. Horizon exists iff ∫^∞ dt/a converges. If expansion decelerates (ä ≤ 0), then a(t) ≤ a(t0) + ȧ(t0)(t − t0), so ∫ dt/a ≥ ∫ dt/(linear) = ∞: no horizon. Example: Einstein-de Sitter, a ∝ t^{2/3}, ∫_t^∞ t'^{-2/3} dt' = [3 t'^{1/3}] = ∞. So "If the expansion ... continues" is not sufficient; "accelerating" (the Implied Invisible wording) is.
- Thermometer: entropy rises with temperature, so more microstates at 22 °C than at 20 °C. Post correct.
- Prefix-free prior: Kraft inequality gives Σ 2^{−L(P)} ≤ 1. Post correct.
- Probability theory and priors (final note): the Kolmogorov axioms constrain a prior only to be non-negative and sum to 1; a prior that gives lower weight to hypotheses positing more objects (for example weight ∝ 2^{−(code length + log2 of number of worlds)}, renormalized) satisfies them. So the rejection of object-counting priors is a choice, argued for, not a theorem of probability. The post's own argument for the choice is the history paragraph.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. "proven equivalent" cpara: "stronger than the sources and than the author's own earlier posts." Evidence: the author's own "nearly equivalent" plus footnote, and Wallace and Dowe's "attempt to establish a parallel". I did not use "false". It also states that the argument survives, and it is in the Response but, per the Book III rule, framed as overstatement.
2. Final-paragraph note and the "In short" line ("a dispute about priors is presented as a mathematical error"). The post says "entirely defective as probability theory ... bad math. 2+2=3." I read "2+2=3" as a figure of speech and quote only the literal sentences. The note credits the post's side as widely held (Lewis via SEP) and faults only the status claimed. A defender may say "probability theory" in the post means Solomonoff/MML; then the note reduces to "the post calls its choice of prior probability theory", which I think is still fair.
3. RAM note: "this answer does not show that resources cannot count." The post's sentence is an answer to "Why doesn't the prior include RAM or running time?" I treat it as offered as a reason. The speed-prior point was made in full in the Implied Invisible notes; here it is a different sentence ("not mutually exclusive"), so I gave it a short note with a pointer. The Schmidhuber quotation about parallel universes is information, not a charge.
4. Born-rule cpara and the Hemmo/Shenker-Hutter clogic: these state standard objections to simplicity arguments, as requested, with the counterpoint (Papineau; Zeh in the decoherence note). They do not say the post is wrong; the Response says the argument removes one objection and "as the post itself allows" does not decide overall simplicity (post: "if there are probability penalties for decoherence, the largeness of the implied universe, per se, is definitely not their source!"). Hutter (2010) postdates the post; the note says "after the post".

7. "Suggests, with 'it may be,' that critics mistake their shock..." cpara: the post's own hedged guess about critics' psychology; I note only that no evidence is given. No "My reading".
