# Report: my-wild-and-reckless-youth

## 1. The argument in three sentences

As a young "Traditional Rationalist" the author gave a mysterious answer to a mysterious
question (neurons exploiting quantum gravity, after Penrose) while carefully obeying the
traditional rules: the theory made a falsifiable prediction and professed to be "just
physics". Those rules did not catch the error, because they ignore retrospective
predictions, tolerate decades spent on a wrong idea as long as it is eventually refuted, and
allow guessing and agreeing to disagree; a Bayesian would have asked why believe anything
more complicated than "I don't know". Traditional Rationality helped the author out of the
hole but is not enough to get things right, and the Way of Bayes, though also imprecise, at
least has underlying mathematics and experimental psychology behind it.

## 2. Sources checked

- Tegmark, "The importance of quantum decoherence in brain processes", arXiv quant-ph/9907009
  (submitted 5 Jul 1999), journal ref "Phys.Rev.E61:4194-4206,2000"
  (`data/sources/arxiv_quant-ph_9907009.html`). Matched: "We find that the decoherence
  timescales ~10^{-13}-10^{-20} seconds are typically much shorter than the relevant dynamical
  timescales (~0.001-0.1 seconds), both for regular neuron firing and for kink-like
  polarization excitations in microtubules. This conclusion disagrees with suggestions by
  Penrose and others that the brain acts as a quantum computer, and that quantum coherence is
