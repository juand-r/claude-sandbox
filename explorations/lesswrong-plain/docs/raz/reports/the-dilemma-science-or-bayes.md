# Report: the-dilemma-science-or-bayes

## 1. The argument in three sentences

The author says one purpose of the quantum physics sequence was to give a clear-cut case
where probability theory and the rules of Science disagree: many-worlds is strictly simpler
than collapse, but it makes no new experimental prediction, so physicists who reject it are
obeying the ideal of Science, which demands that a new theory win by experiment. The
problem cannot be fixed by teaching scientists Solomonoff induction, because Science's
demand for experiment is what protects people from reasonable-sounding armchair theories,
and even Aumann, a Bayesian theorist, needs that protection. So the reader faces a
dilemma: reject the scientific method as traditionally understood, or reject probability
theory and believe in a single world, which the author calls insanity.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

1. "My reading" in the Vassar note: the post does not say how the joke applies. I read it
   as rejecting a strong argument because its conclusion is unbelievable, and note that the
   Science view uses the same kind of ground ("smacks of science fiction"). This is my
   inference about how the joke bears on the dilemma; it could be cut to the first two
   sentences.
2. The note on the omitted middle of the epigraph and the \cpara on the next line ("not
   taken up in this post"): the post promises later answers in its PS, so the claim is
   limited to this post.
3. The ether note says working physicists "have not applied the post's ideal of Science
   strictly". A fair defender can answer that the post says its Science is an ideal, "not
   how things do work". The note's conclusion is therefore limited to "preferring the
   simpler theory need not mean rejecting the scientific method", which is aimed at the
   post's closing phrase "rejecting the scientific method".
4. The "leaves out positions between its horns" note repeats, in two sentences, points made
   in full in Many Worlds, One Best Guess and Decoherence is Simple (STANDARDS 2.5 allows a
   brief pointer). The lines-of-code note is also a pointer with two facts.
