# Report: shut-up-and-do-the-impossible

Batch 6b, Book VI "Becoming Stronger", Challenging the Difficult, book order 315.
Posted 2008-10-08 (the `Posted:` line of the original).

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Above "become stronger", "make a desperate effort" and "make an extraordinary effort" there is
a fourth level, "shut up and do the impossible": setting out to win at a problem that looks
impossible, rather than to try, to make an effort, or to have tried. The author illustrates it
with the AI-Box Experiment, which others called impossible and which the author, playing the
AI, reports winning three times out of five, "the hard way", without a trick. The attitude
asks you to hold both the full view of why the problem is impossible and the intent to solve
it, without expecting either to win or to lose; it is costly, you can lose, and it should be
reserved for very special occasions. The author calls Friendly AI impossible in this sense.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- The Oettingen clogic (paragraph "I say again"). It gives credit first ("the nearest research
  I found agrees"), then the 2001 finding that strong commitment followed favorable
  expectations. The last sentence, "gives only the author's experience that this can be done",
  is the note's only charge. A fair defender might say the post asks for expectations that
  are open, not unfavorable; the note says "a problem that looks impossible", which is the
  post's own case. The editor may prefer to keep only the first half.
- The Friendly-AI clogic ("sits uneasily with"). It sets "you can't do it because Friendly AI is
  impossible" beside the post's own gatekeeper sentence, and then grants that the next sentence
  asks only for suspicion. The charitable reading (the rule is a prior about difficulty, backed
  by the author's record) is stated in the note. This carries into the In short line
  ("sits uneasily with"); if the editor thinks the concession answers the point, both should go.
- The AIfolk cpara: says the claim is hedged ("I'm pretty sure"), unnamed and without example,
  and that the following prediction and motive ("to make themselves defensible") rest "on the
  same basis". The hedge is kept; no motive is read by me. The Response repeats it in one
  sentence.
- The Response's second paragraph: "It does not show how, so it illustrates the attitude only
  through the author's own account." Per the brief I judge the reports as reports and do not
  speculate on method. The post's scope is "illustration", which I keep.
- Reserved word "never" appears only as the post's own words ("never did seem like an impossible
  problem") or as fact ("never added", which the post itself says; "never released").
- Credit given where leaving it out would mislead: the account of the 2002 games matches the
  archive, and both gatekeepers' signed confirmations are cited.
- Summary is 244 words (post is about 3,550 words). Honest section is 720 words (under the 900
  limit for long posts).
