# Report: two-cult-koans ("Two Cult Koans", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-12-21)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

In the first koan a novice anxious that his rationalist school is a cult asks the master for reassurance; the master makes him wear a silly hat and then explains that his need for reassurance, not his doubt, is the weakness a cult would exploit, and that the worth of a method shows in using it on real questions ("Use the techniques and do not mention them"). In the second koan a novice objects that the school's robes are cultish and irrational; the master mocks the idea that clothing interacts with probability theory by requiring a wizard's hat. The two stories suggest that worrying about the look of rationality, whether reassurance or dress, is a distraction from its use.
