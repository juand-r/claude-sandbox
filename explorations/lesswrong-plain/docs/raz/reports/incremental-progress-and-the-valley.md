# Report: incremental-progress-and-the-valley ("Incremental Progress and the Valley", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-04-04)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

The post grants that a step toward rationality need not make a person win more, since the
optimality theorems hold only for perfect reasoners and there is no theorem that each step
toward the ideal improves performance; so there can be a "valley" in which partial
rationality does worse. It gives three personal reasons to take the step anyway (the author's
work demands precision, the author wants to push the art further, and seeing through a
deception cannot be undone), then reports that the author's experience suggests that once a
person stops doing other things wrong, more rationality does leave them better off, and that
this is plain in answering confusing questions, though not yet shown for motivation and
happiness. It illustrates the valley with two reader comments about a deconversion and a
break-up, says the arguments against losing illusions usually consider only one step, and
ends with a joke telling readers who prefer comfortable illusions to stop reading.
