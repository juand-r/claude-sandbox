# Report: incremental-progress-and-the-valley ("Incremental Progress and the Valley", Yudkowsky, posted 2009-04-04)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

The post grants that a step toward rationality need not make a person win more, since the
optimality theorems hold only for perfect reasoners and there is no theorem that each step
toward the ideal improves performance; so there can be a "valley" in which partial
rationality does worse. It gives three personal reasons to take the step anyway (the author's
work demands precision, the author wants to push the art further, and seeing through a
deception cannot be undone), then reports that the author's experience suggests that once a
person stops doing other things wrong, more rationality does leave them better off, and that
this is plain in answering confusing questions, though not yet shown for motivation and
happiness. It illustrates the valley with two reader comments about a deconversion and a
break-up, says the arguments against losing illusions usually consider only one step, and
ends with a joke telling readers who prefer comfortable illusions to stop reading.

## 6. Judgment calls for the editor

- The positive-illusions note. The post's belief is about the best a person could do ("the
  happiest we can possibly live"); the studies measure ordinary people. The note ends: "they show
  that the belief is contested, not that it is mistaken." The Response says the 2019 meta-analysis
  "fits the objection the post answers, though it says nothing about the best a person could
  achieve." The 2019 study is hindsight; it is kept out of the In short line.
- The CBT note sets the post's "If these parts of the Art have been developed systematically, I do
  not know of it" beside the author's own CBT sentence of eight days earlier. I judged it
  information, not a contradiction, and the Response says so (the word "contradiction" is flagged
  there only in that negated sentence).
- The cryonics note is a neutral scope note (the argument is in a post the book omits), following
  the Book III lesson on the editors' selection.
- The In short line: "answered with the author's reasons, experience and hopes, and with no study
  on either side." The post does not cite studies; it hedges its experience and hopes. I think this
  passes the fair-defender test, but "no study on either side" also covers the objector's side,
  which the post voices without studies too.
- The curvilinear-religion note is information that supports the valley; it uses a 2022 study
  (hindsight) summarizing "some findings", with the hedge kept.
