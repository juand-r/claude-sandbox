# Report: scarcity ("Scarcity", Yudkowsky, posted 2008-03-27; book order 214)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

The post defines scarcity as things becoming more desirable as they appear less obtainable,
and gives five studies, taken from Cialdini's *Influence*, in which barriers, bans,
censorship, instructions to disregard and news of a shortage raised desire or belief.
It explains them by psychological reactance, resistance to lost freedom and lost options,
which it suggests may have suited hunter-gatherers but is costly in a money economy, as a
salesman's "last one sold" trick shows. Its remedy is to notice when you want to possess
rather than use something, and it closes that desiring the unattainable ensures frustration,
since whatever is obtained stops being unattainable.
