# Report: Living in Many Worlds (batch 4c, order 241)

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Many-worlds, like every successful physical theory, must reproduce the ordinary world
("Egan's Law: It all adds up to normality"), so it gives no reason to live or decide
differently: decisions do not cause splitting, choices in one world do not affect another,
and a 90% chance can be treated like 9 worlds in 10. Worlds outside your future are
beyond your influence and can be treated as a "generalized past", and very improbable
worlds deserve attention only in proportion to their tiny weight. The author allows a few
ethical implications (average utilitarianism looks better; being "first" to know something
is meaningless) but otherwise advises: if your grasp of many-worlds is shaky, do not adopt
any strange belief, feeling or strategy because of it.
