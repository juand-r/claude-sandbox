# Report: excluding-the-supernatural

## 1. The argument in three sentences

Some people say creationism and Intelligent Design are excluded from science a priori because
they invoke the "supernatural"; the author says this dodges the real verdict (tested and found
false) and hands creationists a fair complaint. Taking Richard Carrier's definition (the
supernatural appeals to ontologically basic mental things), the author argues that
non-reductionism, and so supernaturalism, is a confusion that a reducible brain cannot even
concretely envision, so that one can never get Bayesian confirmation for irreducibility. Yet ID
should not be excluded a priori: every supernatural hypothesis has a natural counterpart making
the same predictions (a reducible alien instead of an irreducible God), those predictions can be
tested, and they fail, so religious claims are excluded a posteriori; a corollary says an AGI
designer who speaks respectfully of religion is no expert on reducing the mental.

## 2. Sources checked

Saved in `data/sources/4b_excluding-the-supernatural/` unless noted.

- Kitzmiller v. Dover Area School District, Memorandum Opinion, 20 Dec 2005,
  https://www.pamd.uscourts.gov/sites/pamd/files/opinions/04v2688d.pdf (`kitzmiller.pdf`,
  `kitzmiller.txt`, pp. 64-66). Matched: "while ID arguments may be true, a proposition on which
  the Court takes no position, ID is not science. We find that ID fails on three different
  levels, any one of which is sufficient to preclude a determination that ID is science. They
  are: (1) ID violates the centuries-old ground rules of science by invoking and permitting
  supernatural causation; ... and (3) ID's negative attacks on evolution have been refuted by the
  scientific community." Also: "We are in agreement with Plaintiffs' lead expert Dr. Miller, that
  from a practical perspective, attributing unsolved problems about nature to causes and forces
  that lie outside the natural world is a "science stopper."" and "once you attribute a cause to
  an untestable supernatural force".
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Creationism", https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/creationism/
  (`sep_creationism.txt`). Matched: "a number of prominent philosophers (most notably the American
  Larry Laudan) objected strongly to the very idea of using falsifiability as a 'criterion of
  demarcation'"; "Not that people like Laudan were themselves Creationists. They thought
  Creationism false."; "the United States Constitution does not bar the teaching of false
  science. It bars the teaching of non-science, especially non-science which is religion by
  another name."
- Wikipedia, "McLean v. Arkansas" (raw, `wiki_McLean.txt`): decided "January 5, 1982"; Laudan

- Richard Carrier, "Defining the Supernatural" (January 2007),
  `data/sources/b2b_death_spirals/carrier_defining_supernatural.txt` (saved by an earlier agent;

## 6. Judgment calls

- The clogic on "what is it that we're supposed to exclude?": says the post argues against the
  rule under Carrier's definition, not under its opponents' ("the untestable"). A defender can
  reply that the post refers readers to Carrier for why that definition is bad. I kept it
  because the post never states the opponents' meaning; the editor may prefer to soften.
- The motive note (paragraph 3): the post's motive reading of others, "It seems clear enough",
  is called unsupported; alternatives are sourced (SEP legal reason; Dover court's "science
  stopper"). The Dover "refuted" clogic is a separate point (the ruling did the a posteriori
  work the post says the rule avoids). The post says only "Occasionally, you hear someone", so the
  ruling is an example, not the post's stated target.
- Three notes draw on the emergence literature (observations named; collapse objection;
  basic vs fundamental). The last cites O'Connor 2018, after the post; phrased as "have since
  answered", not as a fault. The editor may think three is heavy; the collapse note is partly
  credit.
- Kingsley note: supports the post's explanation; "ignored" is not called wrong.
- Corollary clogic: "does not follow as stated", with the conditional reading acknowledged.
  Reserved-word flags ("false", "never", "wrong", "refuted") are all descriptive or quoted, not
  charges.
- Pronouns: none for the post's author. Carrier is referred to by name ("his" was removed).
  Honest section: 516 words, a little over the 500 guideline.
