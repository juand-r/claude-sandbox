# Report: the-halo-effect ("The Halo Effect", Yudkowsky, posted 2007-11-30)

## 1. The argument in three sentences (written before annotating)

The halo effect, the social-psychology form of the affect heuristic, makes one good trait
raise judgments of others; a long passage from Cialdini reports that attractive people are
judged more talented, kind, honest and intelligent, get more votes, better jobs and pay,
lighter sentences and more help, without the judges noticing. Attractiveness has no
legitimate link to honesty, so this is plainly bias, though traits like honesty and
intelligence may really go together. By extension, readers should suspect a halo among
kindness, intelligence and other virtues in people they admire, and should be a little
more skeptical of attractive candidates.
